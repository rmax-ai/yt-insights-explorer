Topic Map

    • Moore's Law is effectively over for general-purpose computing, meaning intelligence consumption must rely on accelerated computing.
    • The cost of intelligence decreases exponentially year-over-year while consumption expands massively.
  • Examples:
    • Consensus forecasts for lab revenues shifting upward by 100% to 200% year-over-year.

Key Points

End of Moore's Law

  • Explanation: Traditional CPU scaling has stalled, necessitating GPUs and accelerated computing to drive continuous performance gains.
  • Evidence: Historical compute cost reduction curves have flattened for general-purpose processors.
  • Practical implication: All future data center and enterprise compute investments must pivot to accelerated computing architectures.

Fungibility as an Economic Moat

  • Explanation: Hardware and software that can be repurposed across multiple workloads (simulations, training, inference, graphics) retain higher utilization and value.
  • Evidence: NVIDIA's platforms power gaming, scientific simulation, cloud rendering, and AI training simultaneously.
  • Practical implication: Reduces capital risk for enterprise buyers by ensuring infrastructure remains useful across changing technology cycles.

Power and Infrastructure Constraints

  • Explanation: Energy availability is the ultimate ceiling on data center expansion, making power procurement and electrical efficiency critical competitive advantages.
  • Evidence: Multi-gigawatt data center announcements in regions like Australia and constraints on land-based power permits.
  • Practical implication: AI scaling requires close collaboration between tech companies, energy providers, and governments.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

The Three-Legged Stool of AI Safety and Governance

  • How it works: Balances frontier model review, government/regulatory oversight, and industry-wide coordination to ensure responsible AI scaling without stifling innovation.
  • Components:
    • Frontier model review by government and safety bodies
    • Internal and external regulatory audits and oversight
    • Industry coordination and cybersecurity alignment
  • When to use: When designing governance frameworks for autonomous and superintelligent systems.

Zero-Trust Autonomous Containment

  • How it works: Treats AI agents and autonomous systems with strict initial access boundaries, out-of-band monitoring, and dynamic escalation controls.
  • Components:
    • Zero-trust permission boundaries
    • Out-of-band offline monitoring
    • Automated shutdown or escalation protocols
  • When to use: When deploying autonomous AI agents into enterprise and physical workflows.

[~]Tradeoffmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Fungibility — the ability of the same hardware/software stack to serve training, inference, simulation, and graphics workloads — is framed as an economic moat, because specialized infrastructure is claimed to lose both durability and value as workloads shift. The explicit principle stated is that specialization is the enemy of fungibility.

Why it matters

It inverts the usual intuition that workload specialization maximizes performance: it argues that the dominant economic variable for infrastructure buyers is residual usefulness across technology cycles, not peak throughput on one workload. This reframes capacity planning as a portfolio/optionality problem rather than a per-workload optimization problem.

Generalization

Applies to any long-lived platform layer — agent runtimes, model-serving stacks, tool interfaces, memory layers: the abstraction that stays useful across shifting workload mixes beats the one tuned for the current dominant task, because the workload mix changes faster than the capital cycle.

Project ideas

Gatehouse Agent Containment Runtime

gatehouse

An agent runtime with strict initial access boundaries, out-of-band monitoring, and runtime privilege escalation can contain tool-using agents without reducing task success by more than 10% compared to an unrestricted baseline.

Proof of concept

Implement a control plane that sits outside the agent's execution path, logs all tool calls to an immutable out-of-band channel, and requires a separate escalation service to grant new privileges at runtime; run a suite of tool-use tasks under both contained and unrestricted modes.

Measurement

Task success rate, containment escape attempts detected, false-positive escalation blocks, and monitoring completeness (percentage of agent actions captured out-of-band).

Fungibility Meter for Accelerated Fleets

movement-lab

Multi-workload accelerated fleets retain at least 30% higher residual utilization than workload-specialized fleets when the dominant workload shifts by more than 50% over a 12-month period.

Proof of concept

Instrument a heterogeneous compute fleet with workload-mix telemetry and compute realized utilization, stranded capacity, and repurposing latency when simulating a workload-mix shift from training to inference/simulation.

Measurement

Fleet-level utilization before and after mix shift, stranded GPU-hours, time-to-repurpose, and total cost per workload-hour.

Source video ↗

Intelligence Cost Elasticity Tracker

beyond-evals

For production agentic workloads, a 50% reduction in cost per inference/action predicts at least a 100% increase in consumption volume within two quarters, confirming elastic demand.

Proof of concept

Deploy a lightweight instrumentation layer that tracks cost per token/inference and consumption volume across a set of agentic use cases before and after a deliberate cost-optimization intervention (e.g., caching, smaller models, batching).

Measurement

Elasticity coefficient (percentage change in consumption volume / percentage change in unit cost), time lag to demand response, and threshold cost at which new use cases become economically viable.

Sim-to-Real Transfer Gap Benchmark

new

Policies trained in a digital-twin environment achieve at least 80% of their simulated task success on physical hardware with a standardized transfer protocol, and the gap is dominated by lighting and contact-friction variations.

Proof of concept

Train a manipulation or navigation policy in a simulated environment, deploy to a physical robot on the same task suite, and ablate environmental factors (lighting, texture, friction, latency) to measure which drive the transfer gap.

Measurement

Simulated vs. physical success rate, transfer gap percentage, and sensitivity of gap to each ablated factor.

Architectural implications

4

Moore's Law is asserted to be effectively over for general-purpose computing, forcing a shift from classical CPU-based general-purpose compute to specialized accelerated compute in data centers and enterprises.

Before

Capacity planning assumes general-purpose CPUs deliver continuous per-generation performance gains at declining cost.

After

Capacity planning assumes gains come from accelerated architectures, and all compute investment is reallocated toward them.

Consequence

Software that was implicitly tuned for CPU-shaped economics (sequential, latency-tolerant, cheap per-core) must be re-architected around accelerator-shaped economics (parallel, throughput-batched, memory-bandwidth-bound).

Source video ↗

Physical AI development is staged inside digital twins before physical deployment.

Before

Robotics and autonomous systems are trained and validated primarily through physical prototyping and field trials.

After

Robots and autonomous systems are pre-trained inside simulated environments, with physical deployment as the later validation step.

Consequence

The development harness gains a simulation dependency: teams need environment authoring, fidelity validation, and transfer testing (sim-to-real) as first-class engineering steps.

Source video ↗

Agents and autonomous systems are treated as untrusted principals under zero-trust containment.

Before

Autonomy is granted at deployment time and monitored through the same path the agent controls.

After

Strict initial access boundaries, out-of-band monitoring, and dynamic escalation govern the agent at runtime.

Consequence

The control plane must sit outside the agent's own execution path, and privilege must be mutable at runtime, which constrains how tool-calling and orchestration layers are implemented.

Source video ↗

Fungibility is presented as the property that preserves value across technology cycles.

Before

Infrastructure is chosen for peak performance on today's dominant workload.

After

Infrastructure is chosen for repurposability across simulation, training, inference, and graphics.

Consequence

Evaluation shifts from single-workload benchmarks to multi-workload utilization and residual-value measures, changing what procurement and internal platform teams optimize.
