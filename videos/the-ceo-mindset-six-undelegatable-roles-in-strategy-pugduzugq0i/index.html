Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Sean Brown, Andy West, Carolyn Dewar, Kurt Strovink, and Whitney Zimmerman
  • Channel: McKinsey & Company
  • Main topic: The six strategic roles and responsibilities that only a CEO can fulfill.
  • Purpose: To provide leaders, particularly CEOs and aspiring executives, with a framework of the six core strategic responsibilities that cannot be delegated, highlighting how to lead through uncertainty, allocate resources dynamically, and drive sustainable corporate performance. In this discussion based on Chapter 26 of Strategy & Value, McKinsey & Company partners Andy West, Carolyn Dewar, Kurt Strovink, and Whitney Zimmerman explore the six undelegatable roles in strategy that only a CEO can play. They discuss how CEOs set the conditions for successful strategy by engaging the right way, owning the value thesis, making hard resource allocation calls, navigating uncertainty, pacing the organization, and acting as a catalyst for change. The panel also covers the impact of AI on CEO strategy and the importance of balancing nuance, simplicity, and conviction.

Key Points

Strategy requires bold trade-offs and saying 'no'.

  • Explanation: Great strategy dies on the vine without funding. Saying 'no' to non-strategic initiatives is essential to reallocate scarce resources like capital, executive time, and attention.
  • Evidence: Research showing high-performing CEOs reallocate resources dynamically and eliminate non-consistent activities.
  • Practical implication: CEOs must actively de-prioritize projects that do not align with the core value thesis, despite internal resistance.

The CEO is the ultimate owner of the value creation thesis.

  • Explanation: While teams build business cases, the CEO must hold the underlying logic of how the company creates distinct economic value in its markets.
  • Evidence: Examples like Jamie Dimon's fortress balance sheet at JPMorgan Chase.
  • Practical implication: Every major resource allocation decision must tie back to a crystal-clear value creation thesis understood across the enterprise.

Managing strategic tempo is critical.

  • Explanation: CEOs must know when to accelerate organizational momentum and when to slow down to allow markets, technology, or internal capabilities to catch up.
  • Evidence: Netflix holding both DVD and streaming businesses in parallel for four years before scaling streaming globally.
  • Practical implication: Avoid short-termism while maintaining the flexibility to time major strategic bets.

Uncertainty is an opportunity, not just risk.

  • Explanation: Uncertainty creates unfreezing moments where strategic advantage can be seized if leaders possess the conviction to act.
  • Evidence: Market disruptions like AI and shifting geopolitical landscapes forcing companies to pivot.
  • Practical implication: CEOs should cultivate organizational cultures that embrace uncertainty rather than paralyzing operations with excessive risk aversion.

Role modeling authenticity and alignment.

  • Explanation: Employees read into every word and action of the CEO; therefore, leadership behavior must be intentional and aligned with stated strategic priorities.
  • Evidence: Founders and CEOs in Asia doubling market share over 25 years through mission-focused leadership.
  • Practical implication: CEOs must walk the talk, spending time on strategic priorities and modeling continuous learning.

Examples & Case Studies

Doug McMillon took over Walmart when retail was widely considered 'dead' due to e-commerce and lack of physical assets.

  • Illustrates: Reframing uncertainty into an operational advantage.
  • Lesson: Walmart reframed its 4,700 locations within 90% of the US population as an unmatched fulfillment engine, transforming uncertainty into a massive competitive moat.

Reed Hastings and Netflix maintained both the DVD rental business and the nascent streaming business in parallel for four years.

  • Illustrates: Managing strategic tempo and holding strategic tension.
  • Lesson: Knowing when to hold both models until technology, content, and market conditions aligned for a simultaneous global rollout in 130 countries.

Satya Nadella shut down phone hardware at Microsoft and brought mobile-first cloud-first strategy to life.

  • Illustrates: Breaking collective action problems and stopping obsolete activities.
  • Lesson: Decisive leadership requires stopping legacy operations to fund future growth engines.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Six strategic decisions are claimed to be structurally undelegatable to anyone but the CEO: choosing how to engage, owning the value-creation thesis, resource allocation, pacing/tempo, navigating uncertainty, and acting as catalyst. The framework is a role-based partition of decision rights rather than a task decomposition.

Why it matters

It gives a concrete template for partitioning decision authority between a root orchestrator and sub-agents: a small set of reserved, root-only decisions (objective/valuation model, budget reallocation, tempo control, escalation on uncertainty) versus delegable execution. Delegating these produces drift, not just worse output.

Generalization

Any hierarchical planning/delegation system should explicitly enumerate which decisions are non-delegable at the root, and treat that enumeration as an architectural artifact rather than an emergent property of the prompt.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The CEO role is justified by three distinct assets: cross-company perspective (broader information range than any sub-unit), cumulative experience/calibration judgment, and structural authority that can break collective action problems and organizational inertia. Authority, not intelligence, is what resolves deadlock.

Why it matters

Multi-agent systems often rely on negotiation or consensus between peers with local views. This claim implies deadlocks are resolved by a node holding write authority over shared resources (priorities, budgets, shared state), not by smarter local reasoning.

Generalization

Coordination architectures need a designated authority with unilateral write access to shared scarce resources; perspective breadth and authority should be co-located in the same node, otherwise the node with the global view cannot act on it.

AI increases the supply of data and analysis but not thinking, judgment, or conviction — the effect is to raise the premium and returns on good judgment by the leadership team, rather than to substitute for it.

Resource allocation is a strategic act, not an administrative one: strategy 'dies on the vine without funding,' and high performers reallocate dynamically and eliminate activities inconsistent with the strategy. The reallocation includes non-financial scarce resources — executive time and attention.

Why it matters

Maps directly onto runtime resource control in agent systems: token/compute/tool budgets, and also the scarce 'attention' resource (context, human review cycles). Static allocation plus additive accretion of workstreams is the named failure mode.

Generalization

Any system with accumulating parallel workstreams needs an explicit de-funding mechanism — a scheduled, authority-backed revocation of budget from work not aligned with the current objective — or it degrades into an unfunded portfolio.

Strategy and tactics fail in asymmetric ways: strategy without tactics is slow; tactics without strategy is noise before defeat. Separately, tempo is itself a managed variable — knowing when to speed up or slow down the organization — and uncertainty is treated as an unfreezing moment that permits changes normally blocked by inertia.

Why it matters

Argues against one-way planner→executor pipelines and against decoupled autonomy. The plan must be continuously re-grounded by execution feedback, while execution must stay tethered to a thesis. Tempo control (when to pause, batch, or accelerate) is a first-class runtime knob rather than a fixed loop rate.

Generalization

Agent runtimes should expose an explicit tempo/phase controller (explore vs commit vs pause-and-replan) and treat disruption windows as the sanctioned time to change architecture or prior commitments.

Delegation-ablation of root-only decisions in multi-agent runtimes

Research question

Which of the six claimed undelegatable decisions actually cause objective drift, deadlock, or budget waste when delegated to sub-agents, and which are merely organizational artifacts?

Why

The pass-1 framework asserts a reserved root decision set but provides no ablation evidence. A runtime that delegates thesis ownership, allocation, or tempo may fail silently rather than loudly, so the authority boundary must be tested, not assumed.

Quantifying the adjudication premium as analysis gets cheaper

Research question

Why

If cheap analysis raises the premium on judgment rather than substituting for it, then instrumentation should target selection criteria, commitment, and de-commitment—not tool-call volume or sub-agent count. Project ideas

3

Reserved-Rights Runtime

gatehouse

Delegating any root-only decision (value thesis, budget allocation, tempo control) to sub-agents increases objective drift and deadlock rate compared with a coordinator holding an explicit capability token for those decisions.

Proof of concept

Build a coordinator with capability tokens for thesis, allocation, and tempo; sub-agents lack write access to shared budget and objective state. Run the same tasks with one root decision delegated at a time.

Measurement

Objective-drift score against the declared thesis, deadlock rate, re-planning frequency, budget waste, and success rate versus a baseline where all decisions are delegated.

Source video ↗

Judgment Gate Benchmark

beyond-evals

Outcome variance attributable to the adjudication gate exceeds variance attributable to analysis volume when analysis volume is held constant.

Proof of concept

Construct paired decision tasks with fixed generated options; vary gate quality across random selection, weak adjudicator, strong adjudicator, and human commitment. Measure selected-option quality and regret.

Measurement

Decision accuracy, regret versus oracle, option count generated, time-to-commit, and percentage of options killed versus left open.

Source video ↗

Dynamic Defund Scheduler

movement-lab

A periodic authority-backed defunding pass that revokes budget and context from strategy-inconsistent workstreams reduces token waste and improves long-horizon success versus static allocation.

Proof of concept

Add a scheduler that scores each workstream against the current objective, revokes compute/context from low-consistency workstreams, and reallocates to high-consistency ones. A/B against a fixed-budget baseline.

Measurement

Token and context waste, latency, number of abandoned workstreams, reallocation frequency, and long-horizon task success rate.

Architectural implications

4

Decision rights are partitioned into a reserved root set and a delegable set, rather than every capability being pushed to sub-agents.

Before

All planning, prioritization, and budgeting is delegated to sub-agents or emergent from their interaction.

After

A small enumerated set of root-only decisions — engagement choice, value thesis, allocation, tempo, uncertainty escalation, change catalysis — is enforced by the runtime and cannot be delegated downward.

Consequence

Reduces objective drift across agents and makes the authority boundary inspectable and testable.

Source video ↗

Coordination shifts from peer negotiation to a structurally authorized node that can unilaterally change shared priorities and budgets.

Before

Agents negotiate or vote on shared resource allocation; deadlocks are resolved by timeout or escalation heuristics.

After

A coordinator holding global-view information also holds write authority over budgets, priorities, and shared state, using it to break inertia and collective action problems.

Consequence

Deadlock resolution becomes deterministic and attributable, at the cost of requiring the coordinator to actually possess the broad view it acts on.

Source video ↗

Analysis capacity is decoupled from decision authority.

Before

More data, more tools, and more parallel agents are assumed to improve decisions.

After

A cheap, wide analysis layer feeds a narrow judgment/adjudication gate where options are selected, committed to, and killed.

Consequence

Cost scales with analysis; consequences scale with judgment — so instrumentation should target the gate, not the analysis volume.

Source video ↗

'Attention' and budget are treated as revocable, not allocated once.

Before

Workstreams, agents, and contexts persist indefinitely once funded; allocation is additive.

After

A recurring reallocation pass defunds non-consistent work and reclaims context, reviewer time, and compute.

Consequence

Requires a first-class kill/defund mechanism and an explicit strategy-consistency criterion to evaluate workstreams against.
