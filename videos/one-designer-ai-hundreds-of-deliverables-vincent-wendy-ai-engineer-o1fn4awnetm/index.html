Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Vincent Wendy
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: How a single senior creative designer leverages AI (specifically Devin and LLMs) to scale production across hundreds of conference deliverables for the AI Engineer World's Fair.
  • Purpose: To demonstrate practical workflows where AI augments human designers to achieve unprecedented production scale and reduce operational friction in large-scale event production. Vincent Wendy, Senior Creative Designer at AI Engineer, explains how a solo designer can handle hundreds of deliverables for a massive conference (7,000 attendees, 140+ sponsors, 300+ speakers, 600+ sessions) by treating AI tools like Devin as team members. By building a solid design foundation, establishing reusable design systems, automating workflows with MCP and specs, and validating outputs, a single designer can overcome the scale problem and handle exceptions efficiently.

Topic Map

The Scale Problem in Event Design

  • Explanation: Events at scale require thousands of distinct design deliverables across physical and digital formats, ranging from sponsor assets and badges to signage, wayfinding, and landing pages.
  • Key claims:
    • Every single thing needs design at a large conference.
    • A thousand details mean a thousand ways to fail.
    • It seems impossible to handle so many deliverables with a tiny team without automation.
  • Examples:
    • 7,000 attendees, 140+ sponsors, 300+ speakers, 600+ sessions managed by one designer.
    • Physical assets like t-shirts, lanyards, badges, street flags, banners, and swag bags.
    • Digital assets like landing pages, speaker announcements, thumbnails, and digital signage.
  • Terminology:
    • scale problem
    • deliverables
    • design system
  • Why it matters: Traditional design workflows break down entirely when faced with the combinatorial explosion of custom collateral required for massive modern conferences.

Tools vs. Having a Real Problem

  • Explanation: Having access to advanced AI tools is no longer an advantage on its own; having a real, specific problem to solve is what drives effective AI utilization.
  • Key claims:
    • Tools aren't the advantage anymore; having a real problem is.
    • Designers must think outside the box regardless of LLM capability limits.
  • Examples:
    • Simon Willison's 2025 LLM capability test asking LLMs to create an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle.
    • Using ChatGPT to generate a style image (PNG) and vectorizing it in Figma to bypass native SVG generation flaws.
  • Terminology:
    • LLM capabilities
    • vectorize
    • out of the box
  • Why it matters: Relying purely on out-of-the-box LLM outputs often fails for professional design tasks, requiring creative workarounds combined with human expertise.

Five-Step Framework to Solve the Scale Problem

  • Explanation: A structured approach to scaling design operations using AI: Foundation First, Reusable Designs, Automated Workflows, Validate Output, and Remove Friction.
  • Key claims:
    • Establish foundation elements (design system, typography, colors, component library, grid system) before deploying AI.
    • Reusable designs allow marketing and other teams to self-serve assets based on established branding.
    • Automating workflows replaces manual Figma tasks with AI agent interactions.
  • Examples:
    • Using Devin to enforce typography and spacing guidelines across mobile and desktop breakpoints.
    • Automated generation of speaker announcements, schedules, and trading cards.
  • Terminology:
    • foundation
    • atomic design
    • automated workflows
    • MCP
  • Why it matters: Without a rigorous foundation and automated pipeline, AI tools will generate inconsistent, unpolished, and off-brand outputs.

Automating Workflows and Validating Everything

  • Explanation: Leveraging AI agents like Devin combined with MCP (Model Context Protocol) and spec sheets to pull live event data, generate accurate collateral, and validate visual consistency.
  • Key claims:
    • Automated workflows eliminate the traditional friction and feedback loops between designers and developers.
    • AI can perform visual validation tasks with high accuracy, such as checking for missing sponsor logos.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

When an agent's target output format is a known weakness of the model, route around it by generating an intermediate artifact in a modality the model handles well and converting it with a deterministic professional tool — e.g., generate a raster style image (PNG) with ChatGPT, then vectorize it in Figma instead of asking for native SVG.

Why it matters

Gives a concrete pattern for capability-boundary workarounds in agent pipelines: rather than iterating on prompts for a format the model cannot reliably produce, insert a lossless conversion step performed by deterministic tooling, preserving correctness of the final artifact.

Generalization

Any multi-tool agent system should identify per-model output-format weaknesses and place deterministic converters (linters, compilers, format transformers) at those boundaries instead of trusting generation directly.

[!]Failure Modemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Generation scales only after a strictly defined foundation: colors, typography, spacing, component library, and grid must be fixed and enforced through agent prompts before automation is applied, because unconstrained LLMs inject random font sizes and styling drift that propagates into every downstream artifact.

Why it matters

Names a concrete propagation failure: a single unconstrained generation step contaminates hundreds of derivative artifacts, making retroactive correction expensive. Front-loading constraint definition is cheaper than post-hoc cleanup at scale.

Generalization

In any high-fan-out generation pipeline, immutable schemas/conventions must be specified and machine-enforced upstream of generation; constraint drift compounds multiplicatively with output volume.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

The design artifact is treated as a derived render of live event data: agents pull room schedules via MCP into Figma and export PNGs, so a schedule change becomes a regeneration of outputs rather than manual rework.

Why it matters

Moves the source of truth out of the design file and into structured event data, making artifacts reproducible and idempotently re-renderable. This is the difference between editable assets and regenerable outputs under agent control.

Generalization

Wherever high-volume artifacts depend on mutable data, architecture should separate data-of-record, generation logic, and rendered artifact so that any data change triggers cheap re-render instead of hand-editing instances.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The robustness metric for an automated design workflow is not throughput on the happy path but how cheaply it absorbs exceptions — last-minute schedule updates and one-off edits — leaving humans to handle only the residual cases.

Why it matters

Reframes evaluation of agentic workflows around exception cost rather than average-case volume; a pipeline that produces hundreds of artifacts but requires manual rework on every change is not actually scaled.

Generalization

Agentic production systems should be benchmarked on time-to-incorporate-perturbation (data change, correction, new requirement), not only on volume generated, because exception handling is where human labor reaccumulates.

[P]Practicelow noveltyweak evidence

Constraint artifacts (templates, schema, style contracts) are the scalable unit of delegation: publishing them lets others generate safely without per-request expert involvement.

Accuracy limits of model-based validation on rendered artifacts

Research question

Under what conditions does vision-model validation of mass-produced artifacts (sponsor logos on banners, headshot matching) actually hold near 100% accuracy, and which defect classes break it?

Why

The entire scaling claim rests on replacing exhaustive human review with model review plus escalation; if accuracy collapses on occluded, low-contrast, or near-duplicate cases, the human review labor reaccumulates exactly where volume is highest.

Deterministic conversion at model capability boundaries

Research question

For vector asset generation, does a raster-generate-then-vectorize pipeline outperform direct model-native SVG generation on fidelity, editability, and downstream render determinism?

Why

It gives a generalizable routing rule for agent pipelines: never ask a model to emit a format it is weak at; generate in a modality it handles well and convert with deterministic tooling that can be tested independently.

Exception cost as the true robustness metric for agentic production

Research question

At what perturbation rate and per-exception human cost does a scaled artifact generation pipeline stop reducing total labor compared to manual production?

Why

Throughput on the happy path can be arbitrarily high while the workflow remains unscaled if every schedule change forces manual rework; exception cost is the measurable threshold that determines whether delegation actually pays off.

Why

Article ideas

4

Your Agentic Pipeline's Only Honest Metric Is Exception Cost

Volume generated on the happy path is a vanity metric for generative production systems; the real scaling property is time-to-incorporate-perturbation, and pipelines that hide their exception rate are repackaging manual labor as automation.

Angle

Reframe evaluation of agentic pipelines around perturbation handling, using last-minute event schedule changes as the canonical test case, and argue that most 'scaled' claims are unmeasured on this axis.

Source video ↗

Stop Asking the Model for the Format It's Bad At

Capability-boundary routing — generating in a modality the model handles well and converting with deterministic tooling — beats prompt iteration on output formats the model cannot reliably produce, and the converter is the testable component.

Angle

Use the raster-then-vectorize workaround as the worked example and generalize it to a routing rule for any multi-tool agent system with a format-specific weak spot.

Source video ↗

Design Systems Must Become Executable Contracts Once Agents Are the Primary Authors

When an agent writes the design file, a design system documented as guidance is worthless; it must be machine-enforceable constraint that is validated on rendered output, because a single unconstrained generation step contaminates every derivative artifact.

Angle

Argue that prompt-level constraint specification and programmatic artifact linting are different guarantees, and that only the latter is auditable at hundreds of outputs.

Source video ↗

The 100% Validation Claim Is Where Agentic Production Will Break

Self-reported near-perfect model validation accuracy on mass-produced artifacts is an unverified load-bearing assumption; without an adversarial defect benchmark, verifier failure ships silently at exactly the volume where it is least detectable.

Angle

Interrogate the sponsor-logo and headshot validation claims as a methodology problem: sample size, defect taxonomy, occlusion and near-duplicate cases, and the escalation path that detects verifier failure.

Project ideas

4

Rendered-Artifact Validator Adversarial Benchmark

beyond-evals

Vision-model verification of 'is this logo present and correct on the banner' falls well below reported accuracy on occluded, low-contrast, partially-overlapping, and near-duplicate-logo cases, and false negatives cluster by defect class rather than at random.

Proof of concept

Generate synthetic banner layouts from a ground-truth sponsor set, inject a labeled defect taxonomy (missing logo, wrong logo, cropped, low contrast, occluded by another element, near-duplicate variants), run multiple vision models as verifiers, and record a confusion matrix per defect class.

Measurement

Per-defect-class false-negative and false-positive rates versus the reported near-100% figure, plus verifier confidence calibration and the fraction of defects that a human must adjudicate.

Source video ↗

Format-Boundary Router

new

For vector-asset tasks, a raster-generate-then-deterministic-vectorize pipeline yields higher fidelity and editability than direct model-native SVG generation, and the gap widens as asset complexity increases.

Proof of concept

Build a harness that takes the same asset spec down two paths — direct SVG emission versus PNG generation followed by vectorization — renders both at multiple scales, and scores them on shape fidelity, path count, and editability heuristics.

Measurement

Fidelity score against a reference asset, number of vector paths/nodes, and human editability rating per path, evaluated across a set of asset complexity tiers.

Source video ↗

Exception-Cost Instrumented Generator

movement-lab

There is a measurable perturbation rate above which a scaled artifact pipeline's total human time exceeds manual production, and that threshold is dominated by per-exception handling cost rather than generation volume.

Proof of concept

Instrument a template-driven artifact generator to log every source-data change, its propagation into a regeneration, the human time each exception consumed, and the escalation path taken; sweep perturbation rates from low to high.

Measurement

Human minutes per exception, exception rate per hundred artifacts, and total human minutes per hundred artifacts versus a manual baseline, locating the break-even perturbation rate.

Source video ↗

Versioned Re-Render Pipeline for Design-Tool Runtime

gatehouse

Artifacts generated by an agent inside a design tool can be made byte-reproducible and rollback-safe when source data and generation logic are both versioned and generation is pinned, contradicting the assumption that design-tool runtimes are inherently non-deterministic.

Proof of concept

Prototype a pipeline where event data-of-record and the generation spec live in version control, the agent renders artifacts from a pinned revision, and any artifact can be regenerated and diffed against the previously shipped version.

Measurement

Regeneration reproducibility rate (identical output at the same pinned revision), diff size per data change, and time-to-rollback after an intentionally corrupted upstream data commit.

Architectural implications

3

The design tool becomes an agent execution target rather than a manual canvas.

Before

A designer manually manipulates Figma documents and exports files by hand.

After

An agent (Devin) is driven by specs and MCP data sources to generate and export artifacts inside Figma.

Consequence

Design-system rules must become machine-enforceable/executable constraints, not just documentation, since the agent is the primary writer of the file.

Verification responsibility moves from exhaustive human review to model-based validation with human escalation.

Before

A human art director inspects every deliverable for correctness and brand fit.

After

AI validates artifacts against source data (logos present, headshots matched) and humans inspect exceptions.

Consequence

Requires defining and instrumenting an escalation path; validation accuracy/coverage becomes a first-class production metric and failure surface.

Source video ↗

Artifacts are treated as renders derived from structured data rather than standalone files.

Before

Edits are applied directly to individual deliverable files.

After

Data changes invalidate and regenerate the derived artifacts (schedule change → new PNGs).

Consequence

Enables cheap idempotent re-render at scale, but raises dependency on the data-of-record being correct and on the generation logic being deterministic enough to reproduce outputs.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Unconstrained generation vs. foundation investment

Benefit

Starting to generate immediately produces artifacts fast.

Cost or risk

Unconstrained LLM output introduces random font sizes and styling inconsistencies that propagate across all derivatives; fixes require upstream redefinition and mass regeneration.

Source video ↗

AI visual validation accuracy claims

Benefit

Automated validation can check large volumes (140+ logos) with claimed 100% accuracy, replacing exhaustive human review.

Cost or risk

Self-reported accuracy with no described methodology, sample size, or adversarial cases; over-trusting the verifier could let errors ship at scale and remove the human signal needed to detect verifier failure.

Source video ↗

Decentralized self-serve asset creation

Benefit

Other teams generate their own emails, flyers, and documents without designer involvement, absorbing demand.

Cost or risk

Brand integrity depends entirely on the templates being correct and sufficient; edge cases outside the template's envelope are handled by non-experts or not at all.

Source video ↗

Eliminating designer/developer feedback loops

Benefit

Automated workflows remove the traditional handoff friction between designers and developers.

Cost or risk

Friction that previously functioned as a review gate is removed; errors must now be caught by the automated validation layer rather than by human handoff.
