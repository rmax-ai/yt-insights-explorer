Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Zhilin Yang
  • Channel: Kimi AI
  • Main topic: Scaling strategies for open-source AI models, focusing on Kimi K2.5, MuonClip optimizer, Kimi Linear attention, attention residuals, and agent swarms.
  • Purpose: To explain the architectural innovations and scaling laws employed by Moonshot AI in developing Kimi K2.5 and its associated open-source techniques. Zhilin Yang, Founder and CEO of Kimi (Moonshot AI), presents the scaling strategies behind Kimi K2.5 at NVIDIA GTC 2026. The talk covers three key scaling dimensions: token efficiency via MuonClip optimizer, long context via Kimi Linear attention, and agent swarms via multi-agent reinforcement learning. Additionally, it highlights Kimi K2.5 as the first open model with native joint vision-text pretraining, achieving superior performance across modalities.

Key Points

MuonClip doubles token efficiency over AdamW

  • Explanation: By applying the second-order Muon optimizer with weight decay and consistent RMS updates, model training achieves equivalent intelligence with half the tokens.
  • Evidence: Scaling law graphs showing ~2x token efficiency vs AdamW.
  • Practical implication: Reduces training compute requirements and effectively expands limited high-quality data budgets.

QK-Clip stabilizes large-scale Muon training

  • Explanation: Scaling Muon to 1-trillion parameter models causes max logits to explode; QK-Clip constrains query and key projections in attention heads to prevent divergence.
  • Evidence: Validation loss and max logits training curves before and after QK-Clip.
  • Practical implication: Allows stable training of large transformer models with advanced second-order optimizers.

Kimi Linear Attention balances efficiency and expressivity

  • Explanation: Using a 3:1 ratio of linear attention (Kimi Delta Attention) to full attention layers with chunkwise formulation cuts memory overhead while outperforming full attention.
  • Evidence: Performance benchmarks on RULER and MMLU across 128k to 1M token lengths.
  • Practical implication: Drastically reduces time-per-output-token (TPOT) at long context lengths.

Agent swarms accelerate complex task execution

  • Explanation: Orchestrator-driven agent swarms decompose complex tasks into parallel subtasks, using specialized RL reward functions to ensure successful task completion.
  • Evidence: Execution time vs task complexity graphs showing up to 4.5x speedups.
  • Practical implication: Enables automated execution of complex workflows like software engineering and comprehensive research.

Attention Residuals generalize residual connections

  • Explanation: Treating attention as an LSTM residual rotated by 90 degrees and applying attention residuals across layers improves pretraining token efficiency by 24%.
  • Evidence: Scaling law and benchmark comparison tables for Attention Residuals (AttnRes).
  • Practical implication: Provides a drop-in architectural upgrade over standard residual connections for transformer models.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Kimi's Scaling Strategy Framework

  • How it works: Scales model capabilities across three orthogonal dimensions: Token Efficiency (MuonClip), Long Context (Kimi Linear Attention), and Agent Swarms.
  • Components:
    • Token Efficiency (MuonClip Optimizer)
    • Long Context (Kimi Delta Attention)
    • Agent Swarms (Orchestrator and Sub-agents)

Agent Swarms RL Reward Function

  • How it works: Guides multi-agent training using three weighted reward components to encourage effective task decomposition and completion.
  • Components:
    • Instantiation Reward (incentivizes sub-agent creation, prevents serial collapse)
    • Finish Reward (encourages completion of assigned subtasks)
    • Outcome Reward (measures whether the overall task achieves the end goal)
  • When to use: When training collaborative multi-agent systems via reinforcement learning.

Core insights

6
[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Reward shaping for orchestrator-driven agent swarms requires at least three distinct reward signals — instantiation, finish, and outcome — specifically to prevent two degenerate behaviors: serial collapse (the orchestrator does everything itself) and spurious parallelism (spawning agents that contribute nothing).

Why it matters

Multi-agent speedups are not automatic; without explicit rewards for when to spawn and when to terminate sub-agents, the orchestrator degenerates into either a single serial agent or wasteful fan-out. This tells builders that the decomposition policy itself is a learned, reward-shaped artifact rather than a static planner rule, and that harnesses need per-behavior reward instrumentation, not just a final task-success signal.

Generalization

Any orchestrator/worker architecture (including non-RL planners) needs explicit incentives or heuristics for spawn justification and termination, otherwise fan-out cost is paid without parallelism benefit.

Source video ↗
[A]Architecturehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Kimi Linear uses a hybrid stack with a 3:1 ratio of Kimi Delta Attention (linear) layers to full attention layers, with a chunkwise formulation, and reportedly beats full-attention baselines while cutting memory overhead.

Why it matters

The design claim is that you do not need pure linear attention or pure full attention: a fixed ratio mixture preserves expressivity while getting linear-scaling economics. For serving stacks this shifts the KV-cache/memory budget and the time-per-output-token profile at long context, so the scheduler, paging, and batching assumptions of a standard transformer runtime no longer hold unchanged.

Generalization

Hybrid layer-ratio architectures are a tunable knob between expressivity and memory/compute cost, applicable wherever a recurrent or compressed-state layer can substitute for some full-attention layers.

Source video ↗
[!]Failure Modehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Scaling the Muon optimizer to a 1-trillion-parameter model caused max logits to explode; QK-Clip constrains query and key projections in attention heads to restore stability, enabling ~2x token efficiency over AdamW without divergence.

Why it matters

Second-order/orthogonalized optimizers buy token efficiency but introduce a specific, diagnosable failure mode (attention logit blowup) that must be handled architecturally rather than by lowering learning rate. This gives practitioners a concrete instability signature to watch (max logits) and a targeted intervention (clipping Q/K projections) rather than a generic 'training is unstable' heuristic.

Generalization

Aggressive optimizers can be stabilized by constraining the specific parameter subspace that produces the blowup, instead of globally reducing step size and giving back the efficiency gain.

Source video ↗
[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Attention Residuals (AttnRes) are framed as an LSTM residual rotated into attention space and applied as block attention residuals; they are reported to improve token efficiency by 24% and reduce training loss.

Why it matters

It reframes residual connections as a form of recurrence that can be rotated/generalized rather than a fixed identity skip. If a residual-connection variant alone yields double-digit token efficiency gains, the residual stream is an under-explored design surface, not settled infrastructure — worth prototyping before adding more data or parameters.

Generalization

Cross-pollinating recurrence structure into attention topology (and vice versa) is a productive source of architectural gain, and residual-path redesign is measurable in token efficiency, not just in loss curves.

Source video ↗
[E]Empirical Resultmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Early-fusion (native joint) vision-text pretraining outperforms late fusion, and vision reinforcement learning improves textual performance — i.e., cross-modal transfer flows from vision training into text capability.

Why it matters

This challenges the common practice of bolting a vision encoder onto a frozen or lightly tuned text model with adapters. It implies multimodal capability is a pretraining-pipeline decision, not a post-hoc integration layer, and that multimodal RL is not merely additive to text quality.

Generalization

Modality fusion at pretraining time can act as a regularizer/capability multiplier for the primary modality, so modality investment should be budgeted as core training rather than as a downstream feature.

Source video ↗
[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Kimi frames scaling as three parallel dimensions — token efficiency, context length, and agent swarms — rather than a single axis of parameters or FLOPs, with token efficiency treated as a data multiplier that raises the ceiling of achievable intelligence under a fixed high-quality data budget.

Why it matters

It reframes the binding constraint as data quality/quantity rather than compute, which changes investment priority: optimizer and architecture work that halves tokens-per-unit-intelligence is equivalent to doubling the usable corpus, and is therefore cheaper than acquiring new data. It also gives a portfolio framing for roadmap decisions across training, serving, and agent layers.

Generalization

When a resource is capped (data, budget, latency), efficiency gains in that resource should be evaluated as multipliers on the capped quantity, not as incremental speedups.

Cross-domain transfer and reward hacking of the three swarm reward signals

Research question

Do the instantiation/finish/outcome reward signals produce spawn-efficient orchestration on task families other than the reported parallel-research workloads, or do they overfit and invite reward hacking?

Why

Spawn/terminate behavior is a learned policy with its own degenerate regimes; if the reward mix only works on one task family, teams copying the recipe will pay fan-out cost without parallelism benefit, and their task-success metric will not reveal it.

Attribution of Muon's claimed ~2x token efficiency

Research question

How much of the ~2x token efficiency over AdamW comes from Muon's orthogonalized updates versus weight decay and the RMS-update scheme, and does the ranking survive changes in scale and data distribution?

Why

If most of the gain comes from regularization rather than the optimizer, teams can capture it on a standard optimizer without inheriting the max-logit stability tooling; if not, the efficiency claim justifies the full MuonClip stack and its operational cost.

Source video ↗

QK-Clip mechanism, clip-threshold sensitivity, and its cost to attention expressivity

Research question

What subspace does QK-Clip constrain, and does clipping query/key projections degrade attention sharpness, long-context retrieval, or interact with hybrid linear-attention layers?

Why

QK-Clip is what makes the Muon token-efficiency gain usable at trillion-parameter scale; if it silently refunds that gain as reduced long-context retrieval quality, the two headline results cannot be treated as independent wins.

Attention Residuals composed with hybrid 3:1 KDA/full-attention stacks

Research question

Do AttnRes's 24% token-efficiency gains persist when residual topology is placed over recurrent-state (KDA) layers, or do the gains assume homogeneous full-attention blocks?

Why

Both AttnRes and Kimi Linear are recurrence-flavored modifications to the same stack; if they are reported separately with no interaction ablation, an architecture team cannot know whether to adopt one, both, or neither.

Article ideas

4

Your Orchestrator Is a Learned Policy, Not a Planner

Once task decomposition is trained with spawn and terminate rewards, the decomposition policy acquires its own failure modes — serial collapse and spurious parallelism — so agent harnesses need per-agent lifecycle telemetry and cost attribution at the same priority as the final task score; teams that measure only task success will pay for parallelism they never received.

Angle

Translate the reward-shaping result into concrete harness instrumentation requirements: spawn/terminate accounting, marginal-contribution logging, and cost-per-quality dashboards.

Source video ↗

Token Efficiency Is a Data-Acquisition Strategy, Not an Optimizer Tuning Detail

If halving tokens-per-unit-intelligence is equivalent to doubling the usable high-quality corpus, then optimizer and architecture work should be budgeted against data-acquisition economics — and headline efficiency multipliers must ship with ablations separating the optimizer from its weight-decay and update-normalization components.

Angle

Reframe efficiency research as a multiplier on a capped resource and demand matched-baseline ablations before treating any headline number as portable.

Source video ↗

Hybrid 3:1 Attention Breaks Your Serving Stack's Assumptions

A mixture of linear-to-full attention layers turns time-per-output-token and memory overhead into configurable properties, which means schedulers, paging, batching, and chunk-boundary kernels must be re-derived per layer class instead of inherited from a uniform-transformer runtime.

Angle

Walk through which runtime assumptions (single KV-growth model, homogeneous kernels, fixed TPOT profile) stop holding and what must replace them.

Source video ↗

The Residual Stream Is Not Settled Infrastructure

If reshaping residuals buys double-digit token efficiency, residual topology deserves the ablation budget currently spent on layer count and width — but AttnRes must first be validated on hybrid recurrent stacks, since a gain proven only on full-attention blocks is not yet a general-purpose gain.

Angle

Argue the residual path is an under-explored design surface and specify the ablation matrix needed to promote it from curiosity to default.

Spawn Ledger

beyond-evals

Instrumenting multi-agent runs with per-agent marginal-contribution accounting will expose redundant-agent rates and true cost per unit quality that final-task-success scoring hides, and these figures will differ systematically across reward configurations.

Proof of concept

Wrap an orchestrator/worker harness so every spawn, termination, sub-agent token cost, and each sub-agent's marginal effect on the outcome is logged; replay one task suite under outcome-only reward versus instantiation+finish+outcome reward.

Measurement

Spawn efficiency (contributing agents divided by spawned agents), redundant-agent rate, cost per unit of task quality, and speedup versus a single agent at matched total cost.

Logit Sentinel

movement-lab

Max-logit trajectory is a measurable early-warning signal of large-scale optimizer divergence, and constraining query/key projections restores stability across seeds without degrading long-context retrieval beyond a bounded clip-threshold window.

Proof of concept

Instrument per-step max logits during Muon-style training runs at multiple scales, sweep Q/K clip thresholds, and evaluate long-context retrieval and downstream loss at each threshold.

Measurement

Steps-to-divergence and max-logit slope per configuration; retrieval and loss deltas as a function of clip threshold and random seed.

Efficiency Attribution Harness

movement-lab

A measurable share of the reported ~2x token efficiency over AdamW is attributable to weight decay and RMS update normalization rather than to orthogonalized updates, distinguishable as tokens-to-target-loss at matched settings.

Proof of concept

Run controlled ablations at several model scales comparing AdamW with matched weight decay and RMS normalization against Muon, with tokens-to-target-loss as the dependent variable.

Measurement

tokens-to-target-loss ratio per arm per scale, divergence rate per arm, and the portion of the reported multiplier retained once regularization is matched.

Hybrid-KV Planner

new

A runtime that models a 3:1 linear-to-full attention stack as two layer classes with distinct memory and state-update behavior predicts long-context time-per-output-token and peak memory within a small error margin, where a uniform-transformer model does not.

Proof of concept

Build a scheduler/paging simulator with separate KV-cache growth for full-attention layers and fixed-size recurrent state for linear layers, then validate predictions against measured TPOT and memory across context lengths.

Measurement

Predicted versus observed TPOT and peak memory at long context, and the deviation of the uniform-transformer baseline's predictions from the same measurements.

Architectural implications

4

Attention layer composition is no longer homogeneous across the stack.

Before

Uniform full self-attention in every layer, with a single KV-cache growth model and quadratic long-context cost.

After

A 3:1 interleaving of Kimi Delta Attention (chunkwise, recurrent-state) with full attention layers.

Consequence

Serving runtimes must account for mixed layer types: memory budgeting, chunk boundaries, matrix-inversion/state-update kernels, and batching/parallelism strategies differ per layer class, and long-context TPOT becomes a configurable rather than fixed property.

Source video ↗

Multi-agent swarms are trained end-to-end with reward shaping over orchestration behavior, not assembled from a static planner.

Before

A single agent with a long context and hand-written decomposition logic.

After

An orchestrator that spawns specialist sub-agents in parallel, optimized with instantiation, finish, and outcome rewards.

Consequence

Task decomposition becomes a learned policy with its own failure modes (serial collapse, spurious parallelism), so harnesses need per-agent lifecycle telemetry, spawn/terminate accounting, and cost attribution to detect useless parallelism.

Source video ↗

The residual stream itself becomes a design surface.

Before

Standard identity residual (x + f(x)) repeated uniformly across layers.

After

Attention residuals / block attention residuals, motivated as an LSTM residual rotated into attention space.

Consequence

Layer-level memory and gradient-flow properties change, so architecture search and ablation budgets shift from layer count/width toward residual topology.

Source video ↗

Multimodal integration moves from the post-training/integration layer into pretraining.

Before

Late fusion: adapters or projection heads attached to a text-pretrained backbone.

After

Early fusion: native joint vision-text pretraining.

Consequence

Data pipeline, tokenizer, sequence packing, and training-time modality mixing become coupled concerns; vision RL can feed back into text quality, so modality experiments stop being isolated feature work.

Source video ↗

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Muon-style second-order optimization at scale

Benefit

~2x token efficiency over AdamW, effectively multiplying a limited high-quality data budget.

Cost or risk

Max logit explosion / training divergence at trillion-parameter scale, requiring QK-Clip intervention and presumably extra stability tooling and monitoring.

Source video ↗

Linear attention hybridization

Benefit

Memory overhead reduction and superior long-context performance versus full-attention baselines, plus lower TPOT at long context.

Cost or risk

Reduced expressivity of recurrent-state layers plus implementation complexity (chunkwise formulation, matrix inversion), and the 3:1 ratio is an added tuning dimension that must be validated per scale.

Source video ↗

Parallel agent swarms

Benefit

Up to 4.5x speedups on complex tasks, e.g. a 100-page literature review via parallel researchers and fact-checkers.

Cost or risk

Reward hacking toward degenerate regimes — serial collapse (no real parallelism) or spurious parallelism (agents spawned without contributing) — which inflates cost and latency without quality gain.

Source video ↗

Early-fusion multimodal pretraining

Benefit

Better results than late fusion, with cross-modal transfer from vision RL into textual performance.

Cost or risk

Commitment to joint modality data pipeline and pretraining from the start instead of cheaper adapter-based retrofitting; abandoning the single-modality backbone path limits reuse of existing text-only checkpoints.
