Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Vinoo Ganesh
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Preventing LLMs from hallucinating numerical data in financial applications using deterministic code, retrieval, and streaming filters.
  • Purpose: To explain Kepler's architecture for verifiable numerical data retrieval, computation, and citation filtering in AI systems. Vinoo Ganesh, CEO of Kepler, presents an architecture and methodology for preventing language models from inventing numbers in mission-critical environments like finance. By separating planning (handled by LLMs) from execution (handled by deterministic code, retrieval, and strict citation registries), Kepler ensures every figure is retrieved, computed, and verified. The talk explores how standard RAG and LLM wrappers fail when dealing with financial data, why a third of citations are often math models fail to do, and five core rules for ensuring verifiable numerical AI systems.

Topic Map

The Problem of Numerical Hallucination in LLMs

  • Explanation: LLMs are probability machines at their core. While this makes them creative, it makes them unreliable for mission-critical numerical tasks like financial analysis where every number must be traceable.
  • Key claims:
    • LLMs do not read documents directly; they chunk text, perform semantic search, and retrieve semi-relevant information.
    • Attaching sources after the fact leads to wrong sources, wrong numbers due to scaling issues, and incorrect mental math.
    • A third of all citations in financial tasks are math the model did not actually perform.
  • Examples:
    • Pulling information from random blogs stating Palantir is worth $100,000 a share.
    • Reading a 10K and confusing the number 17 for millions, billions, yen, or page numbers.
  • Terminology:
    • probability machines
    • post-hoc citation
    • semantic search
  • Why it matters: Financial analysts and enterprise users cannot trust AI outputs that invent or miscalculate critical figures.

Kepler's Architecture: Separation of Planning and Deterministic Software

  • Explanation: Kepler implements an architecture where language models handle planning, while deterministic code handles retrieval, computation, and post-processing verification.
  • Key claims:
    • The model cites numbers but never writes them.
    • Retrieval finds the source, deterministic code does the math, and a post-processing filter checks every receipt.
    • Every number must be citable only if a tool registered it.
  • Examples:
    • Processing SEC data across 30+ million documents using citation registries.
  • Terminology:
    • citation registry
    • streaming citation filter
    • determinism
  • Why it matters: Ensures absolute verifiability and auditability of all numerical outputs.

Search and Reranking Strategy

  • Explanation: Combining Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) vector search with BM25 keyword search, followed by multi-stage reranking to ensure precise source retrieval.
  • Key claims:
    • Vector and BM25 searches run concurrently, over-fetching to top K.
    • Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) combines search results.
    • A trusted cross-encoder reranks results to filter out conflicting disclosures across documents.
  • Examples:
    • Resolving discrepancies between earnings call transcripts and 10-K filings.
  • Terminology:
    • ANN
    • BM25
    • Reciprocal Rank Fusion
    • cross-encoder
  • Why it matters: Ensures high recall and precision when querying vast unstructured and structured enterprise data.

Tenant Isolation and Security

  • Explanation: Enterprise AI requires strict document-level security to prevent data leakage between different users and internal datasets.
  • Key claims:
    • Every search hit is re-authorized in Postgres across tenant identity, ownership, and document access.
    • Builders add tenant filters dynamically to queries.
    • Only the auth subsystem can create scoped tokens.
  • Examples:
    • Preventing Citadel Desk 1 from accessing Citadel Desk 2's research.
  • Terminology:
    • tenant isolation
    • scoped token
    • post-authorization
  • Why it matters: Protects sensitive internal documents, SharePoint files, OneDrive data, and proprietary financial models from unauthorized access.

Key Points

The model plans; deterministic software handles every number

  • Explanation: LLMs should never write raw numbers directly. They produce plans and tool calls, while deterministic code executes math and formatting.
  • Evidence: Kepler's production data showing thousands of rejected citations due to mismatches.
  • Practical implication: Reduces hallucination rates to zero for numerical output generation.

Streaming citation filter strips unverified numbers mid-stream

  • Explanation: If a generated number cannot be matched against a registered source in the citation registry, the filter strips it during generation.
  • Evidence: Over 10,981 citations refused by the filter in production tests.
  • Practical implication: Guarantees that users never see unverified or hallucinated figures.

Traditional RAG is insufficient for numerical accuracy

  • Explanation: Standard vector database retrieval combined with unstructured LLM prompting fails at auditing, math, and scaling.
  • Evidence: A third of citations in naive RAG setups are unverified math or incorrect source lookups.
  • Practical implication: Engineers building financial or audit AI must adopt hybrid structured-unstructured registries.

Research question

Why

The architecture's guarantee is only as strong as its coverage. Coverage rate, not model quality, may dominate end-task usefulness.

Article ideas

4

Your RAG Pipeline Doesn't Have a Retrieval Problem, It Has an Arithmetic Problem

Retrieval accuracy and computational accuracy are independent failure surfaces, and teams conflate them: they invest in embeddings and recall while the system keeps emitting figures it never computed and numbers whose units it silently rescaled. The fix is not better retrieval but a value registry that makes the model a reference emitter rather than a number generator.

Angle

Decompose real failure cases into misattribution, unit scaling, and fabricated arithmetic, then show that only the first responds to retrieval improvements.

Source video ↗

Make It Unrepresentable: Turning Hallucination Mitigation from Prompting into Type-Checking

Behavioral guardrails (better prompts, polite instructions, self-check passes) are probabilistic and regress with every model swap. Structural guarantees — the model emits plans and references, tools emit values — convert numerical correctness into a type error. The honest trade is coverage brittleness for structural impossibility, and that trade should be made explicitly rather than discovered in production.

Angle

Frame the shift as moving a correctness property from the model's behavior to the system's type system, and be candid about what breaks when the tool surface is incomplete.

Source video ↗

Guardrails Belong in the Token Stream, Not the Eval Suite

Post-hoc verification and offline evaluation audit outputs after the fact and depend on the model behaving as it did when you measured it. Enforcement placed in the output path — validating each value as it is generated and suppressing anything without a registered source — survives model regression, prompt drift, and planner degradation, at the cost of streaming complexity and content loss that must itself be measured.

Angle

Compare enforcement points along the generation pipeline and argue the gate should sit where the user's read happens.

Source video ↗

Rerankers Aren't Just Rankers: Handling Contradictory Sources in Enterprise Corpora

In real corpora, documents disagree — a transcript and a filing state the same fact differently. Relevance ranking cannot resolve this, because the most similar passage is not the authoritative one. Retrieval pipelines need an explicit arbitration stage where source authority is a modeled input, and the reranker is the natural place to encode it.

Angle

Treat cross-document contradiction as a first-class retrieval stage rather than an edge case, with hybrid over-fetch plus fusion plus authority-aware reranking.

Coverage Gap Harness

beyond-evals

On realistic analyst-style queries, a registry-only numeric gate strips a substantial share of figures that are legitimate but unregistered, and end-task usefulness is dominated by registry coverage rather than by model quality.

Proof of concept

Build a deterministic-number agent over a financial QA set with a deliberately incomplete tool surface. Log every number the streaming filter suppresses, then hand-label each as true hallucination versus coverage gap, and re-run with an expanded tool surface to measure the delta.

Measurement

Strip precision/recall, coverage-gap rate per query, fraction of queries answered fully versus partially versus refused, and usefulness delta between incomplete and expanded tool surfaces.

In-Flight Numeric Gate

gatehouse

Verifying values in the token stream catches strictly more unregistered figures than post-hoc verification of completed responses, at a latency cost that stays within an interactive budget at p95.

Proof of concept

Implement two enforcement variants behind one interface — a streaming filter that matches partial numbers against a registry as they are emitted, and a post-hoc validator over finished text — and run both against identical generated responses containing seeded numeric errors.

Measurement

p50/p95 time-to-first-verified-token, catch rate on seeded errors, false-strip rate on valid numbers, and total tokens suppressed per response.

Unit-Carrying Value Registry

new

Most scaling and unit confusion in filing-derived figures disappears when values are registered as typed tuples carrying magnitude, currency, and source span, and rendering is done by deterministic code rather than by the model.

Proof of concept

Define a value schema of (amount, unit, scale, period, source_span) with a deterministic renderer, back it with a small tool set for retrieval and arithmetic, and compare against a baseline where the model writes bare number tokens.

Measurement

Unit/scale error rate per generated figure against a gold-annotated filing set, plus registry hit rate and rendering mismatch count.

Source Authority Arbitration Bench

movement-lab

An authority-aware reranking stage selects the correct source on contradictory-fact queries significantly more often than relevance-only top-K selection, at measurable latency cost.

Proof of concept

Construct a benchmark of queries where two retrieved documents state conflicting values with different authority levels, run hybrid ANN+BM25 with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and compare a standard relevance cross-encoder against one conditioned on source metadata.

Measurement

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Registry-only numbers

Benefit

Every emitted figure is traceable to a tool-registered source, eliminating invented numbers.

Cost or risk

Coverage brittleness: any legitimate value not registered by a tool is stripped, so the tool surface and registry schema must be complete or answers degrade to silence.

Source video ↗

Streaming citation filtering

Benefit

Unverified numbers are removed before display, independent of model behavior.

Cost or risk

Production-scale rejection volume (10,981 filtered citations) implies frequent silent content loss and added streaming latency.

Hybrid retrieval with cross-encoder arbitration

Benefit

High recall from dual search plus precision from reranking resolves cross-document conflicts.

Cost or risk

Multiple retrieval and reranking stages increase latency and operational surface area.

Per-hit post-authorization

Benefit

Prevents cross-tenant leakage at the document level.

Cost or risk

Authorization on every search hit adds a database round-trip / check in the hot retrieval path.
