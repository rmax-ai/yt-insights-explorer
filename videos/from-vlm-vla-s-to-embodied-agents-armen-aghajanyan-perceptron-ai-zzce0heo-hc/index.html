  • Main topic: Embodied foundation models, physical AI, and multi-modal architectures for robotics and agentic perception.
  • Purpose: Explain the transition toward embodied foundation models and discuss research findings, architectures, and scaling laws for physical AI systems. Armen Aghajanyan, Co-founder and CEO of Perceptron, discusses the evolution from traditional VLM and VLA models to Embodied Foundation Models that unify perception, reasoning, and action. He highlights fundamental challenges like weak traditional training objectives and context bloat in multimodal models, presents Perceptron's data-sparse mixture of experts approach and Mk1 model, and demonstrates emergent agentic capabilities in robotic tasks.

Core insights

6
[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

The bottleneck in embodied/agentic perception is the training objective, not model capacity: predicting raw pixels gives dense but low-information supervision, while transcript-only supervision is sparse — both produce poor attention allocation. The fix is a different class of objective ('natural perceptive objectives') rather than a bigger model.

Why it matters

If the objective is the limiting factor, teams that keep fine-tuning bigger VLMs on caption/pixel-prediction losses are investing in the wrong axis; harness and data-curation design (what supervision signal is produced) dominates.

Generalization

Applies to any long-horizon agent: supervision that rewards dense low-value tokens wastes capacity, and supervision that only labels sparse high-value events under-trains attention. Objective design should match information density of the input stream.

[~]Tradeoffhigh noveltymoderate evidence

There is a structural information-density asymmetry in always-on multimodal context: video is token-expensive but information-sparse (roughly a million-plus visual tokens per hour with poor ground truth), while text is token-cheap and information-dense. Context budget should therefore be allocated by information density, not by token count.

Why it matters

Motivates per-modality retention/compression policies in agent memory and context managers instead of uniform truncation or uniform summarization; a token budget spent on video is not equivalent to the same budget spent on text.

Generalization

Generalizes to any agent ingesting screenshots, logs, or continuous telemetry alongside text: cheap text should be retained generously, expensive perceptual streams aggressively compressed or selectively sampled.

Source video ↗
[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Data-sparse mixture-of-experts routing is proposed as the direct architectural answer to input sparsity: instead of spending uniform compute per token, a router allocates dynamic compute so FLOPs track information content, which simultaneously addresses context bloat and cost.

Why it matters

Suggests sparse routing belongs at the token/context-selection layer, not only inside the FFN — i.e., which tokens get processed at all is a first-class design decision that changes both latency and cost profiles.

Generalization

Any agent with heterogeneous input streams (tool outputs, logs, frames, retrieved chunks) can apply learned routing to decide what to process deeply versus skip, rather than fixed pipelines.

Deep dives

5

Objective design as the real bottleneck: what replaces pixel and transcript prediction

Research question

Which training objective produces measurably better attention allocation than dense pixel prediction or sparse transcript prediction on the same multimodal data, and at what cost in labeled supervision?

Why

If the objective rather than capacity is the limiting factor, then compute spent on bigger VLMs is misallocated; the team that finds an objective with the right information density per token wins on both cost and grounding. Without a concrete loss/target definition, 'perceptive objectives' remains a slogan that cannot be implemented or falsified.

Source video ↗

Silent recall loss in token-level sparse routing

Research question

Does dynamic per-token compute allocation skip rare-but-decisive perceptual evidence over long horizons, and can a cheap audit signal detect that skip before downstream task failure?

Why

The claimed benefit is cost and focus; the unmeasured risk is that the router becomes a silent single point of failure whose errors never surface as exceptions, only as wrong answers. If rare frames are systematically dropped, any agent built on this architecture has an invisible reliability ceiling that standard accuracy averages will hide.

Source video ↗

Per-modality context budgeting by information density

Research question

Does allocating context budget by information-per-token rather than raw token count improve downstream task performance at fixed cost versus uniform truncation or uniform summarization?

Why

A uniform token budget implicitly prices a cheap dense text token the same as an expensive sparse video token, which is the wrong exchange rate. Per-modality retention and eviction policies are a concrete architectural lever that changes cost, latency, and what the agent can recall, and they need observability into what was dropped to explain misses.

Source video ↗

Agentic perception versus tuned classical vision pipelines

Research question

On accuracy, latency, and cost, when does perception-as-tool-calls (write code, zoom, adjust contrast) beat a tuned fixed CV pipeline, and what does the extra nondeterminism cost in production?

Why

Reframing perception as tool use makes failures debuggable as wrong tool sequences and lets image logic change without a code deploy, but it converts a deterministic stage into a multi-step, latency-added, nondeterministic one. Teams need a decision rule for which perception stages to agentify, not a blanket migration.

Source video ↗

Auditing the 15x-cheaper embodied reasoning claim

Research question

Under an independently specified benchmark and cost accounting (tokens, hardware, latency), does the reported embodied-reasoning advantage over a general frontier model hold, and what does the 15x figure actually include?

Why

A single-vendor comparative claim with no named benchmark, protocol, or cost boundary is not decision-grade evidence, yet it is exactly the kind of number that gets quoted in build-versus-buy decisions. Reproducing or refuting it determines whether specialization is a real economic moat or a benchmark artifact.

Article ideas

4

Your Model Isn't Too Small — Your Training Objective Is Wrong

In multimodal and agentic systems the binding constraint is supervision information density, not parameter count: dense pixel prediction burns capacity on predictable detail while transcript-only supervision under-trains attention on the events that matter, so objective design should be treated as an architectural decision.

Angle

Take the two failure poles (dense-but-low-information pixels, sparse-but-high-information transcripts) and argue that teams scaling models are optimizing the wrong axis; make the case through attention-allocation reasoning and the concrete cost of a million-plus visual tokens per hour of video.

Source video ↗

Token Budgets Are the Wrong Unit for Agent Context

Allocating context by token count silently overprices dense text and underprices perceptual streams; context managers should budget by information density per modality, which means per-modality retention, compression, and eviction policies plus observability into what was dropped.

Angle

Argue from the asymmetry that an hour of video costs millions of tokens while carrying poor ground truth, versus cheap dense text; derive concrete policy rules for screenshots, logs, telemetry, and frames, and show why uniform truncation and uniform summarization both lose.

Source video ↗

The Router Is a Silent Failure Mode

Learned compute allocation is a legitimate answer to input sparsity, but it converts a cost problem into a recall problem that never raises an error — so sparse routing must ship with recall audits for rare-but-decisive tokens, not just FLOPs savings charts.

Angle

Reframe sparse routing as context selection: the router decides what the model never sees, and mis-routing shows up only as downstream task failure, which is invisible in average-accuracy reporting. Propose audit instrumentation and degraded-mode thresholds.

Source video ↗

Perception Should Be a Trace, Not a Pipeline

Moving zoom, crop, and contrast adjustment out of hardcoded preprocessors and into the model's action space makes vision failures debuggable as wrong tool sequences and removable by prompt/tooling change — but the trade is added latency and nondeterminism, so agentifying perception should be a per-stage decision rule, not a migration.

Angle

Compare a fixed detect-crop-OCR-parse stage against an inspectable action trace on debuggability, latency, and cost, and give criteria for which stages deserve agency.

Project ideas

4

Objective Bake-Off Harness

beyond-evals

On identical multimodal data and a fixed FLOP budget, a region/event-grounded perceptive objective yields lower attention entropy and higher downstream task accuracy than dense pixel prediction or transcript-only prediction.

Proof of concept

Build a three-arm training harness over one fixed video/telemetry corpus: (a) raw-pixel prediction, (b) transcript/caption prediction, (c) region- or change-grounded prediction with tool-invoked inspection traces. Hold parameters and token budget constant and log per-layer attention entropy plus downstream probe scores.

Measurement

Attention entropy over semantically annotated events, downstream task accuracy on held-out multi-step inspection tasks, and supervision cost per labeled example across the three arms at matched FLOPs.

Source video ↗

Per-Modality Context Eviction Policy

gatehouse

Allocating context budget by estimated information density per modality beats uniform token-count truncation and uniform summarization on downstream task success at equal total token cost.

Proof of concept

Implement a context manager with pluggable policies (uniform truncation, uniform summarization, density-weighted retention with aggressive perceptual sampling and generous text retention) over sessions mixing screenshots, logs, and text. Run all policies against the same long-horizon task suite with a fixed token ceiling.

Measurement

Task success rate at fixed total tokens, plus drop-recall: fraction of retained key facts/events that were present in the source stream and survived eviction, with per-policy eviction traces for failure explanation.

Source video ↗

Rare-Frame Router Recall Audit

new

Learned token-level sparse routing skips a measurable fraction of rare but task-decisive frames that dense attention retains, and this skip is detectable by injected canary frames before it appears as task failure.

Proof of concept

Construct long perceptual streams seeded with labeled canary frames carrying the only evidence needed to solve a downstream query. Compare router-based selection against dense-attention baselines at matched compute and instrument whether canaries are selected, deferred, or dropped.

Measurement

Canary retention rate versus dense baseline, downstream accuracy on canary-only queries, compute savings, and the gap between canary-retention warnings and observed task failures.

Source video ↗

Two-Tier Latency Contract Simulator

movement-lab

Splitting an embodied agent into a slow semantic orchestrator and a fast tactile/execution policy with an explicit state-goal-safety contract yields more stable control and clearer failure attribution than a single end-to-end observation-to-action loop, at equal task success.

Proof of concept

Build a simulated task loop with a scripted slow planner (variable, occasionally stalling) and a fast deterministic executor. Run conditions: single-tier baseline, two-tier with defined contract, two-tier with planner stalled mid-task. Instrument tier-level traces.

Measurement

Control stability (variance of execution commands), task success under planner stalls, end-to-end latency distribution per tier, and fraction of failures attributable to plan quality versus control instability.

Architectural implications

4

Perception is reframed as agentic tool use rather than a fixed preprocessing pipeline.

Before

Hardcoded CV stages (detect → crop → OCR → parse) with tuned thresholds before the model sees anything.

After

Model emits code/tool calls to zoom, adjust contrast, and re-inspect, producing an inspectable action trace.

Consequence

Perception failures become debuggable as wrong tool sequences, and image-processing logic can be changed by prompt/tooling rather than code deploys — but adds latency and nondeterminism to what used to be a deterministic stage.

Source video ↗

Compute allocation is routed per token rather than applied uniformly.

Before

Every token in a long multimodal context costs the same forward-pass FLOPs.

After

A router dynamically selects which tokens are processed deeply and which are skipped, so compute tracks information density.

Consequence

Cost and latency scale with content rather than raw context length; the router becomes a new single point of failure and a new thing to evaluate for recall loss.

Source video ↗

Control is split into orchestrator and tactile policy tiers.

Before

One model end-to-end maps observation to action at a single update rate.

After

A slow semantic orchestrator plans and a separate tactile control policy executes real-time low-level control.

Consequence

Requires an explicit contract (state, goals, safety envelope) between tiers, and separate SLIs for plan quality versus control stability.

Source video ↗

Modality-specific context management replaces uniform context handling.

Before

Truncate or summarize context uniformly by token count.

After

Retain dense text aggressively and compress/sample sparse perceptual streams selectively.

Consequence

Context managers need per-modality policies and eviction rules, plus observability into what was dropped to explain downstream misses.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Token-skipping sparse routing

Benefit

Dynamic compute per token cuts cost significantly while directing attention to high-density information.

Cost or risk

The router decides what is ignored; mis-routing a rare but critical token produces silent recall loss that only shows up as downstream task failure, not as an error.

Source video ↗

Always-on perception streams

Benefit

Continuous camera/robot input enables real-time grounding and interaction.

Cost or risk

Context bloat: an hour of video explodes into millions of visual tokens whose ground truth is poor, so more context does not mean more usable signal.

Source video ↗

Dense pixel-level video objectives

Benefit

Provides a dense, always-available supervisory signal without human labeling.

Cost or risk

Poor attention allocation and inefficient training — the model spends capacity on predictable pixel detail instead of semantically meaningful events.

Source video ↗

Unified model vs. tiered control (internal tension in the talk)

Benefit

A single embodied foundation model unifies perception, reasoning, and action and reduces architectural fragmentation.

Cost or risk

The same talk describes separating orchestrator models from tactile control policies, implying unification at the representation level coexists with separation at the control-latency level; collapsing both risks latency and safety problems in real-time loops.
