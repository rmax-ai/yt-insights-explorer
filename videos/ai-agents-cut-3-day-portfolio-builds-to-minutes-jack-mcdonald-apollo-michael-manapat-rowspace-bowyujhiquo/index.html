Core insights

6
[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

In regulated financial workflows the agent's primary job is data engineering — reconciling conflicting values across many internal and external sources into an authoritative record — rather than reasoning over already-clean data. The reasoning layer is comparatively commoditized; the reconciliation and provenance layer is the hard, defensible part.

Why it matters

If reconciliation is the bottleneck, engineering effort should go into source-of-truth resolution, conflict policy, and lineage tracking rather than prompt/agent-loop tuning. It relocates the hard problem from the model to the data plane.

Generalization

Any enterprise agent operating on multi-source operational data should be architected as a reconciliation engine with an explicit conflict-resolution policy per field, not as a chat wrapper over a data warehouse.

[E]Empirical Resultmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The useful operating point for these agents is roughly 80-90% autonomous completion with a human closing the final gap, rather than end-to-end autonomy. The economic win comes from compressing a 2-3 day manual build to minutes and reassigning human time to strategic refinement.

Why it matters

It gives a concrete target for agent product design: optimize the last-mile handoff surface (diffing, review UI, what the agent flags as uncertain) because that is where the residual human time is spent, not on pushing raw accuracy past 90%.

Generalization

For high-stakes analytical work, measure value as time-to-90%-draft plus quality of the human review interface, rather than as full-task success rate.

Source video ↗
[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Trust in an enterprise agent is a data-lineage property, not a model property. When 20 sources disagree, the platform must cite and override with the verified internal record; the model's parametric knowledge is explicitly treated as lower-authority than internal provenance.

Why it matters

This implies a precedence hierarchy enforced outside the LLM — internal verified source > external vendor (Bloomberg) > model priors — with attribution rendered to the user. Without it, outputs are unauditable and unusable for decisions.

Generalization

Give agents a deterministic source-precedence resolver and per-field provenance attached to every emitted value; treat model memory as a fallback tier, never the top tier.

Source video ↗
[~]Tradeofflow noveltymoderate evidence

General-purpose models are positioned as research assistants, not system-of-record components: they are strong at open-ended exploration but structurally lack the proprietary context required for decision-grade output, so they are deliberately kept out of the data-authority path.

Why it matters

This is a clean architectural boundary — the LLM is a reasoning/parsing engine, the platform owns truth. It suggests avoiding architectures where the model's own recall is implicitly trusted for numeric or contractual facts.

Generalization

Separate the reasoning engine from the truth engine; route all factual assertions through a retrieval/verification layer and treat unverified model output as a hypothesis to be checked.

Source video ↗
[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Deep, rules-based legal/financial documents (indentures, waterfall tests for CLOs) are an unusually favorable agent substrate: the rules are explicit and checkable, so the agent can be validated against the document rather than trusted. This is why automation rates reach 80-90% in this domain.

Why it matters

Suggests a selection heuristic for where agents will work first: domains where the governing rules are written down and mechanically checkable, giving a free oracle for evaluation and self-verification.

Generalization

Prioritize agent deployments in domains with machine-checkable specifications (contracts, schemas, test suites, compliance rules) because they supply both the task definition and the verifier.

Source video ↗
[P]Practicelow noveltymoderate evidence

Deployment succeeded because engineers embedded with front-office teams to map exact workflow bottlenecks before building, rather than shipping a generic platform capability. Workflow immersion precedes automation.

Why it matters

Enterprise agent quality is bounded by task specification fidelity; the spec lives in the operators' heads and in the artifacts they manipulate, so extraction of the spec is a first-class engineering phase.

Generalization

Budget explicit discovery cycles where engineers sit with operators and capture artifacts (spreadsheets, emails, statements) as fixtures for both the agent build and its regression suite.

Deep dives

4

Per-field precedence and arbitration across conflicting internal systems of record

Research question

When multiple internal systems disagree on the same field (not just internal vs. external), what deterministic precedence and arbitration policy yields the lowest field-level error rate, and how much of that policy can be learned from lineage rather than hand-specified?

Why

The pass-1 insight establishes that trust is a lineage property enforced outside the model, but the described hierarchy (internal > vendor > model priors) collapses as soon as two internal systems conflict. If arbitration is undefined, the 'authoritative record' the agent emits is unauditable, and the reviewer is forced back into full re-derivation, wiping out the time savings that justified the deployment.

Source video ↗

Review-surface information design for auditing a 200-400 name artifact in minutes

Research question

Which review-surface design — full provenance-annotated table diff, exception-only queue, or flag-confidence triage — minimizes time-to-signoff without increasing missed-error rate on a large multi-name artifact?

Why

The residual human effort sits entirely in the last-mile handoff, so the interface, not the model, is the binding constraint on realized value. If the agent's uncertainty flags are imprecise, the reviewer must redo the whole artifact and the days-to-minutes gain is fictive; this makes flag precision and diff rendering first-class engineering targets rather than UI polish.

Source video ↗

Operationalizing '80-90% completion': field accuracy, artifact completeness, or rework rate

Research question

Does a single 'percent complete' figure predict realized time savings, or does rework rate (fraction of agent-produced fields the expert rewrites) dominate the outcome regardless of headline completion numbers?

Why

Without a defined error taxonomy the productivity claim is unfalsifiable and cannot be used to decide where to invest engineering effort. A rework-centric metric would redirect effort from raising raw accuracy to improving which fields get flagged, and would let teams compare platforms on a common basis instead of on demo narratives.

Source video ↗

Specification explicitness as the predictor of achievable agent automation rate

Research question

Across domains, does automation rate scale with the availability of a machine-checkable specification (written rules, schema, testable compliance condition), and what is the automation ceiling in document-grounded work that lacks such a verifier?

Why

The structured-credit result may be an artifact of indentures being explicit and mechanically checkable, which supplies a free oracle for both task definition and self-verification. If so, domain-selection for agent pilots becomes a measurable property of the corpus rather than a judgment call, and teams stop burning quarters on domains where no verifier exists.

Article ideas

3

Your Agent's Hard Problem Is Reconciliation, Not Reasoning

In decision-grade enterprise work the reasoning layer is commoditized and the reconciliation-and-provenance layer is the entire product; teams that tune prompts and agent loops while leaving source precedence unspecified ship confidently wrong numbers at machine speed.

Angle

Reframe the engineering budget: show that when a value is drawn from many sources only one is authoritative, so the deliverable is an explicit per-field conflict policy plus rendered lineage — and a chat wrapper over a warehouse is structurally disqualified from decision-grade use.

Source video ↗

The 80% Agent Is the Product

Optimizing for full autonomy is a misallocation; the economically dominant design target is time-to-90%-draft plus the precision of the uncertainty surface, because the residual 10-20% is where all remaining human cost lives and a bad flag forces a full re-derivation.

Angle

Argue against autonomy roadmaps by treating the review interface — diffs, exception queues, provenance display, flag precision — as a first-order systems concern with measurable effects on signoff time and missed-error rate.

Source video ↗

Automate Where the Rules Are Already Written Down

High automation rates are a property of the corpus, not the model: domains whose governing rules are explicit and mechanically checkable hand the agent both its task definition and its verifier, so specification explicitness should be the primary screening criterion for agent pilots.

Angle

Convert a sector anecdote into a selection heuristic — score candidate domains on rule explicitness and verifier availability before committing engineering effort, and predict which ones will stall without one.

Project ideas

3

Precedence Engine

gatehouse

A deterministic per-field source-precedence resolver with rendered lineage reduces expert rework rate on a multi-source artifact by a measurable margin versus a retrieval-only baseline that lets the model choose among conflicting values.

Proof of concept

Ingest four realistic source tiers (internal system of record A, internal system of record B, external vendor feed, document-extracted values) for ~50 fields; the agent emits value, chosen source, conflict set, and confidence; the review surface shows only conflicted or low-confidence fields with the rejected alternatives and their provenance.

Measurement

Field-level accuracy against a hand-verified ground truth; fraction of emitted fields rewritten by an expert; misses on a seeded conflict set (recall of deliberately injected wrong values); time-to-signoff on the full artifact.

Source video ↗

Exception-Only Review Bench

movement-lab

An exception-only review queue with calibrated confidence flags cuts time-to-signoff on a 200+ item artifact by more than half versus a full provenance-annotated table diff, without increasing missed-error rate.

Proof of concept

Generate a 200-name artifact from a rules-based document set with a known ground truth; render three review surfaces (full diff table, exception-only queue, confidence triage) and run the same reviewers across each; log signoff time, edits, and errors escaped.

Measurement

Time-to-signoff per surface; edits per artifact; missed-error rate measured against the seeded ground truth; reviewer-reported trust and re-derivation incidents.

Source video ↗

Spec-Explicitness Automation Curve

beyond-evals

Achievable agent automation rate is predicted by the availability of a machine-checkable specification in the target corpus, not by model capability, so automation rate should decline monotonically as verifier availability falls across domains at fixed model and effort budget.

Proof of concept

Hold model and tooling constant; run the same document-grounded agent on four tasks spanning a fully checkable rule set, a partially checkable set, a schema-constrained extraction task, and a judgment-heavy narrative task; grade each against its available oracle and record where self-verification is impossible.

Measurement

Automation rate per task; gap between agent self-reported confidence and graded correctness per task; fraction of outputs the agent can mechanically self-verify; slope of automation rate versus a verifier-availability score.

Architectural implications

3

The agent's value concentrates in reconciling fragmented private datasets across loan pools, legal documents, and bank statements before any decision is produced.

Before

Agent sits directly on top of a model plus ad-hoc retrieval; data quality is assumed or handled downstream by humans.

After

A dedicated ingestion/validation/provenance layer sits between sources and the reasoning layer, with per-field precedence rules.

Consequence

Most engineering effort shifts to connectors, entity resolution, and conflict policy; the LLM becomes a replaceable component and model swaps become low-risk.

Source video ↗

Human-in-the-loop is scoped to the final 10-20% review rather than spread across the workflow.

Before

Humans check intermediate steps and re-derive results, or humans are excluded entirely and outputs are unverified.

After

Agent produces a complete near-final artifact plus the delta/uncertainty surface; human reviews only flagged items.

Consequence

The review interface (diffing, provenance display, flag precision) becomes a core product surface and a primary lever on realized time savings.

Source video ↗

Generic chat interfaces are rejected for mission-critical financial decisions in favor of domain platforms that own the data layer.

Before

Off-the-shelf LLM chat as the delivery surface for enterprise analysis.

After

Domain-specific platform with embedded verified data, reproducible report generation, and visualization.

Consequence

Vendors who do not own or integrate the source-of-truth layer are structurally locked out of decision-grade use cases.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

3

Model knowledge vs. verified internal data

Benefit

General LLMs provide broad research capability and fast exploration without bespoke data engineering.

Cost or risk

They lack proprietary context and can assert generic or stale facts where only one internal source is correct, producing confidently wrong decision inputs.

Source video ↗

80/20 automation boundary

Benefit

Captures most of the time savings (days to minutes) while avoiding the cost of chasing full autonomy.

Cost or risk

The residual 10-20% requires expert attention; if the agent's uncertainty flags are imprecise, reviewers must redo the whole artifact, erasing the gain.

Boots-on-the-ground customization vs. platform reuse

Benefit

Deep workflow immersion with domain experts yields accuracy in complex, regulated processes.

Cost or risk

Per-client workflow modeling is expensive and does not generalize; scaling requires templating the immersion output.
