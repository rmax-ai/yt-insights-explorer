Overview

  • Speaker: Doneyli De Jesus & Annabell Schäfer
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Evaluating and tracing AI agents in regulated industries using Langfuse
  • Purpose: To educate AI engineers on how to systematically trace, monitor, benchmark, and evaluate AI agents in production to ensure compliance, safety, and reliability. Annabell Schäfer and Doneyli De Jesus from Langfuse present a workshop on evaluating and tracing AI agents, specifically focusing on high-stakes, regulated industries like banking and customer support. They walk through the AI Engineering loop—Trace, Monitor, Build Datasets, Experiment, and Evaluate—using Langfuse to tackle the non-deterministic nature of LLMs, mask PII, and build automated CI/CD gating for AI prompts.

Key Points

Agents can fail while system metrics look green

  • Explanation: HTTP status codes and latency metrics do not reflect whether the agent successfully accomplished the task or lied to the user.
  • Evidence: An agent claims a refund was issued without ever invoking the refund tool.
  • Practical implication: Engineers must implement trace-level observability and semantic evaluation.

The importance of PII masking in regulated domains

  • Explanation: Regulated industries cannot store sensitive customer data in observability logs without violating compliance rules.
  • Evidence: Automatic masking of credit card numbers in Langfuse traces.
  • Practical implication: Implement automated redaction pipelines before data hits logging or telemetry tools.

Blending LLM-as-a-judge and deterministic evaluators

  • Explanation: Relying 100% on LLM judges introduces variance; combining them with hard-coded checks ensures robustness.
  • Evidence: Using regular expression matching alongside LLM-based evaluation metrics.
  • Practical implication: Create a hybrid scoring framework for comprehensive quality assessment.

Trace-level outcome verification for fabricated agent side effects

Research question

How can an agent observability layer verify that a claimed side effect (e.g. refund issued) actually corresponds to a tool invocation and system-of-record event, independently of HTTP status and latency health?

Why

Infrastructure dashboards can stay green while the agent tells the user an action succeeded that never happened, so teams need a semantic outcome check at the trace layer before automated agents create liability.

Calibrating hybrid evaluators and CI gate thresholds against judge variance

Research question

What procedure yields stable pass/fail gates when scores come from a mix of deterministic checks and LLM-as-a-judge scoring, and how should thresholds be set from repeated-run score distributions rather than point estimates?

Why

Prompt promotion depends on evaluation gates, and noisy judge scores make those gates flaky, turning evaluator variance into a release-engineering problem that must be measured before thresholds are enforced.

Ingestion-boundary PII redaction and residual leakage in trace stores

Research question

When PII masking is applied as telemetry enters the observability platform, what leakage remains in nested span attributes, tool payloads, and intermediate steps, and how can that residual exposure be audited continuously?

Why

If sensitive data is persisted before redaction, the trace store itself becomes a compliance incident; masking must be designed as part of the instrumentation pipeline and then tested against real trace shapes.

Failure-mined datasets and representative expert annotation under traffic drift

Research question

How should production traces be filtered by unsuccessful outcomes and stratified for expert annotation so that benchmark datasets remain representative as production traffic and failure modes drift?

Why

Datasets built from observed failures are more predictive than hand-invented examples, but only if sampling and refresh policies preserve coverage of the failure taxonomy over time.

Side-Effect Auditor

gatehouse

A trace-level verifier that compares agent natural-language claims against actual tool invocations and system-of-record events will detect a measurable share of fabricated completion claims that pass green HTTP-status and latency monitors.

Proof of concept

Build a refund-flow demo agent with a mock refund API, inject cases where the agent claims success without calling the tool, and attach a span-level auditor that checks claim text against tool-call records and downstream events.

Measurement

Detection rate on injected fabricated claims, false-positive rate on genuine successful refunds, and delta versus status/latency-only monitoring.

Judge Variance Gate Calibrator

beyond-evals

Setting CI score thresholds from repeated-run confidence intervals of a hybrid deterministic-plus-judge evaluator will reduce flaky gate outcomes versus fixed point thresholds at comparable false-pass rates.

Proof of concept

Run the same benchmark dataset through repeated judge and deterministic evaluations, compute score distributions per metric, and compare a point-threshold gate with a confidence-aware gate on simulated prompt changes.

Measurement

Gate flake rate, false-pass rate, false-block rate, and run-to-run score variance across repeated evaluations.

Source video ↗

Ingestion Redaction Leak Auditor

new

Top-level regex masking alone will leave PII in nested span attributes and tool payloads at a measurable rate, while schema-aware ingestion-time redaction will reduce residual leakage without eliminating the trace structure needed for evaluation.

Proof of concept

Instrument a sample tracing pipeline with nested LLM and tool spans, inject synthetic card numbers and identifiers into different attribute depths, and compare regex-only masking against schema-aware masking at ingestion.

Measurement

Residual PII leakage rate by span depth, false-negative masking rate, and retained trace completeness for debugging and evaluation.

Failure-Mined Dataset Builder

beyond-evals

A benchmark dataset stratified from unresolved production traces and refreshed against drifting failure-mode proportions will predict prompt regressions better than a same-size synthetic dataset.

Proof of concept

Filter traces marked unresolved, group them into failure modes, sample a stratified annotation queue for experts, and build versioned datasets that are re-sampled when production stratum weights shift.

Measurement

Correlation between experiment score changes and post-release production regression rates, plus failure-mode coverage and annotation agreement over time.

Architectural implications

4

Sensitive-data handling belongs in the tracing/instrumentation layer as an ingestion-time transform, not in application business logic or post-hoc log scrubbing.

Before

PII redaction is treated as an application or prompt-level concern, with traces stored raw for debugging convenience.

After

The observability pipeline owns a redaction stage that masks entities before persistence, so the trace store only ever holds tokenized placeholders.

Consequence

Trace stores become admissible in regulated environments, at the cost of losing raw values needed for some debugging and replay.

Source video ↗

Prompt changes become deployable events that trigger external CI workflows acting on the evaluation platform's datasets and metrics.

Before

Prompt edits are applied manually to a running system and validated by eyeballing outputs.

After

Prompt registry → CI trigger → experiment run against dataset → threshold gate → promotion, with the gate as the only promotion path.

Consequence

Prompt engineering inherits release-engineering overhead (dataset maintenance, threshold calibration, flaky-gate triage) in exchange for regression prevention.

Source video ↗

Traces must be rich enough to isolate which step failed — individual tool invocations and guardrail triggers — for error analysis and dataset construction to be possible.

Before

Logging captures only final input/output pairs of the agent turn.

After

Span-level structure exposes each tool call, guardrail trigger and intermediate step as a queryable unit.

Consequence

Feature work shifts toward trace schema design; poor instrumentation silently caps evaluation quality.

Source video ↗

Evaluators and datasets are a separate subsystem consuming traces, decoupled from the runtime that produces them.

Before

Quality checks are embedded in the agent runtime or done by manual spot checks.

After

Eval harness reads from the trace store, runs deterministic + LLM-judge scorers, and writes scores that gates and dashboards consume.

Consequence

Enables offline re-scoring as evaluators improve, but introduces versioning coupling between evaluator definitions and historical scores.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

LLM-as-a-judge vs deterministic evaluators

Benefit

Deterministic code checks are stable and cheap; LLM judges capture semantic quality that regex cannot express.

Cost or risk

Judge-only scoring introduces variance that makes automated gates flaky, while code-only scoring cannot assess semantic correctness.

Source video ↗

Trace fidelity vs compliance redaction

Benefit

Persisting full request/response data maximizes debuggability and replayability.

Cost or risk

Storing sensitive customer data in observability logs creates legal and financial exposure in regulated industries.

Source video ↗

Green infrastructure metrics as a false assurance signal

Benefit

Status codes and latency are cheap, universal, and already instrumented.

Cost or risk

They remain green while the agent fails its task or fabricates side effects, so teams can ship broken agents with fully passing monitors.

Source video ↗

Automated gating vs iteration speed

Benefit

Threshold gates block regressions before production and make quality a precondition for release.

Cost or risk

Every prompt change now requires a dataset run and multi-metric threshold pass before shipping, adding latency and requiring maintained thresholds.

Source video ↗

Human expert annotation vs coverage

Benefit

Domain experts supply ground truth that automated judges cannot, in the vocabulary of the business.

Cost or risk

Expert time is scarce, so only a sampled subset of traces can be labeled, leaving the rest unevaluated for subtle failures.
