If accurate, the dominant lever on capability growth is no longer just more GPUs or more headcount but the tightness of the AI-assisted research loop itself. Generalization

Any engineering org can instrument its own 'research velocity' metric and attribute the delta to AI-assisted vs human-only work; the measurement, not the anecdote, is the transferable asset.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltyweak evidence

The described 'AI-native SDLC' is a closed-loop self-improving generation pipeline: generation model → generation asset → Gecko Autorater → fail/score critique → rewrite prompt → optimizer agent, applied at every SDLC stage with full code context.

This is a concrete harness pattern: an independent rater produces structured critique that is fed back as prompt mutation, with an agent optimizing the loop. The architectural boundary that matters is separating generation from scoring, so the rater can be swapped/versioned independently of the producer.

Generalization

Any agentic system that must improve without fine-tuning can adopt producer/critic/rewriter with an explicit scoring contract; the rater is the real product surface and should be versioned, evaluated, and monitored like a model.

Rater fidelity as the ceiling of closed-loop generation

Research question

When a generate → score → critique → rewrite → optimizer loop runs without human review, at what point does it converge on artifacts the autorater rewards rather than artifacts the task requires, and which probe designs detect that drift earliest?

Why

Project ideas

4

Velocity Attribution Harness

movement-lab

On matched task sets, logging AI-assisted versus human-only work at the task level produces a velocity multiplier with a confidence interval reproducible across teams, and the AI-assisted share of the delta is stable within ±15% across two independent task batches.

Proof of concept

Instrument a research or engineering backlog with per-task metadata (assisted flag, model, time-to-accept, rework count) and run matched A/B pairs of comparable tasks over 4-6 weeks, then compute per-batch velocity deltas and attribution shares.

Measurement

Velocity multiplier with confidence interval per batch; between-batch variance of the AI-assisted attribution share; correlation between logged assistance and reviewer-judged output quality.

Source video ↗

Rater Drift and Hack Detector

beyond-evals

Closed generate→score→rewrite loops measurably degrade on human-graded holdouts within N iterations even as autorater scores rise monotonically, and a divergence signal between autorater score and holdout human score appears before the drift is visible in output review.

Proof of concept

Run the producer/critic/rewriter loop on a fixed task suite, tracking autorater score and a periodically sampled human-graded holdout; add adversarial probe tasks whose correct answers are known to be low-scoring under the current rater.

Measurement

Autorater-score vs human-holdout-score divergence curve over iterations; iteration index at which divergence exceeds a set threshold; probe-task pass rate decay.

Source video ↗

Rater Gate

gatehouse

Treating the autorater as a versioned, gated dependency (with its own regression suite and canary thresholds in CI) prevents quality regressions that pass silently when the rater is swapped or updated without gates.

Proof of concept

Wrap the autorater behind a versioned service contract, attach a golden-set regression suite and a drift threshold, and wire a CI gate that blocks pipeline promotion when rater version changes exceed the threshold.

Measurement

Number of rater-version-induced quality regressions caught pre-merge versus post-merge; false-block rate of the gate; mean time to detect rater drift.

Source video ↗

Siting Risk Leading-Indicator Model

new

A small set of observable local indicators predicts data center permitting outcomes well enough to rank candidate sites, outperforming a baseline that assumes planned buildout proceeds as announced.

Proof of concept

Assemble a jurisdiction-level table of permitting outcomes, opposition signals, and grid interconnect queue positions for a sample of announced projects; fit a simple classifier and backtest on the most recent cohort.

Measurement

Out-of-sample AUC against the proceed-as-announced baseline; lead time between indicator movement and public permit decision; calibration of predicted versus realized restriction rates.

Architectural implications

3

Autorater-driven prompt rewriting creates a producer/critic boundary inside the development pipeline.

Before

Prompt and code generation treated as a single model call judged ad hoc by humans.

After

An explicit scoring service (autorater) sits between generation and acceptance, emitting structured fail/score signals consumed by a rewriter and an optimizer agent.

Consequence

The evaluator becomes a versioned, monitored production component; its drift or bias silently caps system quality and must be gated and regression-tested independently.

Source video ↗

Compute placement decisions are increasingly governed by non-technical constraints (local politics, power, water) rather than pure latency/cost.

Before

Capacity planning modeled as silicon availability plus capex.

After

Capacity planning includes jurisdiction-level risk, permitting timelines, and community opposition as explicit constraints.

Consequence

Deployment architectures may need multi-region/multi-jurisdiction portability and workload placement policies that can react to regulatory changes.

Source video ↗

Terrestrial power and regulatory limits are framed as eventually forcing compute into orbit under sun-synchronous orbits.

Before

Data centers assumed to be terrestrial, grid-connected, and latency-bound to population centers.

After

Long-horizon architectures consider space-based compute with different power, cooling, and connectivity assumptions.

Consequence

Training vs inference placement may diverge; bandwidth and radiation tolerance become design variables rather than exotic concerns.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Closed-loop autorater optimization

Benefit

Enables iterative self-improvement without human review of each artifact, targeting large engineering velocity multipliers.

Cost or risk

Quality is bounded by the autorater's scoring fidelity; an explicit fail/score + rewrite loop can converge on what the rater rewards rather than what the task requires.

Source video ↗

Political scarcity of compute

Benefit

Incumbents with existing sites and power agreements gain a durable competitive moat.

Cost or risk

New entrants face restricted buildout, reducing competition and raising costs for anyone without pre-existing allocations.

Source video ↗

Scaling via software vs scaling via hardware

Benefit

Recursive software improvement can raise capability without new megawatts.

Cost or risk

Over-indexing on projected RSI gains risks under-investing in physical infrastructure if the acceleration does not persist.

Source video ↗

Terrestrial vs orbital compute

Benefit

Orbital placement escapes land, water, and permitting constraints and taps continuous solar.

Cost or risk

Requires launch economics, radiation-hardened hardware, and downlink bandwidth that are not established; the summary offers no cost comparison.
