  • Purpose: To introduce the concept of self-driving production and how AI SRE platforms solve enterprise causality challenges. Eric Schwartz, Founding Product Manager at Traversal, discusses how AI coding agents accelerate software development but inadvertently increase production complexity and troubleshooting burden. He outlines the limitations of current observability tools (which show correlation, not causation) and introduces Traversal's 5 levels of self-driving production, powered by a Production World Model and Causal Search Engine.

The 5 Levels of Self-Driving Production

Core insights

6
[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Externalizing causal structure into an explicit 'Production World Model' — a maintained map of entities and relationships built from MELT data plus source code, deploys, runbooks, historical incidents, and Jira — is proposed as the mechanism that lets an AI system reason about root cause, rather than asking an LLM to infer causation from raw telemetry inside its context window.

Why it matters

It reframes the core artifact of an AI SRE system from 'an agent with log-query tools' to 'a queryable model of the environment that agents interrogate.' That places the hard problem in model construction and freshness, not prompt design, and gives a concrete address for the failure mode of LLMs confabulating causal chains from correlated signals.

Generalization

For any agent operating over a large, relational, fast-changing system, the durable design move is to materialize a world/topology model as a separate subsystem and let reasoning operate over that structure instead of raw events. The model becomes the stable interface; the agent becomes replaceable.

[O]Mental Modelhigh noveltymoderate evidence

The five-level model's real discontinuity is scope of agency, not model capability: Levels 2-3 are summarization and single-domain investigation agents, Level 4 is autonomous cross-environment investigation at scale, and Level 5 adds remediation and prevention. The hard boundary is crossing domain/service boundaries with sufficient context.

Why it matters

It tells engineers where the wall actually is. Teams routinely ship a competent single-domain agent and assume more capable models will get them to autonomy; the model says the gate is context coverage and cross-boundary traversal, which is an infrastructure/data problem, not a reasoning-quality problem.

Generalization

Agent maturity ladders are usually bounded by context and permission scope rather than by the underlying model. Progressively widening the blast radius an agent may reason about — then act within — is the axis worth measuring.

[P]Practicehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Traversal states five explicit engineering criteria for AI-adjacent incident response at enterprise scale: ingest all production data without gaps, reason at scale without blowing up costs, map multi-system relationships in real-time, improve autonomously without forward-deployed engineers, and find root causes in minutes. These are stated as a vendor-evaluation checklist but read as a system design spec.

Why it matters

Each criterion is a hard architectural constraint that rules out a common shortcut. Notably 'without forward-deployed engineers' rejects the dominant deployment pattern for enterprise AI (embedded humans tuning the system per customer) and demands a self-improving loop. 'Without blowing up costs' rules out brute-force LLM scanning of the corpus.

Generalization

A useful rubric for auditing any production AI system: data completeness, cost-bounded reasoning, live relationship freshness, autonomy from human tuning, and latency. The forward-deployed-engineer criterion is the sharpest and most frequently ignored.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

AI-accelerated development is presented as a negative externality on operations: faster code generation produces more code volume and less human context per line, so troubleshooting toil and production complexity increase even as build time falls.

Why it matters

It argues against evaluating coding agents on development velocity alone. If the same organizations absorb the output into production, the downstream investigation cost may partially or fully offset the gain — a measurable, non-obvious ROI calculation for agent adoption.

Generalization

Automating one stage of a pipeline relocates the bottleneck to the next stage that has not been automated, and can degrade quality signals at that stage (here: reduced human context). Any agent deployment should be evaluated on the whole lifecycle, not the accelerated stage.

Source video ↗
[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

The stated root-cause task is a graph traversal, not a similarity search: a single checkout API failure can require 5-10 hops across dozens of services and petabytes of data before reaching an expired TLS certificate. This motivates a dedicated 'Causal Search Engine' as the query layer over the world model rather than RAG-style retrieval.

Why it matters

It gives a concrete cognitive-science-free justification for multi-hop structured traversal (with pruning/bounds) as the retrieval primitive, and predicts that plain vector retrieval over logs will retrieve the noisy symptom rather than the distant cause. It also defines the evaluation target: hop depth and accuracy at depth.

Generalization

Where causes are structurally distant from symptoms, retrieval design must be graph-shaped and depth-aware. Benchmarks should measure accuracy as a function of hop distance, not just top-k relevance.

Source video ↗
[E]Empirical Resultlow noveltyweak evidence

Vendor-reported deployments show large operational deltas: >80% root-cause accuracy in under five minutes, American Express MTTR from 63 to 25 minutes across 1,000+ applications, and PepsiCo collapsing 700+ backlogged alerts per engineer weekly into prioritized actionable alerts.

Why it matters

These are the numbers to try to reproduce or falsify. In particular, 80% accuracy is a critical threshold question: it is plausible for assisted RCA with a human verifier but insufficient to justify the autonomous remediation claimed at Level 5 without a separate verification harness.

Generalization

Accuracy figures for diagnostic systems should always be read jointly with the autonomy level they are used to justify; the same metric is adequate or inadequate depending on whether a human or the system acts on it.

Action Gate Harness for Autonomous Remediation

gatehouse

Requiring structured verification probes, canary checks, and rollback criteria before executing an RCA-suggested remediation reduces harmful false-positive actions substantially without increasing MTTR beyond an acceptable threshold.

Proof of concept

Build a simulated incident environment with known root causes, generate RCA hypotheses, and route proposed actions through configurable gates: confidence threshold, dependency-scoped canary, health-probe validation, and automatic rollback. Compare ungated, human-approved, and gated autonomous remediation.

Measurement

False-remediation rate, harmful-action cost, MTTR, human-confirmation latency, and rollback frequency across incident classes.

Architectural implications

4

The primary substrate shifts from telemetry stores emitting alerts to a relationship model that telemetry is indexed against.

Before

Dashboards and static alert rules sit directly on logs/metrics/traces; the human (or LLM) reconstructs service relationships at incident time.

After

A Production World Model holds entity and dependency relationships continuously, with MELT, code, deploys, runbooks and past incidents as inputs; alerts and investigations are queries against that model.

Consequence

Work moves from incident-time reconstruction to continuous model maintenance. Model freshness and completeness become first-class SLOs, and a stale model silently degrades every downstream agent.

Source video ↗

Reasoning responsibilities split between a retrieval/traversal layer and a language layer.

Before

An LLM agent is given log-query tools and asked to figure out the cause, so context window size and tool-selection quality bound achievable investigation depth.

After

A causal search engine performs bounded multi-hop traversal over the world model and returns a candidate causal path; the LLM orchestrates, explains, and acts on structured output.

Consequence

Investigation depth is no longer limited by context length, cost is bounded by traversal budget rather than tokens, and evaluation can target the deterministic search layer separately from the generative narrative layer.

Source video ↗

The autonomy ladder is gated on permissions and closed-loop action, not just investigation quality.

Before

Automation ends at alerting and runbooks that a human executes; systems are considered mature when alerts are accurate and dashboards are complete.

After

Level 4 only concedes cross-environment investigation; Level 5 requires the system to remediate and prevent, meaning write access to production changes.

Consequence

A separate safety apparatus (verification of proposed actions, rollback, scoping of blast radius) becomes mandatory and is architecturally distinct from the RCA pipeline — the RCA pipeline does not by itself justify the action layer.

Source video ↗

Self-tuning replaces forward-deployed engineering as the scaling mechanism.

Before

Each enterprise deployment is accompanied by embedded engineers who hand-tune rules, mappings, and prompts per environment.

After

The system is expected to improve autonomously from its own incident history, with no per-customer human in the tuning loop.

Consequence

Feedback capture from resolved incidents becomes core infrastructure, and evaluation must cover learning-over-time behavior, not just point-in-time accuracy, since drift or self-reinforcing error is now unmitigated by a human tuner.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

Full-fidelity ingestion vs. cost-bounded reasoning

Benefit

Ingesting all production data 'without gaps' maximizes the chance that the true cause — often several hops away, e.g. an expired TLS certificate — is present in the corpus at all.

Cost or risk

Petabyte-scale ingestion plus multi-hop search is expensive; the stated requirement to 'reason at scale without blowing up costs' forces precomputation, indexing, or pruning that can reintroduce the blind spots the completeness requirement was meant to remove.

Source video ↗

Continuous relationship mapping vs. model freshness

Benefit

A real-time map of multi-system relationships is what enables crossing dozens of services during investigation.

Cost or risk

Any world model decays as services are deployed, renamed, or decommissioned; a stale edge produces a confidently wrong causal path, and the failure is silent — the system answers plausibly with an outdated topology.

Source video ↗

Alert reduction vs. suppressed signal

Benefit

Collapsing a 700+ per-engineer weekly alert backlog into prioritized actionable alerts removes the noise that makes on-call unsustainable.

Cost or risk

Ranking and filtering imply suppression; the value is measured in hours saved, while the risk lives in the rare deprioritized alert that was the real incident. The evidence reported is on hours saved, not on missed detections.

Source video ↗

Autonomous remediation vs. diagnostic accuracy

Benefit

Level 5 remediation and prevention removes the human from the loop entirely, which is the only way to keep pace with failure volume generated by faster code production.

Cost or risk

The remediation step would rest on a root-cause capability reported at '>80% accuracy in under five minutes' — implying roughly one in five investigations is wrong, which an autonomous actor would execute on. No verification or approval mechanism for the action is described.

Source video ↗

LLM-only investigation vs. structured causal model

Benefit

Adding a world model and search engine addresses the observed failure that models cannot separate causation from correlation.

Cost or risk

It adds a heavyweight subsystem that must itself be built, kept current, and evaluated; the simpler approach (LLM over observability tools) is far cheaper to stand up and is adequate for shallow, single-domain problems.
