Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Tamar Yehoshua
  • Channel: Lenny's Podcast
  • Main topic: How AI tools are expanding the role of Product Managers (PMs) rather than eliminating it, with practical examples from Atlassian.
  • Purpose: To explain how PM roles are evolving in the era of AI builders, share concrete examples of Atlassian PMs leveraging AI tools, and provide a framework for measuring AI fluency and team acceleration. Tamar Yehoshua, Chief Product and AI Officer at Atlassian, shares insights from the Lenny & Friends Summit on September 10, 2026. Addressing fears that AI would eliminate PMs, engineers, or designers, she argues that roles aren't converging or disappearing; instead, they are expanding. PMs now have a front-row seat to AI acceleration, doing more work across prototyping, writing evals, going deeper on customer feedback, codifying best practices, and consuming information at velocity using Atlassian's Teamwork Graph and Rovo.

Topic Map

The AI Builder Era and Role Expansion

  • Explanation: Discusses the shift from fear of job loss to the reality of role overlap and expansion, where founders and PMs wear multiple hats with AI tools.
  • Key claims:
    • Instead of roles going away, jobs are overlapping in the AI builder era.
    • Founders have always been builders wearing many hats; now PMs and teams can do the same.
    • The job of a PM remains fundamentally the same—finding product-market fit, building products people love, and building viable businesses—while HOW it is done has changed.
  • Examples:
    • Startups hiring builders rather than siloed PMs or designers.
    • Atlassian PMs checking in code, writing evals, and fixing design bugs.
  • Terminology:
    • AI builder
    • role expansion
    • product-market fit
  • Why it matters: Understanding role expansion prevents unnecessary fear and empowers PMs to leverage AI tools to accelerate delivery and customer value.

Three Concrete Atlassian Examples

  • Explanation: Detailed case studies from Atlassian showing how PMs expanded their roles using AI tools across Confluence, RovoClaw, and Jira.
  • Key claims:
    • Confluence team: A PM with zero prior coding experience checked in 26 PRs in a single month by partnering with engineers on harnesses, resulting in 2x throughput in evals.
    • RovoClaw team: PM and designer vibecoded an alpha from scratch, proving the value before bringing in engineers, then shifting from rowing to steering to unblock the team.
    • Jira team: PMs and engineers built AI features in Jira to reinvent the SDLC, resulting in 3x throughput and 22 user-facing features shipped in 10 weeks.
  • Examples:
    • Remix with Rovo and Confluence slides launching in 6 weeks instead of 6 months.
    • Using Figma MCP and coding agents to fix 14 design bugs in an hour.
  • Terminology:
    • vibecoding
    • eval loops
    • Figma MCP
    • coding agent
  • Why it matters: Demonstrates practical, real-world applications of AI tools in enterprise software development and product management.

The AI Fluency Index Framework

  • Explanation: Atlassian's internal framework to train and assess PMs on AI capabilities to help them become AI-native.
  • Key claims:
    • The AI Fluency Index assesses 6 capabilities from 1 (curious) to 5 (pioneering).
    • Capabilities include tool usage, writing evals, automating data insights, prototyping, and technical literacy.
    • AI Builders Weeks (held once a quarter) train PMs and designers with external speakers, internal leaders, and hands-on projects.
  • Examples:
    • Claire speaking at Atlassian's Builders Week.
    • Over 1,000 participants building 120 new workflows during Builders Week.
  • Terminology:
    • AI Fluency Index
    • AI Builders Week
    • technical literacy
    • curious vs pioneering
  • Why it matters: Provides organizations with a structured way to upskill PMs and measure their progress toward AI fluency.

Key Points

Roles are expanding, not converging

  • Explanation: While people initially worried AI would eliminate PMs or engineers, everyone can now do much more across domains.
  • Evidence: PMs checking in code, writing evals, fixing design bugs, and prototyping.
  • Practical implication: PMs must embrace adjacent skills like prototyping and eval writing to remain competitive.

From rowing to steering

  • Explanation: PMs initially roll up their sleeves to build and code (rowing), then transition to steering (unblocking, prioritizing, setting direction) as teams scale.
  • Evidence: RovoClaw project where PM started coding the alpha and later shifted to unblocking the team.
  • Practical implication: Knowing when to shift from hands-on building to strategic steering optimizes team velocity.

Context is everything

  • Explanation: AI tools require enterprise context and teamwork graphs to be effective and safe.
  • Evidence: Atlassian's Teamwork Graph provides full context across Jira, Confluence, and Slack.
  • Practical implication: Organizations must integrate tools and data systems so AI agents have proper context.
