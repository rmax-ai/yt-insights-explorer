Overview

  • Speaker: Ksenia Se
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: AI Engineer New York conference opening, sponsor acknowledgments, and agenda overview for the final day.
  • Purpose: Open day three of the AI Engineer conference, acknowledge sponsors, and preview the conference tracks and sessions. Ksenia Se, founder, writer, and analyst at Turing Post, opens the final day of AI Engineer New York, sponsored by Arize. She welcomes attendees, thanks gold, silver, platinum sponsors and supporting partners, and outlines the day's agenda across different rooms focusing on developer tools, agents, model costs, human verification, and agentic software factories.

Andrey Rybka from Bloomberg presents an analysis of real-world security incidents involving autonomous AI agents and outlines a comprehensive defense-in-depth framework featuring layers like identity and trust, prompt defense, tool governance, execution isolation, data protection, and observability.

An exploration of compute trading, market structure, dark pools, macro economics of GPU hours, Jevons paradox, and financing challenges in AI infrastructure.

Carmen Li, CEO of Compute Exchange, presents a keynote on how compute is becoming a commodity and how their marketplace operates. She details three ways to buy compute (reserved GPU rental, token & forward contracts, and an open hardware market), explains the mechanics of RFQ processing, vendor matching, and normalized quoting, and contrasts market-implied forward curves with accelerated depreciation schedules for GPU infrastructure.

Vinoo Ganesh, CEO and co-founder of Kepler, discusses why Wall Street financial institutions struggle to trust AI models due to hallucinations, incorrect data retrieval, and lack of deterministic computation. He outlines how Kepler addresses these challenges through deterministic storage, pre-processed pointers, and strict verification layers, emphasizing that better infrastructure rather than just smarter models will bridge the trust gap.

Joao Wenzel from Citadel Securities discusses how companies lack a robust world model of themselves. While engineering teams build agents to write code, leadership needs agents that help define and refine company strategy by synthesizing messy historical data, meetings, and communications into a state estimator.

Mitchell Troyanovsky, co-founder of Basis, discusses how to build reliable, production-ready AI agents for long-horizon work. He explores why agent productivity hasn't scaled like developer productivity, arguing that current agent products outsource complexity to the user and lack organizational process. He introduces frameworks like behavior specs, forking, and simulations to supervise agent processes and close the loop.

Ksenia Se and Swyx deliver closing remarks for AI Engineer New York 2026, thanking participants, speakers, sponsors, and community members, while highlighting upcoming events like Code, AI Engineer Europe 2027 in London, and the flagship San Francisco event.

Layered agent security: does defense-in-depth reduce blast radius or just relocate it?

Research question

Across real autonomous-agent security incidents, which defense layers (identity/trust, prompt defense, tool governance, execution isolation, data protection, observability) actually reduce incident severity, and which only shift failures to an adjacent layer?

Why

Agent security is being sold as a stack of independent controls; if the layers are correlated or mutually bypassable, teams will over-invest in prompt defense and under-invest in execution isolation and workload identity where severity is actually bounded.
