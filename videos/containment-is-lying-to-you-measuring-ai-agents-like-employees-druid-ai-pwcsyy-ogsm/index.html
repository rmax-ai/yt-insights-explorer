Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Michael Yang and Dan Balaceanu
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Evaluating AI agent performance, ROI, and business impact using a reference framework and scoring model
  • Purpose: To provide developers and business leaders with a practical framework and maturity scale (Missing, Measured, Managed) for evaluating AI agents beyond simple containment metrics. The presenters discuss the common pitfall in evaluating AI agents solely by 'containment' percentage. Drawing an analogy to hiring interns, they introduce a comprehensive 6-pillar governance and measurement framework consisting of 26 components (covering agent mandate, platform capabilities, skills, tools, tasks, and outputs) to evaluate AI agent ROI, business impact, and performance like real employees.

Topic Map

The Problem with Containment

  • Explanation: Containment percentage (conversations handled without human intervention) is often the only metric used on dashboards, but it can be misleading when agents successfully answer routine questions but fail at high-value business intent like refinancing.
  • Key claims:
    • Containment is a vanity metric but some vendors price on it.
    • An agent with 96% containment can provide the wrong business offer compared to one with 80% containment.
  • Examples:
    • Banking customer service interns: Intern A (80% contained, flagged) vs Intern B (96% contained, on track) where Intern A correctly routes mortgage refinancing inquiries while Intern B mishandles them.
  • Terminology:
    • Containment
    • Escalation
    • Vanity metric
  • Why it matters: Relying solely on containment misleads business stakeholders about actual revenue impact and business value.

The Agent Evaluation Framework (6 Pillars)

  • Explanation: A structured reference model containing 26 components across six pillars, governed by metrics like error tolerance, risk, compliance, observability, drift, accuracy, and tone.
  • Key claims:
    • You can't claim a result you can't inspect.
    • Governance is the most heavily weighted cluster (39/100) because business outcomes depend on expected boundaries.
  • Examples:
    • Evaluating banking customer service agents across Agent Mandate, Platform Capabilities, Skills, Tools, Tasks, and Outputs.
  • Terminology:
    • Agent Mandate
    • Platform Capabilities
    • Skills
    • Tools
    • Tasks
    • Outputs
    • Governance
  • Why it matters: It replaces subjective debates about whether an agent is ready with objective measurements based on mathematical rules.

Maturity Scoring Scale: Missing, Measured, Managed

  • Explanation: A 0-1-2 maturity scale used to score each component of the framework, weighted and normalized to 100.
  • Key claims:
    • 0 = Missing (never reviewed), 1 = Measured (exists and checked, even by hand), 2 = Managed (tracked and versioned over time, continuously managed).
    • Not all measured metrics need to be automated; manual inspection is equally valid for defining risk.
  • Examples:
    • Scoring compliance against SOC 2 and back policies manually vs automatically.
  • Terminology:
    • Missing
    • Measured
    • Managed
    • Maturity scale
  • Why it matters: Provides a rigorous numerical score (e.g., Agent A scoring 83 vs Agent B scoring 45) to determine real agent readiness.

Key Points

Inspect the whole agent, not just outputs

  • Explanation: Evaluating only output success rates misses crucial governance, platform capabilities, and mandate alignment.
  • Evidence: Agent B had higher containment and more skills, but lacked explainability, drift monitoring, and compliance tracking, resulting in a lower overall score.
  • Practical implication: Establish clear boundaries and error tolerances during agent definition rather than just measuring runtime success.

The Interview Never Ends

  • Explanation: Unlike human interns who get an annual review, AI agents must be evaluated weekly against live traffic and human baselines.
  • Evidence: Agent performance drifts over time, requiring continuous instrumentation, evaluation, improvement, and re-testing.
  • Practical implication: Implement weekly automated re-runs against human baselines to catch performance drift early.

Druid AI Agent Evaluation Framework

  • How it works: Scores 26 components across 6 pillars (Governance, Agent Mandate, Platform Capabilities, Outputs, Tools, Skills) using a 0-2 maturity scale and weights normalized to 100.
  • Components:
    • Agent Mandate
    • Platform Capabilities
    • Skills
    • Tools
    • Tasks

Hiring two banking customer service interns where Intern B had 96% containment and Intern A had 80% containment.

  • Illustrates: High containment can mask missed business opportunities (like mortgage refinancing handoffs).
  • Lesson: Containment is not enough; measuring correct routing and business intent matters more.

  • Questions to investigate:

[!]Failure Modehigh noveltystrong evidence

Containment (share of conversations handled without human intervention) is a confounded metric: it can be maximized by smoothly mishandling high-value intents. An agent scoring 96% containment can produce a worse business outcome (wrong offer, missed refinancing handoff) than one scoring 80%.

Why it matters

If containment is the dashboard headline (and even a pricing basis), teams will optimize and vendors will sell against an objective that is anti-correlated with revenue-critical outcomes. Evaluation must key on correctness of routing/business intent, not on non-escalation.

Generalization

Any single scalar proxy metric that rewards sunk conversation within a boundary will be gamed at the expense of value-bearing outcomes; evaluation needs intent-conditional correctness, not a global containment rate.

Project ideas

4

Intent-Aware Containment Replacement (IACR)

beyond-evals

An intent-conditional correctness metric (correct routing/handoff rate for high-value intents) will rank agents differently than containment, and in a banking customer service simulation, an agent with high containment but low intent-correctness will be correctly identified as worse than a lower-containment agent with high intent-correctness.

Proof of concept

Build a simulated banking customer service environment with defined intents (e.g., refinancing, balance inquiry). Instrument two agents: one optimized for containment, one for correct routing. Measure both containment and intent-correctness on a test set of high-value intents.

Measurement

Rank correlation between containment and intent-correctness; delta in incorrect high-value outcomes.

Source video ↗

Human Baseline as a Service (HBaaS)

gatehouse

A sampled human baseline (expert-scored live traffic) can be maintained at acceptable cost and used to detect weekly agent drift, and baseline drift itself is measurable and manageable.

Proof of concept

For a given domain (e.g., banking customer service), sample live agent conversations weekly, have human experts score correctness and routing. Track baseline scores over 6 weeks to measure baseline stability and labeling cost.

Measurement

Cost per labeled conversation, baseline variance, and detection rate of agent drift against baseline.

Source video ↗

Drift Detection Cadence Optimizer

movement-lab

There exists a cost-optimal evaluation cadence (e.g., daily vs weekly vs monthly) that minimizes total cost (evaluation + missed drift damage) for a given domain, and weekly is not universally optimal.

Proof of concept

Instrument a deployed agent's performance on live traffic, compute a drift signal at daily granularity. Simulate evaluation at different cadences using historical data to estimate time-to-detection and cumulative drift damage. Optimize cadence.

Measurement

Time-to-detection at each cadence, total cost (evaluation cost + drift damage cost).

Source video ↗

Weight Sensitivity Analyzer for Agent Readiness Scores

new

Agent readiness rankings are sensitive to the weighting of the 26 components, and small changes in weights (e.g., reducing governance from 39 to 30) can flip the ranking of two agents, indicating that weights must be set explicitly according to business priorities.

Proof of concept

Implement the 26-component maturity scoring model. For a set of 10 agent configurations with component scores, run a Monte Carlo simulation varying weights within plausible ranges. Measure ranking stability (e.g., Kendall tau) and identify weight thresholds that flip rankings.

Measurement

Ranking stability metrics (e.g., probability of rank inversion), sensitivity indices for each weight.

Architectural implications

3

Evaluation evidence lives in both static configuration and runtime behavior.

Before

Evaluation reads only output logs to compute a success or containment rate.

After

Evaluation reads declarative artifacts (mandate, tools, skills, platform capabilities) plus governance signals (drift, compliance, explainability) alongside runtime outputs.

Consequence

The evaluation harness must couple to the agent's config/registry layer, not just its telemetry pipeline, making config a versioned and inspectable first-class input.

Source video ↗

Governance signals such as drift, compliance tracking, and explainability must exist to be scored.

Before

Governance is a launch-time review or policy document.

After

Governance metrics are continuously emitted, tracked, and versioned as part of agent runtime.

Consequence

Observability and drift detection become prerequisites for hitting the 'Managed' maturity state rather than optional add-ons.

Source video ↗

A 'Managed' state requires tracked and versioned metrics over time.

Before

Metrics are point-in-time snapshots used for a readiness decision.

After

Metrics are stored as time series with versions, enabling drift analysis against baselines.

Consequence

Requires persistent metric history and baseline comparison infrastructure, which also enables the weekly re-run cadence the framework prescribes.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

3

Optimizing for containment

Benefit

Simple, single-number dashboard metric that some vendors price on, and can be used as a quick operational signal.

Cost or risk

Rewards non-escalation over correct handling of high-value intent, so high-containment agents can silently mishandle revenue-critical requests like refinancing.

Source video ↗

Automating measurement versus manual review

Benefit

Manual inspection is valid for defining risk and avoids the upfront cost of building automated instrumentation.

Cost or risk

Manual review does not scale to weekly, continuous re-runs against live traffic and human baselines, capping progress at the 'Measured' state rather than 'Managed'.

Source video ↗

Capability breadth versus governance maturity

Benefit

Adding skills and tools increases what the agent can do and can raise contained-conversation volume.

Cost or risk

More capabilities without explainability, drift monitoring, and compliance tracking lowers overall readiness score despite higher containment.
