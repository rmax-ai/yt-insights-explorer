Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Adit Abraham
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Better agents through better data: how AI teams ingest unstructured data at scale.
  • Purpose: Provide AI engineers with actionable insights, frameworks, and ingestion pipeline recommendations for building reliable document intelligence and agentic workflows. Adit Abraham, co-founder and CEO of Reducto, discusses the practical challenges and strategies of building AI agents that process unstructured documents like PDFs, spreadsheets, and images at scale. He highlights the shift in AI applications from simple information synthesis (RAG) to autonomous execution (agents) and emphasizes that bad data inputs lead to compounding bad outputs in multi-step agent pipelines. The talk covers why PDFs are difficult, the tradeoff between traditional computer vision and vision-language models (VLMs), agentic OCR (speculative decoding and agent-in-the-loop verification), formatting strategies for LLMs versus embedding models, and agent harnesses for complex data tasks.

Topic Map

The Shift from Information Synthesis to Autonomous Action

  • Explanation: AI applications have shifted from retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and question-answering search tools to autonomous agents executing multi-step end-to-end tasks like reading, writing, and delivering complete workflows.
  • Key claims:
    • Initial AI products were information synthesis tools (search, chatbots, summarization).
    • Today's teams build agents to execute real human work end-to-end.
    • Agentic workflows introduce compounding risks when fed bad data inputs.
  • Examples:
    • Search interfaces vs. agents executing end-to-end tasks in finance, insurance, and legal sectors.
  • Terminology:
    • Information synthesis
    • Autonomous action
    • RAG
    • Agent pipelines
  • Why it matters: When agents make sequential decisions across multiple files, data errors compound and lead to catastrophic pipeline failures.

The Enterprise Data Bottleneck

  • Explanation: Enterprise data is predominantly unstructured, scattered across various repositories (Google Drive, Box, local drives), and multimodal.
  • Key claims:
    • Most enterprise data lives in unstructured formats like PDFs, spreadsheets, and scanned images.
    • Data is scattered across organizational silos with no uniform schema.
    • Downstream problems include parsing accuracy, context retrieval, and editing.
  • Examples:
    • Global financial institutions and insurance organizations with decades of historical unstructured records.
  • Terminology:
    • Unstructured data
    • Multimodal
    • Parsing extraction accuracy
    • Context retrieval
  • Why it matters: Language model capabilities only apply within the context to which intelligence is applied; poor ingestion bottlenecks agent performance.

Why PDFs Are Still Hard

  • Explanation: PDFs are fundamentally an image format designed in the 1990s for printing fidelity, making semantic extraction exceptionally challenging.
  • Key claims:
    • Current frontier models score around 30% on rigorous benchmarks like GDP-PDF.
    • PDFs include handwritten text, large tables, poor scans, multi-column layouts, and embedded charts.
    • Traditional OCR and post-processing fail on complex or non-standard document layouts.
  • Examples:
    • W-2 forms, financial tables with merged cells, line charts, and messy handwritten prescriptions.
  • Terminology:
    • GDP-PDF benchmark
    • OCR
    • Layout analysis
    • Multimodal
  • Why it matters: Enterprise workflows rely heavily on PDFs, yet foundation models struggle to accurately read and reason over them out of the box.

Traditional Computer Vision vs. Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

  • Explanation: Balancing traditional sub-100M parameter computer vision models for structure detection with VLMs for semantic reasoning.
  • Key claims:
    • Traditional CV models (sub-100M parameters) running on CPU are highly efficient at layout segmentation, object detection, and reading order.
    • VLMs introduce horizontal semantic understanding and handle long-tail elements like handwriting.
    • A hybrid approach combining traditional CV and VLMs yields superior accuracy.
  • Examples:
    • Using classical object detection for table and bounding box extraction combined with VLM reasoning for semantics.
  • Terminology:
    • Computer Vision (CV)
    • Vision-Language Models (VLMs)
    • Object detection
    • Semantics
  • Why it matters: Neither approach alone solves all document processing challenges at enterprise scale without performance or latency trade-offs.

Agentic OCR and Agent-in-the-Loop Frameworks

  • Explanation: Moving from human-in-the-loop to agent-in-the-loop using speculative decoding and token-level edits to correct OCR errors automatically.
  • Key claims:
    • Agentic OCR replaces manual review teams with verification and correction agents.
    • Speculative decoding applies token-level edits to correct OCR hallucinations or extraction errors.
    • Agents can cross-verify table totals and correct digit misreadings autonomously.
  • Examples:
    • Correcting 'LO' to '10' in medication dosages or fixing table sums.
  • Terminology:
    • Agentic OCR
    • Speculative decoding
    • Token-level edits
    • Agent-in-the-loop
  • Why it matters: It achieves high-confidence outputs without the high latency and cost of manual human review.

Data Formatting and Optimizing for RAG

  • Explanation: Tailoring document representations specifically for LLM reasoning versus embedding model retrieval.
  • Key claims:
    • Simple tables are token-efficient in Markdown; complex tables with merged cells require HTML.
    • Embedding models struggle with HTML tag noise and prefer natural language representations.
    • Splitting parses into dual representations (HTML for LLMs, natural language for embeddings) optimizes retrieval and reasoning.
  • Examples:
    • Transforming financial table data into natural language summaries for embeddings while preserving HTML tables for LLM reasoning.
  • Terminology:
    • Markdown
    • HTML tables
    • Natural language representation
    • Embedding models
  • Why it matters: Matching data format to the specific model (LLM vs. embedding) drastically improves retrieval accuracy and reduces token costs.

Effective Ingestion Pipelines and Pipeline Stages

  • Explanation: Decomposing ingestion into modular pipeline stages: classification, parsing, splitting, extraction, and formatting.
  • Key claims:
    • Classification and splitting are underinvestested stages that unlock real agent performance.
    • Routing documents to specialized parsers prevents quality erosion from bloated context windows.
    • Evaluating every stage independently (parse, retrieve, format, agent output) enables precise debugging.
  • Examples:
    • Routing hundreds of pages of mixed paper mail to appropriate parsing sub-pipelines.
  • Terminology:
    • Ingestion pipeline
    • Classification
    • Splitting
    • Parsing
    • Stage-level evaluation
  • Why it matters: End-to-end metrics do not tell you where errors occur; instrumenting every pipeline stage allows targeted investment and debugging.

Agent Harnesses as a New Frontier for Data

  • Explanation: Using structured agent loops (Generate, Evaluate, Refine) with code interpreters and multi-agent harnesses to solve complex extraction and chart analysis tasks.
  • Key claims:
    • Agent harnesses let models repeatedly improve their own output through structured loops.
    • Line chart extraction and complex structured extraction benefit from iterative self-correction loops.
    • File-based agent architectures allow agents to navigate parsed files directly instead of pre-computed snippets.
  • Examples:
    • Reconstructing complex line charts and multi-field CBP forms using iterative code execution and validation.
  • Terminology:
    • Agent harnesses
    • Generate-Evaluate-Refine loop
    • Code interpreter
    • File-based agent architecture
  • Why it matters: Agent harnesses overcome the limitations of single-shot model inference, closing the gap on precision and recall for complex long-tail documents.

Key Points

Bad inputs lead to bad outputs in multi-step agent pipelines

  • Explanation: When agents execute multi-step workflows across diverse files, initial ingestion errors compound throughout the pipeline.
  • Evidence: Insurance loss development factor calculations resulting in wrong outcomes due to incorrect table parsing.
  • Practical implication: AI teams must invest heavily in upstream data ingestion quality and verification before feeding documents to agents.

Hybrid CV and VLM architectures maximize parsing accuracy

  • Explanation: Combining sub-100M parameter traditional computer vision models for layout detection with VLMs for semantic reasoning creates superior efficiency and accuracy.
  • Evidence: Traditional CV efficiently handles object detection, layout understanding, and table segmentation on CPUs.
  • Practical implication: Avoid relying solely on massive foundation models for low-level structure tasks when smaller CV models are faster and cheaper.

Dual representation optimizes RAG for both LLMs and embeddings

  • Explanation: LLMs reason best over structured markup (HTML/Markdown), while embedding models perform better with natural language prose.
  • Evidence: HTML table structures contain too much tag noise for embedding models to correlate with natural language queries.
  • Practical implication: Generate separate representations of parsed content: HTML for LLM reasoning and natural language summaries for vector embeddings.

Stage-level evaluation is essential for production reliability

  • Explanation: Evaluating only end-to-end metrics obscures where failures happen in complex document processing pipelines.
  • Evidence: Best-performing teams evaluate parse, retrieve, format, and agent output independently.
  • Practical implication: Instrument every stage of your ingestion and agent pipeline to pinpoint and fix errors quickly.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Agentic OCR Framework

  • How it works: Combines traditional CV/VLM parsing with an automated verification and correction layer that applies token-level edits (speculative decoding) to fix OCR mistakes.
  • Components:
    • Document input
    • CV + VLM parse
    • Verification and correction layer
    • High-confidence output
  • When to use: When processing enterprise documents with handwriting, low-quality scans, or tables where 100% data fidelity is critical.

Harness Pattern (Generate-Evaluate-Refine)

  • How it works: A structured agent loop where the model generates an initial extraction, the output is checked against defined criteria (consistency, completeness, structural correctness), and flagged issues trigger targeted re-runs.
  • Components:
    • Generate (initial extraction or structured output)
    • Evaluate (check against criteria)
    • Refine (targeted re-run on flagged issues)
  • When to use: For complex extraction tasks like line charts, large financial tables, or multi-page forms requiring high precision.

File-Based Agent Architecture

  • How it works: Agents navigate parsed files directly using code and CLI tools, querying content in context while fetching metadata on-demand rather than relying on bloated static context windows.
  • Components:
    • Parse (structure preserved)
    • Classify (route to type)
    • Agent navigates (real-time decisions)
    • Full context / metadata on demand
  • When to use: When building advanced AI agents operating over large enterprise document corpuses and complex multi-page files.

Actionable Takeaways

  • Immediate:
    • Decompose parsing problems into specialized tools rather than relying on a single monolithic model.
    • Implement agentic verification and speculative decoding to catch OCR and extraction errors.
    • Format data appropriately for its consumer (HTML/Markdown for LLMs, natural language for embeddings).
  • Strategic:
    • Invest in classification and splitting stages in ingestion pipelines to prevent context bloat.
    • Build stage-level evaluations across parsing, retrieval, formatting, and agent outputs.
    • Transition from static RAG pipelines to file-based agent architectures with bidirectional read/write capabilities.
  • Questions to investigate:
    • How can we optimize token consumption during multi-step agent document reasoning?
    • What is the optimal balance between traditional CV models and frontier VLMs for our specific document types?
    • How do we systematically evaluate retrieval recall on complex multi-page enterprise documents?

Serve documents in multiple representations split by consumer model: HTML for LLM reasoning (preserves merged-cell/complex table structure) and natural-language renderings for embedding models (which are degraded by HTML tag noise). Why it matters

Generalization

Any RAG system should generate per-consumer views of the same source artifact and route each view to the model class that consumes it, rather than normalizing to one 'canonical' chunk format.

The Error-Compounding Tax: Why Agentic Pipelines Force a New Ingestion Accuracy Bar

In multi-step agent pipelines, upstream data errors multiply across sequential decisions rather than averaging out, so ingestion fidelity requirements scale with pipeline depth—making the earliest shared input the highest-leverage reliability investment.

Angle

Quantitative reliability engineering for AI pipelines: model error amplification, derive per-stage accuracy budgets, and argue against treating ingestion as a pre-processing afterthought.

Kill the Canonical Chunk: Consumer-Aware Document Representations for RAG and Agents

Error Amplification Simulator for Agentic Pipelines

movement-lab

A single upstream parse error (e.g., one wrong digit) propagates through N sequential agent decisions with a measurable amplification factor; final task failure probability increases superlinearly with step count and error severity, allowing derivation of per-stage accuracy budgets.

Proof of concept

Build a mock multi-step agent pipeline (extract → compute → summarize → decide) fed with controlled parse errors of varying types and severities; instrument each step and measure final task success vs. error severity and number of steps.

Measurement

Amplification factor (increase in final error probability per step), error-sensitive stages, and the per-stage accuracy threshold required to keep end-to-end failure below 5%.

DualView Consistency Checker

gatehouse

HTML and natural-language representations of the same document can drift; an automated divergence score (entity, numeric, table-structure mismatches) correlates with downstream retrieval-reasoning disagreement and can predict agent failures before they occur.

Proof of concept

Parse a corpus into both views, compute divergence metrics, then run retrieval and QA tasks using each view; correlate divergence scores with inconsistencies in answers and retrievals.

Measurement

Correlation between divergence score and drop in retrieval/QA accuracy; precision of flagging documents whose dual views cause agent errors.

Source video ↗

Agentic Correction Precision Benchmark

beyond-evals

Autonomous token-level corrections (speculative decoding) have lower precision on numeric digits and table totals than on prose, and precision varies by document class; a benchmark can derive safe escalation thresholds per error type.

Proof of concept

Take a labeled OCR error corpus, run a correction agent that applies token-level edits, and measure precision/recall per error type and document class against a human review baseline.

Measurement

Precision/recall of corrections, false-correction rate, and the threshold where correction precision drops below acceptable levels for autonomous use.

Hybrid Router Cost-Accuracy Profiler

new

For a document corpus, there exists a routing rule between CPU CV and VLM that achieves ≥90% of VLM-only accuracy at ≤30% of cost; misrouting cost (accuracy loss from routing errors) dominates end-to-end accuracy more than parser quality differences.

Measurement

Architectural implications

3

Document representations must become consumer-aware rather than canonical.

Before

One text extraction per document, shared by the embedding index and the LLM context.

After

Each parse emits multiple views (structured HTML for LLM reasoning, natural-language for embeddings) with explicit routing.

Consequence

Formatting becomes a first-class pipeline stage with its own evaluation, and index/context stores can diverge in representation.

Source video ↗

Verification moves inside the data pipeline as an agentic stage.

Before

Human-in-the-loop review queue corrects extraction errors post-hoc.

After

Agent-in-the-loop verifiers perform token-level corrective edits and invariant checks autonomously.

Consequence

New failure surface: an autonomous correction can silently introduce errors, so correction confidence and audit trails become required instrumentation.

Source video ↗

Ingestion decomposes into modular, independently evaluable stages (classification, parsing, splitting, extraction, formatting).

Before

Monolithic parse-to-text with a single end-to-end accuracy metric.

After

Stage-scoped pipelines where each boundary has its own eval and can be swapped.

Consequence

Per-stage observability enables error attribution and targeted improvement, but requires intermediate artifacts to be persisted and versioned.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Hybrid CV + VLM pipeline

Benefit

Superior accuracy with efficient CPU structure extraction plus VLM semantics.

Cost or risk

Two model tiers to maintain, with routing decisions that can misclassify certain documents, plus latency/throughput variance between CPU and VLM paths.

Source video ↗

Format selection (Markdown vs HTML)

Benefit

Markdown is token-efficient; HTML preserves merged-cell structure for complex tables.

Cost or risk

HTML structure needed for fidelity degrades embedding retrieval due to tag noise, forcing duplicated representations and higher storage/consistency cost.

Source video ↗

Agentic OCR corrections

Benefit

High-confidence output without manual review latency and cost.

Cost or risk

Autonomous token-level edits can silently produce wrong values (e.g., a corrected digit) so errors may become harder to detect than obvious OCR noise.

Source video ↗

Parser routing by classification

Benefit

Specialized parsers prevent quality erosion from bloated context windows.

Cost or risk

Misrouting a document sends it through the wrong parser, and classification itself becomes a new error source upstream of everything.
