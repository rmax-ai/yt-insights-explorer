Key Points

RL scaling is sigmoid-shaped

  • Explanation: Unlike pretraining power laws where models learn immediately, RL learns very little for a long time, then rapidly improves, and finally plateaus.
  • Evidence: Analysis from recent papers on RL compute scaling and Gemini 3 generated Python scripts.
  • Practical implication: Researchers must understand the pass-rate Goldilocks zone to effectively allocate RL compute.

Human vs Machine Evolution

  • Explanation: Humans evolved via RL over billions of years with pre-trained biological priors, whereas deep learning models rely heavily on pretraining data distributions.
  • Evidence: Discussion on brain regions, innate desires, and genetic priors.
  • Practical implication: Understanding biological learning priors can inform better artificial learning architectures.

Core insights

5
[E]Empirical Resulthigh noveltymoderate evidence

RL post-training scaling for LLMs follows a sigmoid curve — long flat region with almost no learning, then rapid improvement, then plateau — rather than the power law seen in pretraining; the pass rate of the task determines where on that curve you land, creating a narrow 'Goldilocks zone' for RL compute allocation.

Why it matters

If RL gains are sigmoid, then the marginal return on RL compute is highly non-linear and budget-dependent: pouring compute into a task whose pass rate sits in the flat region produces almost nothing, while the same compute near the inflection point produces large jumps. This changes how teams should schedule and gate RL training runs, and makes pass-rate instrumentation a first-class metric rather than an afterthought.

Generalization

Any learning loop where reward is sparse and binary (RLHF, RLVR, agentic task success, verifier-gated code generation) should be expected to show sigmoid rather than power-law returns, and should be scheduled by proximity to the inflection point, not by raw compute volume.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

RL's information gain per sample is bounded by the binary entropy of the outcome and degrades as a function of KL divergence from the policy: unrolling an entire trajectory collapses to a single binary reward bit, and the sample count needed for a gradient update scales exponentially with KL divergence.

Why it matters

This reframes RL inefficiency as a measurable information-theoretic quantity rather than a vague complaint. It gives engineers a concrete diagnostic — compute outcome entropy from pass rate, and project the number of rollouts needed as policy diverges — which can be used to decide when a trajectory-based reward is simply not worth the compute versus when a denser reward signal or supervised objective would be cheaper.

Generalization

For any system where a long rollout yields a low-bit reward (binary success/failure, single thumbs-up/down, single test pass), the information-per-token ratio of the training signal is the governing cost constraint; loss functions with dense per-step signal should dominate whenever they are constructible.

[>]Predictionmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The frontier is shifting back from 'age of scaling' to 'age of research' because pretraining data is finite and scaling laws are entering new regimes, so further capability gains must come from algorithmic breakthroughs rather than larger clusters.

Why it matters

This is an architectural-budget claim: infrastructure planning that assumes monotone returns from more data/compute is betting against a finite-resource constraint. Teams should expect the relative value of research, harness design, and data quality work to rise versus cluster size, and should reorganize roadmaps accordingly.

Generalization

Any compute-scaling program that depends on a finite input (web text, human demonstrations, verifier coverage) eventually hits a regime change where the bottleneck migrates from resources to algorithm design — capacity planning should track the scarce input, not the accelerator count.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltyweak evidence

Biological intelligence is framed as an RL process running on top of a pre-trained set of evolutionary/genetic priors (brain regions, innate desires), which is structurally analogous to how LLMs depend on a pre-training distribution before RL post-training — implying priors, not RL mechanics, are the differentiator.

Why it matters

If the human advantage comes from inherited priors rather than from the RL loop itself, then the highest-leverage engineering question is what to bake into the base model or harness before RL rather than how to run more RL. This shifts investment toward architecture, initialization, and environment/curriculum design.

Generalization

In any learning system, the choice of start state determines the achievable region of the RL search; improvements to initialization and environment shaping can dominate improvements to the optimizer.

The shift from the age of scaling to the age of research is driven by finite pretraining data and diminishing returns, not by a sudden loss of capability; therefore, infrastructure roadmaps that anchor on cluster size are betting against a finite resource, and the rate-limiting investment must shift to algorithmic and harness-level innovation.

Angle

Your RLHF Pipeline Is Probably in the Flat Part of the Sigmoid

Most alignment pipelines waste compute because they ignore pass-rate and KL divergence, landing in the flat region of the RL sigmoid; instrumenting outcome entropy and policy divergence is a first-class scheduling requirement, not an afterthought.

Angle

Practical engineering argument with a diagnostic framework for when to adjust task difficulty or switch objectives.

The Information-Theoretic Case Against Trajectory-Based RL

Because a full trajectory collapses to a single binary reward bit, and required samples grow exponentially with KL divergence, dense-reward and supervised objectives will dominate for most tasks; trajectory-based RL should be reserved for cases where pass rate is near the inflection and KL is tightly constrained.

Information-theoretic critique of current RLHF/RLVR methods, with concrete cost formulas and decision criteria.

Architectural implications

3

RL post-training yields one binary signal per full trajectory rollout.

Before

Training budget allocated in proportion to rollouts generated, on the assumption that more trajectories linearly improve the model.

After

Budget allocation must be conditioned on outcome entropy (derived from pass rate) and on policy KL divergence, since rollout count needed scales exponentially with divergence.

Consequence

Harnesses need per-task pass-rate and KL instrumentation as scheduling signals; tasks in the flat sigmoid region should be deprioritized or re-authored rather than scaled.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Trajectory-based RL reward sparsity

Benefit

Binary outcome rewards generalize to any task with a checkable success condition and require no reward model or dense human annotation.

Cost or risk

A full trajectory collapses into one bit, making the scheme information-poor per unit compute and structurally dependent on pass rate being near the sigmoid inflection.

Source video ↗

KL divergence between policy and behavior

Benefit

Staying close to the reference policy stabilizes RL updates and bounds reward hacking.

Cost or risk

Sample requirement grows exponentially with KL divergence, so accepting larger policy movement to gain capability imposes super-linear compute cost.

Source video ↗

Scaling-era continuation

Benefit

Extending compute and data keeps near-term capability gains flowing without requiring new algorithmic insight.

Cost or risk

Pretraining data is finite and scaling laws are entering new regimes, so continued reliance on scaling risks stalling progress once the finite input is exhausted.

Source video ↗

RL plateau as capability ceiling

Benefit

Post-plateau behavior is stable and cheap to maintain, with little further drift.

Cost or risk

If the sigmoid plateaus before the target capability is reached, additional RL compute is wasted; without per-task inflection tracking, teams may not detect that they are in the flat tail.
