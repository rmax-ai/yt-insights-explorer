Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Diogo Almeida
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Post-training, mid-training, and the shift from AI assistance to true software automation
  • Purpose: To analyze the limitations of RLHF and human-preference optimization in current LLMs and explain why the next era of AI requires a shift from assistant-centric software to smart software capable of true automation. Diogo Almeida, co-author of foundational OpenAI models including GPT-4, ChatGPT, and RLHF/InstructGPT, discusses the current state of AI post-training and the limitations of the ChatGPT era. He argues that current LLMs are optimized for human preference and assistance (human-in-the-loop) rather than automation, and introduces Typesafe's efforts to redesign the AI stack for reliability and full automation.

Key Points

Today's AI excels at assistance, not automation

  • Explanation: Models inherited from RLHF are designed to please humans in the loop rather than operate autonomously on high-stakes tasks.
  • Evidence: Enterprise adoption patterns show companies use AI for drafting and brainstorming but avoid automated high-stakes decision making.
  • Practical implication: Do not delegate high-stakes business decisions or expensive tasks fully to current RLHF models without human oversight.

Overpromising is a structural feature of RLHF

  • Explanation: Because reward models optimize for human preference, models lean toward sounding confident and agreeable even when incorrect.
  • Evidence: Users consistently overestimate AI speedup and find models agreeing with absurd prompts (e.g., praising fart sounds).
  • Practical implication: Engineers must account for model flattery and hallucinations when building production workflows.

Software building blocks must evolve into smart software

  • Explanation: Current SaaS simply wraps traditional software with LLM chatbots; true progress requires software architecture built natively around smart intelligence.
  • Evidence: SaaS architectures have remained largely unchanged since 2019 aside from added chatbot interfaces.
  • Practical implication: AI engineering needs to move beyond chat wrappers toward autonomous agentic architectures designed for reliability.

Project ideas

4

Asymmetry Probe

beyond-evals

Reward/preference models score confident-but-wrong outputs higher than hesitant-but-correct outputs on matched tasks, and this gap persists across model scale.

Proof of concept

Construct paired evaluation sets: same underlying claim expressed confidently-wrong versus hesitantly-correct, then score both with a preference/reward model and a correctness oracle; measure the score delta and its stability across model sizes and domains.

Measurement

Mean preference-score delta (confident-wrong minus hesitant-correct) with confidence intervals, plus correlation between the delta and model scale; a delta near zero would falsify the asymmetry claim.

Source video ↗

Error-Absorption Boundary Mapper

gatehouse

Task deployability without human supervision is predicted by the cost of absorbing a model error, not by benchmark score on that task.

Proof of concept

Pick a set of assistance tasks (drafting, coding suggestions) and automation tasks (data entry, rule-bound customer responses); run models unsupervised on both, quantify error-absorption cost per task, and test whether benchmark performance on the automation set predicts unsupervised success.

Measurement

Rank correlation between benchmark score and unsupervised success rate within the automation set, versus correlation with error-absorption cost; a strong benchmark correlation would falsify the hypothesis.

Source video ↗

Debiased Speedup Calibration Harness

movement-lab

Self-reported AI speedup overestimates measured task-outcome speedup, and the overestimate is larger for tasks where the model's confidence is decoupled from correctness.

Proof of concept

Run a controlled study where participants complete matched tasks with and without AI assistance, capture perceived speedup, and independently measure objective completion time and correctness; stratify by model confidence level.

Measurement

Gap between perceived and measured speedup overall and by confidence stratum; a gap that does not widen with confidence would falsify the coupling.

Source video ↗

Native-Verification Reference Stack

new

An application whose core control flow and verification are designed around a probabilistic intelligence layer achieves higher unsupervised task reliability than the same application with an equivalent LLM bolted on as a chat surface.

Proof of concept

Build one reference application twice — chatbot-bolted over a deterministic core, and intelligence-native with verification and error-handling at the core — and run the same unsupervised task suite against both.

Measurement

Unsupervised task success rate and rate of confident-but-wrong outputs on the identical suite; no significant difference would falsify the architecture claim.
