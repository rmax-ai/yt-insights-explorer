Overview

  • Speaker: Francois Chaubard, Alfredo Gonzalez, Yusuf Roohani, Garyk Brixi, Divya Nori
  • Channel: Y Combinator
  • Main topic: AI for Biology, Genome Language Modeling, and Personalized Cancer Research
  • Purpose: To discuss cutting-edge research papers at the intersection of artificial intelligence and biology, exploring how foundation models, genome language models, and autonomous research loops can accelerate biological discovery and healthcare applications. In this final YC Paper Club session, guest speakers from Arc Institute, Stanford, and Omanta discuss breakthroughs in AI for biology. The speakers explore genome language models like Evo 2, universal cell embedding models, automated research loops, and building personalized research laboratories for every cancer patient by shifting from population averages to individual patient data integration.

Key Points

Decline in sequencing costs drives biological data explosion

  • Explanation: The cost per raw megabase of DNA sequence has plummeted below Moore's Law, generating massive datasets in NIH public archives.
  • Evidence: Sequence Read Archive (SRA) growth charts showing multi-terabase scale data.
  • Practical implication: Provides unprecedented training data for genomic foundation models.

Moving from population averages to N-of-1 patient analysis

  • Explanation: Traditional cancer studies relied on cohorts and averages due to data scarcity; modern genomic abundance allows personalized multi-omic analysis per patient.
  • Evidence: Comparison between Cancer Genome Atlas summary maps and individual patient multi-dimensional data cubes.
  • Practical implication: Enables custom therapeutic option discovery for rare tumors and individual metastatic pathways.

Genome language models learn biology directly from DNA sequence

  • Explanation: Training next-token prediction models on raw genomic data across all domains of life captures complex regulatory, structural, and evolutionary dependencies.
  • Evidence: Evo 2 benchmark performance on BRCA1 variant effect prediction outperforming supervised human-specific models.
  • Practical implication: Allows zero-shot prediction of pathogenicity and functional design of genetic elements without laborious task-specific data collection.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

RSI / STaR for Bio (Recursive Self-Improvement loop)

  • How it works: A continuous loop of sampling hypotheses, selecting top candidates, testing drugs or sequences, measuring efficacy, and retraining foundation models.
  • Components:
    • Sample drugs/sequences
    • Select top n
    • Test in silico or in vitro
    • Measure efficacy
    • Retrain models
  • When to use: When scaling AI-guided experimentation to optimize biological designs and therapeutic discovery.

In-Context Learning (ICL) for Biological Sequence Design

  • How it works: Prompting genome language models with a small handful of functional examples to steer the generation of novel, functional biological sequences.
  • Components:
    • Initial prompt pool of natural sequences
    • Genome language model with ICL
    • In silico filters
    • Wet lab testing
    • Prompt updating with measured hits
  • When to use: When designing novel proteins, antibodies, enhancers, or phage genomes with limited labeled training data.

Core insights

6
[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Framing scientific discovery as a search problem over a vast hypothesis space lets you apply RL/post-training agent loops (sample → select top-n → test in silico or in vitro → measure → retrain) rather than one-shot prediction, making the wet lab or simulator a tool call inside the optimization loop.

Why it matters

It relocates the agent's responsibility from 'answer the question' to 'drive an experiment loop with measurable reward', which changes how you design harnesses, reward signals, and retry policy for any domain with an expensive external evaluator.

Generalization

Any agentic system where ground truth is expensive and slow (wet lab, A/B tests, hardware-in-the-loop, human review) should be architected as a sample-select-test-retrain loop with the expensive evaluator treated as a rate-limited tool, not as a post-hoc validation step.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

In-context learning over a handful of functional natural sequences can substitute for task-specific fine-tuning when designing novel biological sequences; the prompt pool itself becomes the trainable artifact, updated with wet-lab-measured hits.

Why it matters

It suggests a design where the 'model' is frozen and the mutable state is a curated example set — a much cheaper iteration surface than fine-tuning, and one that can be closed-loop updated from experiment results.

Generalization

For low-data design tasks, prefer frozen foundation model + curated in-context exemplar pool (versioned, updated from measured results) over fine-tuning; treat the prompt pool as a training dataset with lineage.

[E]Empirical Resulthigh noveltymoderate evidence

Training next-token prediction purely on raw DNA across all domains of life produces zero-shot variant-effect prediction that outperforms supervised models built specifically for one organism/task.

Why it matters

It is a concrete counterexample to the assumption that task-specific supervised data beats general pretraining; it argues for investing in breadth of pretraining corpus over narrow labeled datasets.

Generalization

General-purpose pretraining on broad heterogeneous corpora can beat narrow supervised models even on specialized tasks — measure this before assuming domain-specific labels are the bottleneck.

[E]Empirical Resultmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Unsupervised universal cell embeddings trained on tens of millions of cells recover cell-type hierarchies and developmental lineages without annotation, effectively learning the structure of the domain rather than a task.

Why it matters

The embedding becomes reusable infrastructure (a shared representation layer) rather than a per-task model, which is the architectural move that makes downstream agents cheap to build.

Generalization

Invest first in a domain-wide self-supervised embedding that recovers known structure, validate it by whether known taxonomy/lineage 'falls out', then layer task agents on top.

Source video ↗
[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The binding constraint on personalized per-patient analysis was data scarcity, not model capability; once sequencing cost dropped below Moore's-law trajectory, the correct unit of analysis shifted from cohort average to N-of-1.

Why it matters

It reframes the engineering question from 'how do we build a better general model' to 'what per-instance context can we now afford to assemble' — the same shift that makes long-context and per-tenant retrieval viable in other domains.

Generalization

When a data-acquisition cost curve collapses, re-examine the unit of analysis: architectures designed around aggregates need to be re-derived for per-instance inference.

Source video ↗
[P]Practicelow noveltyweak evidence

Designed outputs are not accepted on model score alone: generation is followed by in silico filters and then wet-lab testing, with measured hits fed back into the prompt pool.

Why it matters

It shows a concrete multi-stage verification funnel where cheap filters gate an expensive validator, and the validator's output is training signal — a pattern directly transferable to code-gen, content, and design agents.

Generalization

Use a cheap learned/heuristic filter to cut candidate volume by orders of magnitude before the expensive external validator; always close the loop by feeding validator results back into the generation context.

Project ideas

4

Exploration-Quota Discovery Loop

movement-lab

Adding uncertainty-weighted selection with an explicit exploration quota to a sample-select-test-retrain loop finds higher-reward candidates than greedy top-n selection under the same number of expensive validator calls.

Proof of concept

Build a harness with a cheap generator, a configurable selector, and a synthetic expensive oracle with hidden multimodal reward; run greedy top-n versus uncertainty-plus-quota selection for fixed oracle budgets.

Measurement

Best reward found after N validator calls, regret versus oracle optimum, coverage of hidden high-reward regions, and variance across seeds.

Source video ↗

Prompt-Pool vs Fine-Tune Budget Bench

beyond-evals

At equal expensive-validation budget, a frozen foundation model with a versioned in-context exemplar pool achieves a higher hit rate than a fine-tuned specialist on a sequence-design proxy task.

Proof of concept

Use a synthetic sequence-design task with a hidden fitness function; compare ICL pool updated from measured hits against LoRA fine-tuning, holding oracle calls and candidate counts constant.

Measurement

Validated hit rate per oracle call, iteration latency, performance variance, and degradation after pool contamination.

Source video ↗

N-of-1 Context Assembly Validator

new

Per-instance context assembly with pathway-level holdouts produces personalized recommendations that correct more subgroup misalignments than a cohort-average baseline in retrospective N-of-1 simulation.

Proof of concept

Simulate multi-omic patient profiles with known latent subgroups; compare aggregate model recommendations against per-instance retrieval/assembly with held-out pathways and provenance tracking.

Measurement

Misalignment correction rate on held-out pathways, false discovery rate, calibration of confidence, and stability under simulated noise.

Source video ↗

Cheap-Filter Funnel Harness

gatehouse

A learned cheap filter placed before an expensive validator cuts validator calls by more than 10x while losing less than 5% of true hits compared with unfiltered top-n selection.

Proof of concept

Build a cascade on a code-generation or design task with an expensive execution/eval oracle; compare random gating, heuristic gating, and learned filter gating at matched total cost.

Measurement

Validator calls saved, hit rate retained, false-negative rate, and end-to-end cost per validated hit.

Architectural implications

3

Experimental validation is slow and expensive, but model sampling is cheap.

Before

Model output treated as the final artifact; validation is a downstream human/QA gate outside the system.

After

Validation becomes a callable, rate-limited tool inside the agent loop, with top-n selection gating how many expensive calls are made.

Consequence

Harness must budget validator calls explicitly, log every sample-to-result pair for retraining, and handle partial-feedback loops as first-class state.

Source video ↗

Personalization requires assembling a per-instance multi-omic profile rather than retrieving from a population aggregate.

Before

One shared model/context built from cohort-level averages; per-user variation treated as noise.

After

Per-instance context assembly becomes the core retrieval problem; the shared model is the substrate, the instance bundle is the input.

Consequence

Context construction, provenance tracking, and per-instance evaluation move into the critical path; aggregate benchmarks stop predicting per-instance quality.

Source video ↗

A frozen genome/native-sequence foundation model plus an in-context exemplar pool replaces per-task fine-tuning.

Before

Each new design task requires collecting labeled data and fine-tuning a specialist model.

After

New tasks are expressed as curated prompt pools over a shared frozen model; the pool is the versioned, mutable artifact.

Consequence

Version control, curation quality, and contamination checks on the exemplar pool become the primary reliability levers, not training runs.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Zero-shot / few-shot generalization vs. task-specific supervision

Benefit

No task-specific labeled data collection; a single model covers variant effect prediction and sequence design across species.

Cost or risk

Performance and calibration outside the pretraining distribution are uncharacterized; generalization claims rest on a small number of headline benchmarks.

Source video ↗

Breadth of pretraining corpus vs. contamination and safety review

Benefit

Training on all domains of life yields higher-order regulatory, structural, and evolutionary signal.

Cost or risk

A model capable of designing functional phage genomes and antibodies is dual-use; the summary gives no discussion of screening or biosecurity gating.

Source video ↗

Closing the loop with retraining vs. iteration latency and feedback bias

Benefit

Measured efficacy feeds back into the model and prompt pool, compounding improvement.

Cost or risk

Each loop iteration requires wet-lab turnaround; only top-n candidates get tested, so the feedback signal is biased toward the selector's preferences.

Source video ↗

Per-patient analysis vs. statistical power

Benefit

Individual multi-omic profiles can correct misclassifications that cohort averages hide.

Cost or risk

N-of-1 conclusions have no statistical power and risk overfitting to noise; validation of a per-patient recommendation is inherently difficult.
