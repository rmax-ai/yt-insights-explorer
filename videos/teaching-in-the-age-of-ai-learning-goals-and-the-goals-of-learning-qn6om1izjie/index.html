Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Aaron Bauer
  • Channel: Jane Street
  • Main topic: Software developer education and developer tooling in the age of AI
  • Purpose: To discuss the philosophy and practice of developer education at Jane Street, how teaching methods have evolved, and the impact of AI tools on learning goals and software engineering workflows. A conversation exploring how software developer education, specifically at Jane Street through programs like OCaml bootcamps and internships, intersects with the rise of AI tools and LLMs. The discussion covers the evolution of teaching methodologies, the balance between conceptual understanding and practical tools, the role of code reviews and editors as home bases, and the challenges AI introduces to learning goals and cognitive load.

Note-Taking and Cognitive Processing

AI in Software Engineering and Education

Key Points

The Value of Struggling with Material

  • Explanation: Struggling through problems and writing code manually builds deep mental models that passive consumption or AI-generated solutions bypass.
  • Evidence: Cognitive psychology research on active learning and note-taking.
  • Practical implication: Educational programs must resist the temptation to make everything too easy or automated.

Editors as Integrated Workspaces

  • Explanation: Keeping development, version control, and code reviews inside a unified editor environment reduces context switching and improves developer flow.
  • Evidence: Jane Street's extensive use of Emacs and internal tools like Iron integrated directly into editors.
  • Practical implication: Tooling should unify workflows rather than fragmenting them across browsers and external apps.

The Challenge of AI-Generated Code in Education

  • Explanation: When AI writes code, learners miss the incremental feedback loop of catching syntax errors and debugging, which harms long-term retention.
  • Evidence: Observations from intern bootcamps where reliance on AI tools masks knowledge gaps.
  • Practical implication: Curricula must adapt to teach verification, debugging, and system design alongside AI utilization.

Why it matters

Generalization

Any workflow where an automated system produces artifacts the human must validate (code, configs, plans) inverts the cost curve: generation gets cheap, comprehension and verification become the bottleneck.

Generation Is Cheap; Verification Is the Job: Why AI Code Review Needs New Cognitive Tooling

AI-assisted development does not eliminate work; it transfers cognitive load from author to reviewer. Treating review as a secondary cost hides the real bottleneck and silently degrades defect detection. Teams should invest in review-time mental-model reconstruction—staged diffs, generated rationale, and prediction prompts—as first-class infrastructure.

Angle

Architectural implications

2

Code review moves from a web app to inline within the developer's primary editor.

Before

Edit, version control, and code review are split across multiple tools and browser apps, forcing context switches between the authoring and reviewing surfaces.

After

All three activities live inside one editor surface, with customized formatting and inline comments during review.

Consequence

Reduced context switching and faster review loops; agentic review or generation capabilities likely belong as editor-native affordances rather than standalone dashboards.

Source video ↗

The bottleneck in AI-assisted development shifts from code authoring to code verification and comprehension.

Before

Cost is dominated by writing code; the author builds a mental model as an inherent byproduct of the act of writing.

After

Generation is cheap, so the load moves to the reviewer, who must build the same mental model without the authoring process.

Consequence

Tooling and process must be redesigned to help reconstruct mental models of generated artifacts (explanations, provenance, staged diffs, prediction steps) or review quality silently degrades.

Benefit

Cost or risk

Bypasses the struggle and incremental feedback loop that build deep, retained mental models, weakening long-term competence.

Benefit

Cost or risk

Passive video learning

Cost or risk
