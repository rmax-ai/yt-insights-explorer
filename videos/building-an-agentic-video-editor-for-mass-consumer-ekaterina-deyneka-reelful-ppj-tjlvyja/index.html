Ekaterina Deyneka, founder and CEO of Reelful, discusses the challenges of manual video editing and introduces agentic video editing designed for mass consumers. She details the technical infrastructure combining media understanding, creative planning, sandbox execution, remotion composition, and a verification layer to automate professional-looking video creation from raw footage and simple prompts or directional templates.

[A]Architecturehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Representing the video timeline as React code (Remotion) turns video editing into a code-generation problem, so the agent's action space becomes programmatic composition rather than latent-space video synthesis — tracks, timing, and assets become editable, diffable artifacts.

Why it matters

Choosing the artifact representation determines what the agent can reliably manipulate, verify, and let humans hand-edit afterward. Code as the intermediate representation gives the agent a deterministic, composable, re-renderable substrate instead of an opaque pixel generator.

Generalization

For generative-media products, pick the most structured, deterministic artifact the domain allows (code, DSL, scene graph) as the agent's output target, then render from it — rather than having the model emit the final medium directly.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

The pipeline makes a verification layer a distinct, terminal stage after sandbox execution and composition — an explicit gate that inspects the rendered result rather than trusting the generating agent's output.

Why it matters

Separating 'produce the edit' from 'check the edit' is what makes an agentic media pipeline shippable; the generator cannot be its own validator, and composition errors are only observable after rendering.

Generalization

Agent pipelines whose output is expensive or user-facing should treat verification as a first-class stage operating on the produced artifact, not as a prompt instruction to self-check.

Why it matters

If the harness shape is domain-independent, sandbox orchestration, skill/tool wiring, and verification loops can be reused across domains — a team can fork the infrastructure and swap the artifact and its checker.

Generalization

Agent products converge on a reusable skeleton: intent capture → plan → isolated execution with tools → artifact render → verification. Domain specificity lives almost entirely in the artifact schema and the verifier.

[O]Mental Modelhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Editing real user footage inverts the difficulty of generative video: the bottleneck is curation and narrative selection (which moments, what structure, what to cut) rather than synthesis quality, because the input is long, messy, and ambiguous.

Why it matters

This changes what must be built: long-context media understanding, moment scoring, and editorial decision-making — not better pixel generation. It also implies different failure modes (bad selects, wrong narrative) and different evaluations.

Generalization

When the raw material already exists, agent value shifts from generation to selection and structure; the hard technical work becomes ingestion/understanding and ranking under an implicit editorial objective that is rarely specified by the user.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

For mass-consumer use, the interface replaces freeform prompting with directional templates (speak-to-camera, B-roll) plus a built-in timeline editor, effectively encoding the creative plan as a selectable policy rather than a natural-language request.

Why it matters

It challenges the default assumption that the prompt is the universal agent interface. Templates constrain the agent's planning space, making output quality more predictable and removing the user's burden of knowing how to instruct the agent.

Generalization

Constrain agent intent capture to a small set of parameterized recipes where the target audience lacks prompting skill; treat freeform prompting as an expert mode and let templates carry the policy.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Media understanding precedes planning: transcription of speech and asset-level analysis feed the creative plan (font pairs, cutting rules, B-roll decisions), and agent skills then execute within a remote sandbox.

Why it matters

It shows the decomposition where analysis produces structured context that constrains downstream generation — the plan is a checkpoint between understanding and execution, which is where human-in-the-loop correction could also be inserted.

Generalization

Design agent pipelines with an explicit, inspectable plan artifact between perception and action; it localizes errors (bad understanding vs bad execution) and enables intervention without re-running everything.

Gatehouse Verifier Escape-Rate Harness

gatehouse

A two-tier verifier combining deterministic render/composition invariants with VLM spot-checks reduces broken-edit escape rate below 2% versus a single VLM judge, at less than 1.3x render cost.

Proof of concept

Generate 100 Remotion compositions from raw clips, inject known faults (bad overlaps, missing assets, wrong timing, broken captions), and compare verifier configurations against human review.

Measurement

Broken-edit escape rate, false-positive rate, verification latency, and added cost per finished minute.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

Verification layer as a mandatory pipeline stage

Benefit

Catches broken edits and composition errors and is claimed to eliminate agent hallucinations in the final output.

Cost or risk

Adds an extra render/inspection pass per output; the summary gives no implementation detail, so the verifier's own error rate and whether it scales to long footage are unknown.

Source video ↗

Directional templates instead of prompting

Benefit

Removes cognitive load and makes the tool usable by people who cannot write effective prompts.

Cost or risk

Constrains output to the template's policy; users with intent outside the template set have no expressible path except manual timeline editing.

Source video ↗

Video-as-code as the agent artifact

Benefit

Gives the agent a programmatic, composable, human-editable representation of tracks, timing, and assets.

Cost or risk

Generated code can fail to render or compose incorrectly, so code validity and composition validity become separate failure surfaces requiring their own checks.

Source video ↗

Editing real footage vs. generative synthesis

Benefit

Serves authenticity-driven, everyday use cases where users want their own material polished.

Cost or risk

Requires advanced video understanding and editorial decision-making, making the problem harder than blank-canvas generation.

Source video ↗

Remote sandbox execution for agent skills

Benefit

Isolates agent-generated edits and provides a controlled environment for composition and rendering.

Cost or risk

Adds remote infrastructure and file/media transfer between the user's media and the execution machine; the summary does not quantify latency or cost impact.
