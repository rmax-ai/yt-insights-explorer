Overview

  • Speaker: Arjun Karanam
  • Channel: Sequoia Capital
  • Main topic: Continual learning platforms for AI agents
  • Purpose: To introduce Trajectory's platform for continual learning and explain how companies can build AI agents that improve with use over time. Arjun Karanam, co-founder of Trajectory, discusses the concept of continual learning for AI agents. He explores why current frontier models optimize purely for IQ without experience, the economic value of experience, and how Trajectory is building a platform that turns every interaction into continually improving systems through traceability, evaluations, model behavior specs, and iterative RL training.

Topic Map

The Experience Gap in Frontier AI Models

  • Explanation: Frontier AI models are optimized for high IQ (PhD level) with zero experience, resembling a brilliant new hire on day one. However, the economy values experience deeply, creating an 'Experience Gap' that companies must close.
  • Key claims:
    • Frontier labs optimize solely for IQ.
    • Today's agents are slow, expensive, and error-prone.
    • The economy values 30-year domain experience much more than raw intelligence alone.
  • Examples:

  • Terminology:

Key Points

The Economy Values Experience

  • Explanation: Companies pay most for personnel with decades of domain experience, not just raw intelligence.
  • Evidence: Comparison between Terence Tao on day one at an accounting firm vs. an experienced accountant.
  • Practical implication: AI agents must transition from static tools to continuous learners that compound in value with use.

Capturing Full Traces and Subagents

  • Explanation: Traceability must capture entire execution trees including subagents, rather than throwing away intermediate tool calls.
  • Evidence: Coding agents frequently break after a few commits if subagent failures are discarded.
  • Practical implication: Build error-correction, edits, and retries natively into product interfaces to capture clean training signals.

Making Evals Rolloutable

  • Explanation: Evaluations must reflect real production traffic and allow full replay of user starting states.
  • Evidence: Static benchmarks do not represent actual user workflows and requests.
  • Practical implication: Sample real distribution of tasks and ensure evaluation harness matches production.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Trace fidelity is a training-signal constraint, not just an observability concern: learning signal is destroyed if intermediate tool calls and subagent failures are discarded rather than captured as part of the full execution tree.

Why it matters

It defines the minimum data contract for any continual-improvement loop: agent success/failure is often caused at the subagent/step level, so outcome-only logging produces data that cannot be credited back to the responsible action.

Generalization

For any agent that learns from its own usage, the capture schema (execution tree + product telemetry) constrains what behaviors can ever be improved; incomplete logging silently caps the ceiling of the learning system.

[A]Architecturehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Product interface affordances (edits, undos, retries, error correction) are treated as native label-generation mechanisms for RL, rather than as mere UX conveniences.

Why it matters

It relocates data-labeling from a separate offline annotation effort into the product interaction loop, where user corrections become clean supervision signals at near-zero marginal cost.

Generalization

Interaction primitives that expose user intent (revert, edit, retry, reject) can be designed deliberately as supervision APIs for any agent product that wants to keep training on production usage.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Evaluations must be 'rolloutable': sampled from the real distribution of production tasks and able to replay actual user starting states, rather than being static benchmarks.

Why it matters

Static benchmarks do not predict production behavior, so a training-loop that gates deployment on them can improve the benchmark while degrading real workflows; replayability makes eval an executable, environment-matched artifact.

Generalization

Any iterative RL or fine-tuning loop needs an eval harness that mirrors production state and task distribution, otherwise model updates and harness updates cannot be compared on the axis that matters.

Deep dives

5

Trace granularity as the hard ceiling on continual learning

Research question

How does the granularity of captured execution traces (outcome-only vs. tool-call level vs. full subagent tree) change the achievable improvement rate and the regression rate of a continually trained agent?

Why

If the learning loop's ceiling is set by the capture schema rather than by the optimizer, then storage/retention/schema design sits on the critical path of model quality; teams that log only final outcomes are silently capped and will misattribute failure to model capacity. Establishing the granularity/regression curve converts an asserted failure mode into an engineering budget line.

Source video ↗

Rolloutable evals as production-state replay

Research question

Does gating model and harness updates on distribution-sampled, state-replayable evals predict production behavior better than static benchmark suites, and by how much does the gap widen as the agent accumulates domain experience?

Why

A training loop gated on static benchmarks can raise the benchmark while degrading real workflows, because benchmark tasks are not drawn from the production distribution. If evals must replay real user starting states, eval infrastructure becomes coupled to product state management — a concrete architectural dependency most teams have not budgeted for.

Source video ↗

Harness/model co-evolution and the weights-versus-code boundary

Research question

When capability can be placed either in weights or in orchestration code, what cost/latency/retrainability tradeoffs determine the optimal split, and does a lighter, more informative harness outperform a hard-coded workflow harness over successive training rounds?

Why

Treating the harness as a trainable artifact breaks the assumption that the model is the only thing that improves, which changes what belongs in code versus in weights. This turns a fixed architectural boundary into a per-iteration optimization decision — a directly actionable design lever for agent teams.

Source video ↗

Product affordances as a supervision channel (edit/undo/retry)

Research question

Do natively instrumented edit/undo/retry affordances yield training signal of sufficient quality and volume to replace a meaningful fraction of offline human annotation, and do they cause regressions when the action space has irreversible side effects?

Why

This relocates labeling from an offline annotation effort into the interaction loop at near-zero marginal cost, which changes the economics of continual learning. It also forces UI/UX and data-pipeline design to be co-designed and requires reversible state, which is nontrivial for agents with side effects.

Source video ↗

Multi-tenant trace retention under privacy and isolation constraints

Research question

What is the learning-signal cost of enforcing per-customer trace isolation and differential-privacy noise budgets when training on multi-tenant production traces, and can isolation be enforced at the trace level without collapsing per-customer improvement?

Why

The same capture completeness that makes the learning loop work is what creates the privacy and isolation obligation, so the two constraints are in direct tension. Without a measured tradeoff curve, teams will either over-retain (compliance risk) or under-retain (capped learning), and the correct point is currently unspecified.

Article ideas

4

You Can Only Improve What You Can See: Trace Fidelity Is the Real Ceiling on Agent Continual Learning

The limiting factor in continually improving agents is not the optimizer or the model but the capture schema: if intermediate tool calls and subagent failures are not retained as first-class training inputs, the learning loop silently plateaus, and teams will misdiagnose that plateau as a model-capability problem.

Angle

Move observability out of the debugging-tooling category and into the training-data-contract category, showing how retention policy, schema design, and privacy controls become model-quality decisions with a measurable ceiling.

Source video ↗

Benchmarks Are Not Evals: Why Rolloutable, State-Replayable Evaluation Is the Gate for Agent Training

Any iterative RL or fine-tuning loop gated on static benchmarks can improve the benchmark while degrading production workflows, so evaluations must be sampled from the live task distribution and able to replay real user starting states — which makes eval infrastructure a dependency of product state management, not a standalone test harness.

Angle

Contrast benchmark-shaped evals with executable, environment-matched replay, and enumerate the state-reconstruction requirements this imposes on the product and data layers.

Source video ↗

The Harness Is a Weight: Rethinking What Belongs in Code Versus in Parameters

Once the harness is understood as a trained artifact that carries domain knowledge and must co-evolve with the model, the boundary between orchestration code and weights stops being a fixed architectural line and becomes an optimization decision driven by cost, latency, and retrainability — and hard-coded workflows become a liability rather than a safety net.

Angle

Argue for light, open, informative harnesses by showing how each hard-coded step is a capability locked out of the training loop.

Source video ↗

Your Product UI Is a Labeling Pipeline

Edit, undo, retry, and error correction should be designed deliberately as supervision APIs — instrumented to emit clean training signals — because that relocates labeling from a scarce offline annotation effort into production interactions at near-zero marginal cost.

Angle

Reframe ordinary interaction primitives as data-collection surfaces, and confront the cost: reversible state for agents with side effects, plus a second responsibility for every product surface.

Project ideas

4

Trace-Granularity Ablation Bench

new

Increasing trace capture granularity from outcome-only to tool-call level to full subagent execution trees monotonically reduces multi-commit regression rate in a coding agent after successive training rounds, with a measurable threshold below which added trace noise reverses the gain.

Proof of concept

Instrument one coding agent to emit three parallel trace streams at increasing granularity over the same task distribution; run identical continual-learning cycles per stream and replay the same task set after each commit sequence; plot regression rate against capture depth and against added-noise volume.

Measurement

Regression rate per commit index per granularity level, plus improvement in rolloutable-eval pass rate and the cost/latency overhead of each capture depth.

Source video ↗

Replay-Eval Harness

beyond-evals

An eval harness that reconstructs production starting states and samples the live task distribution predicts post-update production performance significantly better than a static benchmark suite, and the predictive gap widens as domain-specific experience accumulates.

Proof of concept

Build a replayer that snapshots and restores real user starting states, sample tasks from captured production traces, and run the same set of model/harness updates through both the replay harness and a static benchmark; compare predicted versus observed production outcomes.

Measurement

Rank correlation between gate decision and post-deploy production outcome, false-ship and false-block rates, and the divergence between benchmark delta and production delta over successive training rounds.

Source video ↗

Weights-vs-Workflow Splitter

movement-lab

Moving a defined capability from hard-coded orchestration into model weights (or vice versa) produces a predictable cost/latency/retrainability outcome, and a light, open harness outperforms a hard-coded workflow harness after N co-evolution rounds.

Proof of concept

Take one agent task with a workflow-style harness, enumerate steps, and produce paired variants where each step lives in code versus weights; train both variants on the same trace corpus across several rounds and compare on rolloutable evals.

Measurement

Task success and regression rate per round, inference cost and latency per task, and the size of the training delta needed to absorb each migrated capability into weights.

Source video ↗

Isolated Trace Trainer

gatehouse

Per-customer trace isolation plus a bounded privacy budget can be enforced without collapsing per-customer improvement, and there exists a measurable isolation granularity (trace-level vs. tenant-level) where learning signal per customer remains positive while cross-tenant leakage is eliminated.

Proof of concept

Split a multi-tenant trace corpus into isolated shards with configurable privacy noise budgets; train per-tenant adapters alongside a shared baseline, then measure per-customer improvement plus a leakage-attack probe against the shared components.

Measurement

Per-tenant eval delta versus a non-isolated control, privacy budget consumed per unit of learning signal, and leakage-probe success rate across isolation granularities.

After

Consequence

4

Full trace and telemetry capture vs. privacy and customer isolation

Benefit

Capturing complete execution trees including subagents yields the signal needed to credit and fix failures.

Cost or risk

Retaining user interaction data for continual training raises differential-privacy and per-customer data-isolation obligations that the summary explicitly leaves unresolved.

Open-weight training vs. closed hosted frontier models

Benefit

Open weights unlock continual training and instant deployment of improved models.

Cost or risk

Forfeits the automatic capability gains and reliability of closed hosted frontier models, in exchange for operating your own training and deployment stack.

Human feedback vs. automated completion signals in credit assignment

Benefit

Both signals are available from production usage and can drive RL updates.

Cost or risk

Human feedback is high-quality but scarce and expensive; automated completion signals are abundant but noisy, and the correct weighting is unspecified.

Native edit/undo/retry affordances vs. product surface complexity

Benefit

Produces clean supervision labels from genuine user corrections.

Cost or risk
