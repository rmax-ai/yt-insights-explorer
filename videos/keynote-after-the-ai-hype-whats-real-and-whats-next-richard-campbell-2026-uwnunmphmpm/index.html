Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Richard Campbell
  • Channel: NDC Conferences
  • Main topic: Artificial Intelligence hype, history, financial cycles, and current reality in software development
  • Purpose: To contextualize the current generative AI boom within historical technology cycles, debunk hype, and provide developers and organizations with a realistic perspective on AI's true capabilities and limitations. Richard Campbell explores the history of artificial intelligence, drawing parallels between current generative AI hype cycles and past technology booms like the .com bubble. He discusses the evolution of neural networks, ImageNet, Siri, GPT-2 to GPT-4, OpenAI, Microsoft, DeepSeek, and AlphaFold, analyzing the economic sustainability, infrastructure investments, and societal impact of modern AI tools.

Topic Map

History and naming of AI

  • Explanation: Exploration of the origins of the term 'artificial intelligence' in the 1950s by scientists raising funds from the US military, and the recurring 'AI winters'.
  • Key claims:
    • AI is a terrible name coined in the 1950s by scientists seeking military funding.
    • Humans are hardwired to perceive intelligence and faces everywhere (pareidolia), making us susceptible to AI hype.
  • Examples:
    • Joseph Weizenbaum's 1966 program ELIZA as a rudimentary psychotherapist.
    • Science fiction films like 2001: A Space Odyssey, The Terminator, and Avengers: Age of Ultron shaping public perception.
  • Terminology:
    • Artificial Intelligence
    • AI Winter
    • ELIZA
    • Pareidolia
  • Why it matters: Understanding that AI's naming and perception have been influenced by marketing and science fiction for decades helps separate reality from hype.

The emergence of Neural Networks and ImageNet

  • Explanation: How deep learning, backpropagation, and the ImageNet competition transformed computer vision and speech recognition.
  • Key claims:
    • Geoffrey Hinton's work on deep neural nets and backpropagation laid the foundation for modern deep learning.
    • The ImageNet competition in 2012 used 14 million labeled images to dramatically improve visual recognition.
  • Examples:
    • Siri created via Apple buying voice recognition tech trained by SRI researchers.
    • Google's acquisition of Hinton's team to form Google Brain.
  • Terminology:
    • Neural Nets
    • Backpropagation
    • ImageNet
    • Siri
    • Google Brain
    • Generative Pre-trained Transformer
  • Why it matters: Neural networks solved hard pattern recognition problems once compute power and large datasets became available.

The OpenAI, Microsoft, and hyperscaler economics

  • Key claims:
    • OpenAI started as a public research lab but shifted to heavy commercial funding and capital needs.
    • Microsoft invested billions in OpenAI in exchange for Azure cloud commitments (self-dealing model).
  • Examples:
    • Microsoft investing $1 billion initially, followed by more billions.

The Gartner Hype Cycle and comparison to the .com boom

  • Explanation: Mapping current AI adoption against the Gartner hype cycle and historical technology bubbles like the .com boom and Netscape IPO.
  • Key claims:
    • AI has hit the peak of inflated expectations and is sliding toward the trough of disillusionment.
    • Data center infrastructure buildout resembles the overbuilding of the .com boom.
  • Examples:

AlphaFold and scientific breakthroughs

  • Explanation: How DeepMind's AlphaFold solved protein folding, revolutionizing biology and medicine.
  • Key claims:
    • Protein folding is one of biology's most complex challenges, with 10^35 possible combinations.
    • AlphaFold achieved 90% accuracy by 2020, computing 200 million protein folds and publishing them for free.
  • Examples:
    • John Kendrew winning a Nobel Prize after a decade of studying myoglobin.
    • New malaria vaccines and antibiotics developed using AlphaFold data.
  • Terminology:
    • AlphaFold

  • Why it matters: Demonstrates real, profound scientific utility of AI beyond chat interfaces and marketing hype.

Key Points

AI is software, not a person

  • Explanation: People tend to anthropomorphize software and LLMs, treating them like therapists or conscious entities due to human evolutionary tendencies.
  • Evidence: Users becoming emotionally attached to chatbots or attributing human sentience to systems like ChatGPT.
  • Practical implication: Maintain professional skepticism and remember that LLMs are advanced predictive text engines lacking common sense.

Massive capital expenditure and infrastructure strains

  • Explanation: Hyperscalers are spending hundreds of billions on data centers and GPUs, creating significant power and land demands.
  • Evidence: Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, and Amazon capex numbers distorting broader economic growth metrics.
  • Practical implication: Organizations must evaluate the actual ROI of AI infrastructure investments against cooling market enthusiasm.

Real vs Hype in software engineering

  • Explanation: While coding assistants like GitHub Copilot provide genuine productivity boosts, many ancillary AI products fail to deliver sustainable profit.
  • Evidence: Proliferation and disappearance of numerous small AI coding tools as major players consolidate.
  • Practical implication: Focus on fundamentals and proven tools rather than chasing every new AI product wave.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Gartner Hype Cycle

  • How it works: Tracks a technology's maturity from trigger through inflated expectations, disillusionment, and eventual productivity plateau.
  • Components:
    • Technology Trigger
    • Peak of Inflated Expectations
    • Trough of Disillusionment
    • Slope of Enlightenment
    • Plateau of Productivity
  • When to use: Evaluating emerging technologies to determine whether market enthusiasm matches realistic utility.

Examples & Case Studies

DeepMind's AlphaFold predicted protein structures with high accuracy.

  • Illustrates: Real, high-value scientific application of AI solving previously intractable problems.
  • Lesson: AI delivers transformative results when applied to domains with massive structured data and rigorous validation.

Cambridge Analytica exploited Facebook data for political advertising during Brexit.

  • Illustrates: The risks of manipulative AI systems optimizing for user engagement and deception.
  • Lesson: Powerful recommendation and targeting software can be misused when user engagement is the primary metric.

Actionable Takeaways

  • Immediate:
    • Avoid anthropomorphizing LLMs; treat them as sophisticated software tools.
    • Use coding assistants like GitHub Copilot critically while maintaining personal accountability for code.
  • Strategic:
    • Prepare for the trough of disillusionment as AI hype cycles mature and investment returns face scrutiny.
    • Focus organizational strategy on proven productivity gains rather than speculative AI general intelligence promises.
  • Questions to investigate:
    • How sustainable are current hyperscaler capital expenditures on AI data centers?
    • What are the long-term regulatory and legal liability frameworks for AI-generated code and content?

Core insights

7
[O]Mental Modelhigh noveltymoderate evidence

AI capability value concentrates in domains with a well-defined, verifiable objective and an enormous but searchable space — AlphaFold reached 90% accuracy on protein folding (10^35 combinations), computed 200M folds, and released them for free, whereas open-ended chat interfaces rely on the same model class but lack a ground-truth scoring function.

Why it matters

It suggests the leverage point for engineers is not model scale but problem framing: choosing tasks where correctness is mechanically checkable produces defensible, reusable artifacts (datasets, folds, benchmarks) instead of demos. This reframes where to spend prototyping budget.

Generalization

Prefer constructing agent tasks with machine-verifiable reward (tests, compilers, formal checks, retrieval ground truth) over tasks evaluated by human-perceived fluency; verifiable domains produce compounding data assets.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

A structural circular-financing pattern underpins frontier model access: a hyperscaler invests capital into a lab and receives cloud consumption commitments back, so compute revenue and investor capital flow through the same counterparties.

Why it matters

If a vendor's AI revenue is partly funded by its own investment capital, published demand signals are a poor proxy for external unit economics. Engineers planning multi-year cost models on today's token pricing should treat price as subsidized and subject to repricing at the trough.

Generalization

When a platform's capital and consumption flow between related parties, treat its pricing and capacity guarantees as unstable inputs to your cost model and design for repricing or migration.

[>]Predictionmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Capital expenditure concentration is itself a macro distortion: hyperscaler data center buildout accounted for over half of S&P 500 value increases in 2024, echoing .com-era overbuilding.

Why it matters

Compute supply, GPU availability, and inference pricing are being driven by capital markets rather than by settled demand. That implies procurement terms and capacity can swing sharply if the investment thesis cools, which changes how much infrastructure risk an application team should absorb.

Generalization

Infrastructure-dependent systems inherit the financing cycle of their infrastructure providers; plan capacity and pricing sensitivity against capital-cycle scenarios, not steady-state curves.

[!]Failure Modemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Human pattern-perception bias (pareidolia) is a systemic interface-level failure mode: users attribute sentience and therapeutic intent to LLMs, and the speaker's stated remedy is to treat LLMs as predictive text engines lacking common sense.

Why it matters

Anthropomorphization is not a user error but a predictable property of the audience, so assistant UX, memory features, persona framing, and emotional-support-style responses carry trust and safety risk that must be mitigated in design rather than documented in disclaimers.

Generalization

Systems serving human-facing generative output should treat perceived agency as an emergent hazard: constrain persona claims, avoid memory that implies relationship continuity, and instrument for over-reliance.

[~]Tradeoffmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The tooling market is polarizing: coding assistants such as GitHub Copilot deliver genuine productivity gains while many adjacent AI products fail to reach sustainable profit, and numerous small AI coding tools appear and disappear as majors consolidate.

Why it matters

Dependency selection is now a reliability question. Tools that cannot sustain unit economics disappear or get absorbed, taking APIs, pricing, and support with them, so integration boundaries and exit paths matter as much as raw capability.

Generalization

In a consolidating tool market, prefer thin adapters over deep integration points, and treat vendor viability as an architectural constraint alongside latency and cost.

[E]Empirical Resultmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The 2012 deep learning breakthrough came from pairing an existing algorithm class (neural nets, backpropagation) with a 14-million-image labeled dataset and available compute — data curation, not a new algorithm, was the unlock.

Why it matters

It argues that for teams without novel research, investment in labeled data and evaluation corpora is the higher-leverage activity than chasing model or architecture changes, and that capability jumps historically track dataset scale.

Generalization

Capability step-changes follow from data and compute availability applied to a known method; budget accordingly for dataset construction and labeling over method novelty.

It gives a defensible heuristic for sequencing adoption — pilot now, standardize later — and for timing irreversible commitments such as data-center capacity or platform lock-in during a period of inflated expectations.

Project ideas

Verifiable Agent Benchmark Harness

beyond-evals

Agent tasks with mechanically checkable reward (unit tests, type checks, formal verifiers) show a higher rate of improvement per unit of data/compute than tasks scored by human fluency preference.

Proof of concept

Build a paired task suite: coding repair tasks with unit tests vs. open-ended tasks with LLM/human preference scoring; train/finetune or prompt-optimize on both and compare improvement curves.

Measurement

Success@k per dollar/compute; slope of improvement vs. data/compute; delta vs. fluency-scored tasks.

Source video ↗

Token Repricing Stress-Test

new

Current application token economics invert under plausible capital-cycle repricing scenarios because a material share of provider pricing depends on circular financing and capex-driven capacity.

Proof of concept

Build a model that decomposes provider list price into compute, capital recovery, and margin; simulate repricing and capacity quota scenarios for a reference application; test migration to alternative providers.

Measurement

Break-even price increase; margin variance; time/cost to switch providers before and after abstraction.

Anthropomorphization Guardrail Kit

gatehouse

Constrained persona framing, explicit memory boundaries, and dependency cues reduce user over-attribution and emotional reliance without reducing task success.

Proof of concept

Implement an assistant with configurable persona/memory controls; run A/B tests against an unconstrained baseline on identical tasks.

Measurement

Attribution survey scores; dependency/over-reliance indicators; task success and satisfaction.

Thin Adapter Migration Testbed

new

Thin, swappable adapters reduce migration time and code churn when an AI tool is deprecated or repriced compared with deep vendor-native integration.

Proof of concept

Architectural implications

3

Frontier model access is packaged with cloud-commitment financing between investors and providers.

Before

Model pricing and capacity treated as competitive market outputs that reflect underlying compute costs.

After

Pricing and capacity treated as byproducts of capital deployment by related parties, subject to repricing.

Consequence

Cost models should carry an explicit assumption for price and quota discontinuity, with abstraction over model providers to allow migration.

The AI tooling market is consolidating while sustaining genuine value only in a few proven categories.

Before

Deep integration with the most capable niche AI tool for a given workflow.

After

Thin, swappable adapters in front of a small set of durable vendors.

Consequence

Reduced blast radius when a dependency is acquired, deprecated, or repriced; slight loss of tool-native features.

Before

After

Trust treated partly as an interaction-design problem — persona claims, memory continuity, and dependency cues are first-class controls.

Consequence

Assistants need constrained self-description, deliberate memory boundaries, and instrumentation for over-reliance, not just disclaimers.
