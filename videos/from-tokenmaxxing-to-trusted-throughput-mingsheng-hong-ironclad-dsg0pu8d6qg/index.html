Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Mingsheng Hong
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Measuring and improving AI ROI and throughput in engineering organizations
  • Purpose: To provide engineering leaders with a practical framework and operating model for maximizing AI ROI through trusted throughput rather than raw token volume. Mingsheng Hong, VP of AI Engineering at Ironclad, discusses the shift from tokenmaxxing (maximizing token usage) to trusted throughput. He outlines a practical framework for measuring and improving AI ROI in enterprise engineering, covering cost control, value measurement, bottleneck fixing (specifically code review and CI), and building trust through a three-bucket model of assurance evidence, engineering judgement, and outcome evidence.

Topic Map

The Cost Wake-Up Call

  • Explanation: Media stories highlight massive token spend and usage spikes at companies like Amazon and Meta, signaling that while AI adoption worked, cost management and control are becoming critical challenges.
  • Key claims:
    • Usage is a useful signal, but the wrong outcome to optimize.
    • Token dashboards should act as smoke detectors, not leaderboards.
    • Maximizing token usage can create perverse incentives.
  • Examples:
    • Amazon's KiroRank shut down after usage-score chasing
    • Meta token leaderboard controversy
    • A company spending $500M on Claude in months
  • Terminology:
    • tokenmaxxing
    • smoke detector vs scoreboard
  • Why it matters: Companies need to move past the initial adoption spike and establish policies to measure and control AI costs effectively without discouraging innovation.

First Adopt, Then Optimize

  • Explanation: Organizations must first go through access and adoption before focusing on control and trusted throughput. Trying to optimize costs too early stifles adoption.
  • Key claims:
    • Token dashboards in early stages should be change-enablement tools, not cost controls.
    • Understanding team-level usage helps find lagging teams and unlock habits before ROI optimization.
  • Examples:
    • Engineers moving from hand-crafting code to reviewing AI-slop code.
  • Terminology:
    • access
    • adoption
    • control
    • throughput
  • Why it matters: Premature cost cutting or austerity measures kill AI adoption and developer enthusiasm.

Ironclad and Trust Building

  • Explanation: Ironclad is a legal contracting AI company. Building trust with lawyers, procurement, and internal teams is the number one priority for AI feature adoption.
  • Key claims:
    • AI-enabled engineering work is valuable only when we can responsibly review, validate, deploy, and maintain it.
    • Customers ask: Is this AI answer, recommendation, or action grounded in my actual contracts?
  • Examples:
    • Lawyers feeding conversational search sets of known contracts to test AI output quality.
  • Terminology:
    • legal contracting AI
    • grounded in contracts
  • Why it matters: Trust is essential for enterprise AI adoption; without it, users revert to manual workflows.

Trusted Throughput Framework

  • Explanation: Introducing trusted throughput as a proxy metric for AI ROI, combining cost, value, bottleneck fixing, and leverage.
  • Key claims:
    • The goal is ROI, not austerity.
    • Lines of code (LOC) and raw PR counts are flawed metrics for measuring productivity.
    • Trusted throughput = reviewed, validated, maintainable customer value per dollar of AI spend and unit of human attention.
  • Examples:
    • Counting open PRs vs merged PRs vs PR complexity scores.
  • Terminology:
    • trusted throughput
    • Token ROI
    • PR complexity score
  • Why it matters: Traditional software metrics like lines of code fail when AI generates code rapidly; new metrics focusing on review and merge capacity are required.

Shifting Bottlenecks Downstream (Review and CI)

  • Explanation: AI increases code generation capacity faster than review and merge capacity, shifting bottlenecks downstream into code review and continuous integration (CI).
  • Key claims:
    • Review and CI determine how much AI-generated work the organization can actually absorb.
    • Anti-pattern: making PRs larger to work around merge queue strain. Solution: fix the pipeline.
    • AI should serve as an assistant, not an authority in review.
  • Examples:
    • Using AI for first-pass review (summarizing diffs, classifying risk, finding missing tests) before human judgment.
  • Terminology:
    • downstream bottlenecks
    • AI first pass
    • CI trust gate
    • flaky tests
  • Why it matters: Without improving review and CI infrastructure, AI-generated code floods review queues and burns human attention.

Solution Framework for Token ROI

  • Explanation: A three-part operating framework: Guardrails (budget caps, usage, anomalies), Engineering discipline (agent step limits, prompt caching, context pruning, model routing), and Learning loop (reviewing spikes, failures, and reusable patterns).
  • Key claims:
    • Token optimization is a layered operating system, not a single trick.
    • The winner is not the team that uses the most AI, but the one that converts AI into the most trusted customer value per dollar and unit of human attention.
  • Examples:
    • System prompt fixed at the top with varying content at the bottom for prompt caching.
    • Context pruning in longer chat sessions.
  • Terminology:
    • guardrails
    • engineering discipline
    • learning loop
    • prompt caching
    • context pruning
  • Why it matters: Provides a comprehensive, structured approach to scaling AI engineering sustainably.

Key Points

Usage is a signal, not a scoreboard

  • Explanation: Tracking token usage helps detect adoption gaps and cost spikes, but optimizing purely for token volume creates perverse incentives.
  • Evidence: Stories of teams competing to top internal token leaderboards.
  • Practical implication: Use usage dashboards as smoke detectors rather than leaderboards.

The constraint has moved downstream

  • Explanation: AI code generation outpaces human review and merge capacity, creating bottlenecks in code review and CI/CD pipelines.
  • Evidence: Increased open and merged PR counts without proportional customer value.
  • Practical implication: Fix the pipeline and use AI as an assistant in code review (first-pass checks) rather than increasing PR sizes.

Trusted throughput as the primary metric

  • Explanation: Measure ROI by looking at trusted throughput: reviewed, validated, and maintainable customer value per dollar and unit of human attention.
  • Evidence: Lines of code and PR counts alone are easily gamed and detached from customer value.
  • Practical implication: Combine assurance evidence, engineering judgment, and outcome evidence.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Token ROI Framework

  • How it works: A four-step practical process to measure and improve AI ROI in engineering.
  • Components:
    • Measure cost (where are tokens going)
    • Measure value (what trusted output resulted)
    • Fix bottlenecks (where does value get stuck)
    • Optimize leverage (how to boost trusted value of tokens)
  • When to use: When organizations have passed the initial adoption phase and need to manage AI spend and productivity.

Trust Bucket Model

  • How it works: Defines trusted throughput through three distinct evaluation buckets.
  • Components:
    • Assurance evidence (test coverage, security checks, rollout controls)
    • Engineering judgement (code clarity, maintainability, architecture fit)
    • Outcome evidence (incidents, reverts, customer pain)
  • When to use: When evaluating the quality and trustworthiness of AI-generated code and features.

Token Optimization Operating Model

  • How it works: A three-pillar solution framework for optimizing token usage without starving innovation.
  • Components:
    • Guardrails (budget caps, quotas, usage policies, anomaly alerts)
    • Engineering discipline (agent step limits, prompt caching, context pruning, model routing, smaller models for routine tasks)
    • Learning loop (regular review of spikes, failures, wins, and reusable patterns)
  • When to use: When implementing organizational controls for AI engineering and token consumption.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

AI raises code-generation capacity faster than review/merge capacity, so the binding constraint relocates from authoring to code review and CI — the review pipeline, not the model, determines how much AI work an organization can absorb.

Why it matters

Capacity planning and architecture effort should be redirected to the absorption path (review, CI, merge queue) rather than to further generation throughput; adding generation capacity past this point produces queued, unreviewed work rather than value.

Generalization

Any agentic pipeline has a generation stage and an admission stage; optimizing the upstream stage without scaling the admission stage merely converts compute into backlog. Applies to document processing, data labeling, and content pipelines, not just code.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Trusted throughput makes human attention an explicit denominator alongside dollar cost: value is discounted by the review, validation, and maintainability burden it imposes, so an output that is cheap in tokens but expensive in human scrutiny scores poorly.

Why it matters

Gives a metric that resists the failure mode where token-cheap, high-slop output looks like a win; makes attention cost a first-class term in system design rather than an externality.

Generalization

For any AI system with a human-in-the-loop, define the unit of value per unit of human attention, not per unit of inference. This reframes model/prompt choices as attention-cost decisions.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Position agents upstream of human decisions with narrow, checkable subtasks and structured outputs; reserve authority for the human or for a deterministic gate.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltyweak evidence

Token efficiency is a layered operating system of three distinct layers — guardrails (budget caps, usage limits, anomaly alerts), engineering discipline (agent step limits, prompt caching, context pruning, model routing), and a learning loop (reviewing spikes, failures, reusable patterns) — not a single optimization trick.

Why it matters

Splits cost control into enforcement, runtime design, and feedback layers with different owners and failure modes; identifies agent step limits as a runtime-level control rather than a prompt-level suggestion.

Generalization

Cost governance for agents needs both hard runtime caps and a retrospective loop; either alone leaks (caps without learning throttle legitimate work, learning without caps permits runaway loops).

[M]Mechanismlow noveltyweak evidence

Prompt caching imposes a layout constraint on prompt construction: stable content (system prompt) must occupy the prefix and varying content the suffix, so prompt assembly order becomes an engineering decision with cost consequences.

Why it matters

Context assembly order is not cosmetic; interleaving dynamic content early destroys cache reuse and silently multiplies cost in long-running agents.

Generalization

In any repeated-context system (agents, RAG, chat), stable-prefix/dynamic-suffix discipline plus context pruning should be a structural invariant of the prompt builder.

Article ideas

4

Your Model Isn't the Bottleneck — Your Merge Queue Is

AI raises generation capacity faster than review and merge capacity, so the effective capacity of an AI engineering organization is set by its admission path. Teams that keep adding generation throughput past that point are buying backlog, not value; the correct response is to instrument and widen review and CI, never to batch work into larger PRs.

Angle

Capacity-planning argument that treats review and CI as in-scope subsystems of the AI system, using the larger-PR workaround as the counterexample that trades throughput for review quality.

Source video ↗

Smoke Detectors, Not Leaderboards: The Display Layer Is the Incentive Layer

Ranking any internal AI metric converts a diagnostic signal into a target. The same token telemetry, presented as anomaly alerts, supports adoption and cost control; presented as a leaderboard, it manufactures score chasing decoupled from value — severe enough that such systems have been shut down.

Angle

Measurement-design argument: the rendering of a metric is part of system incentive design, and sequencing matters — change-enablement dashboards before enforcement guardrails.

Source video ↗

Price the Human Attention, Not the Tokens

Token-cheap output is not cheap. Once value is normalized by the review, validation, and maintenance burden it imposes, high-volume low-trust output scores worse than modest output a human can verify — which makes attention cost a first-class term in model, prompt, and harness decisions rather than an externality.

Angle

Reframes AI ROI from cost-per-inference to value-per-unit-of-human-attention, with review-first-pass design as the concrete engineering consequence.

Source video ↗

Adopt First, Then Optimize: Why Early Austerity Breaks the Data You Need

Cost controls deployed during ramp-up distort the very usage signal you would later optimize against, and adoption regressions are harder to reverse than spend overruns. Instrumentation phases should be separated declaratively: change-enablement telemetry first, enforcement guardrails once usage stabilizes.

Angle

Sequencing argument against premature budget caps, illustrated by the shift from hand-authoring code to reviewing AI-generated code.

Project ideas

Advisory Review Agent A/B

gatehouse

An advisory-only AI first pass producing structured diff summaries, risk class, and missing-test flags reduces median review latency and post-merge defect escape rate on matched diffs; granting the same model approve authority improves neither and increases escapes.

Proof of concept

Shadow-mode review agent on one repository emitting structured output with no verdict, rendered as an advisory comment; PR-level randomization on/off with matched-diff stratification by risk class.

Measurement

Median time-to-first-human-comment and time-to-merge, reviewer minutes per PR, revert rate, and escaped defects at 30 days, comparing advisory, authoritative, and no-AI arms.

Source video ↗

Trusted Throughput Scorecard

movement-lab

A composite of PR complexity, review latency, revert/rework rate, and validation evidence predicts customer-visible outcomes (escaped defects, feature retention) better than PR count or LOC, and is uncorrelated with token spend.

Proof of concept

Backfill two quarters of repo history per team; compute candidate metrics; fit them against defect-escape and rework outcomes out of sample; publish as an alerting-only view with no cross-team ranking.

Measurement

Out-of-sample predictive lift versus the PR-count baseline, correlation with token spend (target near zero), and cross-team stability of the score's ranking.

Source video ↗

CI Trust Gate Threshold Calibration

beyond-evals

Per-risk-class acceptance thresholds over assurance evidence can pass a defined subset of low-risk AI-authored changes without human review while holding defect escape rate at or below the human-reviewed baseline.

Proof of concept

Offline replay of candidate thresholds against historical AI-authored PRs, comparing gate decisions with what human reviewers did and with observed post-merge defect outcomes.

Measurement

Defect escape rate per risk class versus the human-review baseline, share of PRs eligible for auto-merge, and false-approve rate on replayed history.

Source video ↗

Step-Cap Calibrator

new

Per-task-class step and dollar caps derived from the tail of observed cost distributions bound runaway agent loops while truncating under 2% of legitimately long successful runs.

Proof of concept

Instrument agent runs with step counts, token cost, and terminal status; cluster by task class; derive caps at a chosen tail percentile; replay sessions with caps enforced at the harness layer and early-abort heuristics enabled.

Measurement

Share of successful runs truncated by the cap, runaway-loop incident count and spend before versus after enforcement, and per-class cost variance reduction.

Before

After

Review/CI positioned as an in-scope subsystem of the AI system, with AI first-pass review and a CI trust gate designed as first-class components.

Consequence

Investment and instrumentation shift to pipeline throughput and flaky-test elimination; generation-side features get deprioritized once the admission path saturates.
