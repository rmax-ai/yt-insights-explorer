Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Edward Hughes
  • Channel: Machine Learning Street Talk
  • Main topic: Training AI agents to replicate scientific research and achieve open-endedness
  • Purpose: To explain the technical and philosophical foundations of training AI scientist systems for open-ended scientific discovery and paper replication. Edward Hughes, co-founder and chief scientist at Inherent, discusses the transition from individual AI intelligence to collective intelligence and the design of AI scientist systems. He covers the Faraday paper on training AI scientists to replicate machine learning and AI-for-science research papers using coding agents, GRPO, and automated rubric-based judges. The conversation explores open-endedness, the limits of optimization, evolutionary algorithms, and the importance of open-ended task spaces and evaluation in building autonomous scientific discovery agents.

Key Points

Faraday Agent Architecture

  • Explanation: Faraday uses a Qwen 3.6-27B base model combined with Codex as a tool, optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with rubric-based judging.
  • Evidence: Performance evaluation charts on ML and AI-for-science held-out tasks.
  • Practical implication: Enables open-weight models to compete with or beat proprietary frontier models on complex scientific tasks.

The Role of Rubrics in Evaluation

  • Explanation: Instead of relying on single binary rewards or simple test cases, Faraday uses per-task rubric generators and LLM judges to assess visual fidelity, claim reproduction, implementation fidelity, experimental depth, and scientific integrity.
  • Evidence: Figure 3 showing higher human agreement and lower noise with rubric-based judging.
  • Practical implication: Reduces reward hacking and provides robust multi-dimensional evaluation for non-verifiable agent tasks.

Turn-Level Credit Assignment

  • Explanation: Assigning reward credit at the individual turn level rather than just the final rollout prevents credit assignment collapse in long-horizon RL tasks.
  • Evidence: Ablation plots showing stability improvements when turn-level credit assignment is used.
  • Practical implication: Stabilizes training for multi-turn coding and scientific experimentation agents.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Faraday Replication Pipeline

  • How it works: Generates replication tasks from published papers, executes them using a coding agent in a container, and evaluates outputs using rubric-based judges.
  • Components:
    • Replica task space
    • Faraday policy with Codex tool
    • Per-task rubric generator
    • Rubric-based judge
    • GRPO optimizer
  • When to use: When training AI agents to execute complex, multi-step scientific paper replication tasks.

Open-Endedness Definition

  • How it works: Defines open-endedness as a system's ability to continually generate artifacts that are both novel and learnable to an observer.
  • Components:
    • System producing artifacts
    • Observer evaluating novelty
    • Observer evaluating learnability
  • When to use: When assessing whether an AI system is capable of unbounded cumulative discovery.

Actionable Takeaways

  • Immediate:
    • Rubric-based judges significantly reduce noise and hacking compared to binary verification.
    • Turn-level credit assignment is essential for stabilizing RL training on multi-turn coding agents.

  • Strategic:
    • Open-endedness and discovery are more fundamental to AGI than static optimization against fixed benchmarks.

Long-horizon multi-turn agent RL collapses under rollout-level credit assignment; assigning reward credit at the individual turn level is what stabilizes training for coding and scientific experimentation agents.

Why it matters

If credit must be resolved per turn rather than per episode, the reward pipeline and trace/logging infrastructure must capture and attribute per-turn signals, which changes how rollouts are stored, replayed, and optimized.

Generalization

Applies to any long-horizon agentic RL loop (coding agents, tool-use chains, multi-step research agents) where sparse terminal reward would otherwise be diluted across many turns.

Deep dives

5

Mechanism of turn-level credit assignment in long-horizon agent RL

Research question

What specifically does turn-level credit assignment change — gradient variance across the trajectory, reward density per step, or optimizer dynamics — such that credit assignment collapse is prevented, and over what horizon lengths does the effect hold?

Why

If the fix is variance reduction, the requirement is a step-aware advantage estimator; if it is reward density, the requirement is a per-turn reward model or rubric. Those lead to different infrastructure: step-indexed traces and replay buffers versus a dense reward scorer. Without isolating the mechanism, teams will copy the surface recipe (GRPO plus turns) without the component that actually matters.

Source video ↗

Rubric judges under iterated optimization pressure

Research question

Do rubric-based LLM judges remain discriminative as the policy is trained against them, or does policy improvement toward the rubric surface cause human agreement to decay over training steps?

Why

The claim that rubric judging reduces reward hacking is measured on an untrained or lightly trained policy. If human agreement decays under iterated pressure, then rubric quality is not a one-time artifact but a continuously maintained, adversarially contested component, and any training pipeline built on rubric rewards needs a rubric-refresh loop with its own cadence and cost.

Source video ↗

Transfer from paper replication to novel problem generation

Research question

Does training an agent to replicate published papers improve its ability to generate genuinely new hypotheses or new problem formulations, or does replication skill only demonstrate mastery of existing literature?

Why

The entire value proposition of an AI scientist rests on this transfer. If replication performance is orthogonal to discovery, then replication benchmarks are a proxy metric that can be maximized indefinitely without producing scientific output — a measurement trap for anyone funding or building on the replication task space.

Source video ↗

Operationalizing observer-dependent novelty into a non-collapsing benchmark

Research question

Can novelty-plus-learnability relative to a named observer model be measured in a way that does not collapse into a fixed scalar objective the system can optimize against?

Why

The definition is testable in principle but brittle in practice: any measurable novelty score becomes an objective once a policy trains against it. Resolving how to specify the observer and rotate or hold it out determines whether open-endedness can be an evaluation axis at all, or whether it must remain descriptive.

Source video ↗

Harness rigidity as a measured generalization tax

Research question

Can the guidance-versus-generalization tradeoff of hand-authored scaffolding be quantified per constraint, such that each prompt, tool schema, or state-machine edge has a measurable effect on out-of-distribution task performance?

Why

Right now the tradeoff is stated qualitatively, which makes harness design a matter of taste. If constraint count or constraint type can be regressed against held-out task performance, harness engineering becomes an ablation discipline with a budget rather than an art, and demo-tuned harnesses become detectable before they cap the policy.

The Faraday result reframes the build-versus-buy decision: on narrow, deeply-specified tasks, investment in harness design and reward shaping dominates investment in base-model scale, and the right comparison is not model versus model but system versus system.

Angle

Your Evaluator Is a Pipeline Component Now

When evaluation is generated per task rather than fixed in advance, rubric quality becomes a first-class engineering surface with its own latency, cost, and failure modes, and treating evaluation as static infrastructure guarantees that reward hacking is discovered late and expensively.

Angle

Argue that the rubric generator plus judge pattern is a reusable abstraction for every domain without unit tests, then detail the operational consequences: per-task artifact production, judge drift monitoring, and rubric-refresh cadence.

Optimization Cannot Discover the Problem It Is Optimizing

Every system that claims open-endedness while training against a fixed objective is optimizing inside a known rule set, and constraints rather than objectives are the workable substitute for specifying search structure without specifying the target.

Angle

Use AlphaGo's fixed rule set as the limiting case, then press on the practical question the argument avoids: how constraint-based search spaces are constructed and how one detects that an 'open-ended' loop has quietly become an objective maximizer.

Project ideas

4

TurnCreditBench

movement-lab

Step-aware credit assignment reduces gradient variance and prevents training collapse relative to rollout-level credit, and the size of the advantage grows monotonically with task horizon length.

Proof of concept

Train the same policy on identical long-horizon agentic traces under three credit schemes — rollout-level, turn-level, and an intermediate discounted scheme — sweeping horizon length from short to long while logging per-step advantage statistics, gradient variance, and held-out task success.

Measurement

Gradient variance per update, collapse incidence (success rate falling to baseline mid-run), and final held-out task success as a function of horizon length across the three schemes.

Source video ↗

RubricRot

beyond-evals

LLM judge agreement with human raters decays measurably over successive rounds of policy training against rubric rewards, and the decay rate depends on rubric granularity.

Proof of concept

Run iterated training rounds where a policy optimizes against a fixed generated rubric while periodically re-scoring a frozen sample of trajectories with human raters and with freshly regenerated rubrics; track both agreement curves against training step.

Measurement

Human-judge agreement coefficient per training round, gap between fixed-rubric and regenerated-rubric scores on the same trajectories, and steps until the gap exceeds a pre-registered threshold.

Source video ↗

RubricGen Service

beyond-evals

Runtime-generated per-task multi-dimensional rubrics, scored by an LLM judge, correlate with human assessment better and are less exploitable than a single binary outcome reward on non-verifiable agent tasks.

Proof of concept

Build a pipeline that ingests an agent trace and a task description, emits a task-specific rubric across the five dimensions used in the Faraday configuration, scores the trace, and exposes rubric provenance and versioning so downstream training can pin or rotate rubric revisions.

Measurement

Correlation with human scores versus a binary-reward baseline, rank correlation under adversarial trajectories written to satisfy the rubric without doing the task, and per-rubric generation cost and latency.

Source video ↗

Replication-to-Discovery Transfer Suite

new

Replication performance on held-out papers does not predict performance on tasks requiring a novel hypothesis or a new problem formulation; the two capabilities are measurably dissociated.

Proof of concept

Take agents at several checkpoints of replication training and evaluate each on a paired task set: a replication task and a matched novel-problem-generation task drawn from the same subfield, scoring both with the same rubric dimensions plus a novelty assessment by a held-out observer model.

Measurement

Rank correlation between replication score and novel-generation score across checkpoints and subfields; evidence of dissociation where replication improves while novel generation stays flat or regresses.

Source video ↗

Architectural implications

3

Evaluation is generated per task rather than fixed in advance.

Before

Evaluation is a static benchmark or unit-test suite written by humans before the agent runs.

After

A rubric generator produces task-specific criteria at runtime, and an LLM judge scores the trajectory against them.

Consequence

Evaluation becomes a pipeline component with its own quality, latency, and cost profile, and rubric quality becomes a tunable lever for reducing reward hacking.

Source video ↗

Reward attribution moves from the rollout boundary into the trajectory.

Before

One terminal reward per episode, assigned at the end of the rollout.

After

Per-turn credit assigned across the trajectory, requiring traces to carry per-step reward signals.

Consequence

Rollout storage, replay, and optimizer integration must be step-aware; training stability depends on this plumbing.

Source video ↗

Model capability is decomposed into a policy plus an external tool rather than absorbed into weights.

Before

Frontier capability assumed to come from a larger proprietary base model.

After

A smaller open-weight policy calls an external coding agent as a tool and is tuned on task-specific reward.

Consequence

Enables open-weight deployment and fine-tuning control, and shifts the optimization target from scale to harness and reward design.
