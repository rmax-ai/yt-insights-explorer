Overview

  • Speaker: Fei Fei Li
  • Channel: Asian American Scholar Forum
  • Main topic: Artificial Intelligence, World Models, Spatial & Physical Intelligence, and Human Agency
  • Purpose: To provide deep insights into the evolution of AI, the philosophical and technical challenges of building spatial and physical world models, and the personal and societal responsibilities of scientists and entrepreneurs. A keynote fireside chat at the 2026 Pioneer Symposium Pioneer Medal Ceremony featuring Dr. Fei-Fei Li, moderated by Richard Lui and introduced by Kai Li. Dr. Li discusses her journey in AI from ImageNet to World Labs, the shift from language intelligence to spatial and physical intelligence, the messiness of AI intersecting with human civilization, the importance of data versus model development, and maintaining human agency and curiosity in scientific pursuits.

Topic Map

The Messy Middle of AI and Humanity

  • Explanation: Dr. Li discusses how AI, as a civilizational technology, is a double-edged sword that intersects directly with the messy reality of human life and society.
  • Key claims:
    • AI is the messy middle between powerful technology and messy humanity.
    • Scientists, scholars, and entrepreneurs have a societal responsibility to communicate and guide technology responsibly.
  • Examples:
    • Caring for elderly parents and sick patients as a real-world physical toll.
  • Terminology:

From Language Intelligence to Spatial and Physical Intelligence

  • Explanation: Evolutionary history shows that perception and physical interaction predate language; thus, AI must move beyond text into spatial and physical world models.
  • Key claims:
    • Most of evolutionary intelligence in animals and humans is pre-verbal and rooted in spatial/physical interaction.
    • Pushing AI beyond language into the 3D physical world is the next scientific frontier.
  • Examples:
    • Animal navigation and manipulation of the physical world prior to language.
  • Terminology:
    • spatial intelligence
    • physical intelligence
    • pre-verbal intelligence
  • Why it matters: Defines the core technical mission of World Labs and the next generation of foundational AI research.

The Three Tiers of World Models

  • Explanation: Dr. Li proposes a functional taxonomy of world models consisting of rendering, simulation, and planning.
  • Key claims:
    • Rendering shows what the world appears to be (pixels, videos).
    • Simulation captures the physical and dynamic reality of the world (physics, interaction).
    • Planning predicts actions and future steps based on the world model.
  • Examples:
    • Video generation models rendering pixels vs. simulating how a water bottle moves when poured.
  • Terminology:
    • rendering
    • simulation
    • planning
    • functional taxonomy
  • Why it matters: Provides a rigorous framework for evaluating the depth and capabilities of different AI architectures.

The Challenge of Data in Physical AI

  • Explanation: Unlike language modeling which benefits from abundant internet text, spatial and physical intelligence suffers from a scarcity of structured multi-modal training data.
  • Key claims:
    • Data remains vastly underappreciated and underfunded in AI relative to compute and models.
    • Physics, geometry, and structure do not come explicitly packaged with standard video data.
  • Examples:
    • Training self-driving cars vs. training language models.
  • Terminology:
    • data scarcity
    • spatial data
    • latent representation
  • Why it matters: Highlights the primary bottleneck for advancing embodied and physical AI systems.

Preserving Human Agency

  • Key claims:

  • Examples:

Core insights

5
[O]Mental Modelhigh noveltymoderate evidence

World models should be evaluated as a three-tier capability ladder rather than as one undifferentiated category: rendering (what the world looks like at the pixel/video level), simulation (the physical and dynamic reality — physics, interaction), and planning (predicting actions and future steps from the model). Each tier subsumes but is strictly deeper than the prior one.

Why it matters

It gives engineers a concrete evaluation axis that separates surface-level generative plausibility from genuine physical grounding. A system can score well on tier 1 (rendering) while failing tier 2 (simulation), so benchmarks and acceptance criteria must be defined per tier rather than aggregated into a single 'quality' score.

Generalization

Any generative or predictive system that claims to model a domain can be audited by asking which tier it actually operates at: does it reproduce appearance, reproduce dynamics, or select actions? This applies to video models, robotics policies, and simulation environments alike.

[~]Tradeoffmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Physical structure is not carried explicitly by the data modality: video shows appearance but does not package physics, geometry, or structure. The supervision signal must therefore be inferred as latent representation, making data collection and sensor methodology — not model or compute scaling — the binding constraint for physical AI.

Why it matters

This inverts the default investment priority. If the constraint is the existence and structure of the data rather than the capacity of the model, then teams optimizing model size or compute budget are optimizing the wrong variable, and effort should shift toward sensor development and data collection methodology.

Generalization

Whenever a learning target (causal structure, physical law, intent) is not explicitly encoded in the raw signal, the bottleneck migrates from modeling capacity to instrumentation and labeling design. The same pattern applies to logs, traces, and telemetry in production systems.

[>]Predictionmedium noveltymoderate evidence

The frontier claim is evolutionary rather than rhetorical: most animal and human intelligence is pre-verbal and rooted in spatial/physical interaction, so language-only systems cover a narrow slice of intelligence. The argument implies language models are not a sufficient substrate for spatial and physical capability, and that 3D representation is a distinct modality with its own research stack.

Why it matters

If spatial and physical intelligence is a separate substrate rather than a downstream application of language models, then agent architectures that route all world understanding through text tokens will hit a structural ceiling for embodied tasks. Representation and data layers must be designed for 3D from the start rather than bolted on as tool calls.

Generalization

When a capability is claimed to be a distinct substrate rather than a feature, it usually implies separate data, separate representations, and separate evaluation infrastructure — a useful signal for deciding whether to extend an existing stack or build a parallel one.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltyweak evidence

The car-brake analogy reframes safety as a systemic and domain-calibrated property: an acceptable failure rate in one domain (a component failing intermittently) is catastrophic in another (brakes), and trust depends on societal guardrails rather than on any single component's reliability. Reliability targets are therefore a function of the deployment context, not a universal number.

Why it matters

It supplies a concrete design rule: define the domain's failure tolerance first, then derive architecture, redundancy, and guardrail requirements from it. Systems whose failure modes are unrecoverable need structural safeguards rather than probabilistic confidence thresholds.

Generalization

Failure budgets should be derived per deployment context, and safety mechanisms should be placed at the system boundary (guardrails, oversight, escalation paths) rather than assumed to emerge from component-level accuracy.

Source video ↗
[P]Practicemedium noveltyweak evidence

Data-centric research programs can be structurally rejected by algorithm-centric peer review before their value is recognized, because the review culture presumes where high-status work should be done. This is an institutional failure mode, not a purely scientific one.

Why it matters

For teams choosing where to invest, it means the absence of established funding or publication consensus is not evidence that a data-centric direction is wrong. It also warns that internal review processes can systematically filter out the data work that later becomes the bottleneck.

Generalization

Evaluation and funding pipelines inherit the assumptions of the era in which they were built; when the binding constraint shifts (from algorithms to data, or from models to harnesses), the review apparatus lags and rejects work that is ahead of it.

Deep dives

4

Operationalizing the rendering-vs-simulation boundary as a measurable criterion

Research question

What behavioral test reliably separates a model that reproduces physical dynamics from one that only reproduces appearance, and can that test be standardized into a benchmark with ground-truth dynamics as the reference?

Why

The three-tier taxonomy is only actionable if the rendering/simulation boundary can be probed rather than asserted. Without an intervention-based test, systems that merely render pixels will be accepted as systems that simulate, and downstream planning claims built on them collapse under contact, pouring, or conservation constraints.

Source video ↗

Latent recoverability of physical priors from appearance-only data

Research question

What fraction of physics, geometry, and structure can be recovered as latent representation from appearance-only video, versus only appearing when explicit geometric or physical supervision is supplied?

Why

This determines whether the field's bottleneck is model capacity or instrumentation. If priors are not recoverable from video alone, then sensor suites, annotation methodology, and dataset lifecycle management become first-class engineering surfaces with their own budget and ownership rather than downstream of model work.

Native 3D representation versus text-mediated world understanding in agent architectures

Research question

Do embodied agents that route world understanding through text tokens hit a structural ceiling on spatial and physical tasks that agents with native 3D representations do not?

Why

If spatial and physical intelligence is a separate substrate rather than a downstream application of language models, the abstraction boundary in agent stacks must move: representation and data layers designed for 3D from the start, not bolt-on tool calls. This is a testable claim about where systems fail, not a philosophical preference.

Context-calibrated failure budgets and structural guardrails for embodied systems

Research question

How should failure-rate budgets be derived per deployment context when failure modes are unrecoverable, and when do probabilistic confidence thresholds need to be replaced by structural safeguards at the system boundary?

Why

The brake analogy implies that reliability targets are a function of deployment context, not a universal number. That converts safety from a component-accuracy property into a system-design requirement: redundancy, oversight, and escalation paths must be specified before architecture is chosen for safety-adjacent physical deployments.

Your Video Model Doesn't Know How Water Pours

Single-score generative quality metrics are structurally misleading for world models: perceptual fidelity is a rendering-tier metric that can be maximized while physical dynamics are violated, so evaluation harnesses must stratify rendering, simulation, and planning and report them separately, with dynamics-under-intervention probes as the reference test.

Angle

Benchmark design critique grounded in the water-bottle example, arguing that the current eval stack rewards the shallowest tier and thereby mismeasures progress.

Project ideas

4

Pour-Bench

beyond-evals

Models that score highest on perceptual-similarity metrics for video generation will systematically fail on intervention probes that require predicting the outcome of physical actions (pouring, contact, containment), and the gap between rendering rank and dynamics rank will be large and reproducible.

Proof of concept

Build a small set of recorded physical interactions with ground-truth outcome state (fluid level, object position after contact), then query video/world models to predict the post-intervention outcome and compare against ground truth rather than against pixel similarity.

Measurement

Rank correlation between models' perceptual-fidelity scores and their dynamics-prediction error; and the fraction of trials where a high-rendering-score model produces a physically impossible outcome.

Source video ↗

Appearance-to-Dynamics Ablation

new

Physical priors are only partially recoverable from appearance-only video: models trained solely on appearance data will generalize poorly to unseen physical interactions compared with models given explicit geometric or physical supervision, and the size of that generalization gap quantifies how much supervision must come from instrumentation.

Proof of concept

Train matched models on the same scenes with and without explicit geometric/physical supervision, hold out interaction types, and test transfer to the held-out interactions under identical compute.

Measurement

Held-out interaction prediction error difference between appearance-only and supervision-augmented models, reported at fixed compute budget.

Source video ↗

Embodied Failure-Budget Classifier

gatehouse

Deployment contexts can be partitioned into a small set of failure-severity classes such that unrecoverable failure modes reliably predict the need for structural guardrails rather than probabilistic confidence thresholds, and this classification is stable across tasks.

Proof of concept

Enumerate failure modes for a set of embodied tasks, label each as recoverable or unrecoverable, and check whether the recoverable/unrecoverable split predicts which systems in production actually needed boundary-level safeguards.

Measurement

Agreement rate between the classifier's guardrail recommendation and the safeguards retrospectively required, plus the count of unrecoverable failures that probabilistic thresholds failed to catch.

Source video ↗

Tier-Stratified Agent Evaluation Harness

movement-lab

Agents whose world understanding is mediated entirely through text tokens will underperform agents with native 3D representations on spatial and physical tasks, even when both are given the same task budget and tool access.

Proof of concept

Implement two agent variants over the same task suite (text-mediated tool calls vs. native 3D state interface), run identical spatial/physical tasks, and compare success and failure-mode distributions.

Measurement

Task success rate by variant and the proportion of failures attributable to representation loss versus planning error.

Video and world-model outputs are currently judged largely on perceptual fidelity, which is a rendering-tier metric and says nothing about physical correctness.

Before

After

Consequence

New benchmarks and harnesses are needed that probe dynamics (e.g. object interaction, conservation, contact) rather than pixel similarity, otherwise systems that merely render will be mistaken for systems that simulate.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Perceptual fidelity vs. physical fidelity in generative models

Benefit

Pixel-level generation produces visually convincing, high-coverage outputs that are easy to produce and easy to demo.

Cost or risk

Convincing rendering can mask the absence of simulation — a model may depict a bottle without modeling how water actually pours, producing outputs that look correct but violate dynamics when used for downstream action.

Source video ↗

Compute/model investment vs. data investment

Benefit

Scaling models and compute yields measurable capability gains and is straightforward to fund and benchmark.

Cost or risk

Data remains underappreciated and underfunded, so capacity can be built that cannot be trained on adequate physical/spatial data — a mismatch between built capacity and available supervision.

Source video ↗

Technological determinism vs. human agency

Benefit

Embracing the prevailing narrative of inevitable AI-driven change drives urgency, investment, and rapid iteration.

Cost or risk

Dystopian framing and hype can erode human judgment and critical thinking; tools may displace rather than empower creativity and productivity, and teams may abandon promising directions under narrative pressure.

Source video ↗

Industry-standard review norms vs. unconventional research directions

Benefit

Algorithm-centric review conventions maintain methodological rigor and comparability within the established paradigm.

Cost or risk

The same conventions can reject data-centric work that later becomes the field's bottleneck, delaying research directions that do not fit the prevailing assumption about where senior work belongs.
