Overview

  • Speaker: Roland Gavrilescu
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Auto-research and autonomous agent loops in AI engineering
  • Purpose: To present a roadmap and framework for building, tuning, and scaling autonomous AI agent systems through introspection recipes, OODA loops, and feedback-driven distillation. Roland Gavrilescu from Introspection discusses the evolution of AI engineering from raw models to harnesses and fully autonomous execution loops. He introduces a blueprint for 2026 emphasizing that 'the loop is the product,' system distillation as the key moat, and optimizing valued work per watt.

Key Points

The Loop is the Product

  • Explanation: Moving beyond static model prompting or fixed harnesses to autonomous long-running execution loops.
  • Evidence: Clawbot negotiating car prices and matching dealer inventory autonomously.
  • Practical implication: Engineers must build robust orchestration loops that incorporate feedback traces rather than just writing static code.

System Distillation as the Moat

  • Explanation: Capturing human maker taste, failure modes, and successful trajectories into reproducible configuration recipes.
  • Evidence: Distilling user frustration into memories/extensions and failure patterns into judges/evals.
  • Practical implication: Proprietary advantage comes from how effectively you refine and version your agent recipes over time.

Valued Work Per Watt

  • Explanation: Measuring success by the economic and computational efficiency of agent outputs relative to cost, tokens, and latency.
  • Evidence: Moving from base recipes to Pareto-optimal recipes through systematic calibration and A/B testing.
  • Practical implication: Scale requires ensuring that autonomous agent execution provides superior economic value compared to traditional alternatives.

Core insights

6
[A]Architecturemedium noveltymoderate evidence

The inner/outer loop split makes self-improvement an architectural boundary: the inner loop executes tasks with a recipe, while a separate outer loop analyzes artifacts, generates candidate recipe variants, runs evals, and promotes winners. Improvement lives outside the worker, not inside it.

Why it matters

It gives a concrete place to put optimization machinery (artifact analysis, candidate generation, eval gating, promotion) without contaminating the execution path or the harness the worker runs in. It also implies you need artifact/trace capture as a first-class product output, since the outer loop consumes it.

Generalization

Any system meant to improve over time needs an explicit separation between the execution loop and the meta-loop that mutates it, mediated by logged artifacts and an eval gate.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

The proposed distillation mapping is mechanical rather than ad hoc: repeated behaviors become prompts/skills, failure patterns become judges/evals, and user frustration becomes memories/extensions. Each class of observed signal has a designated target artifact type.

Why it matters

It turns vague 'learn from production' advice into a routing table for where feedback should land. That matters for triage design: without a mapping, teams either dump everything into the prompt or build evals for things that should be skills.

Generalization

Feedback loops need typed signal classes with assigned destination artifacts; the quality of the loop depends on the correctness of that routing, not on the volume of feedback collected.

[A]Architecturemedium noveltyweak evidence

Agent systems should be packaged as portable, git-versioned 'recipes' that bundle harness configuration together with human maker taste, and that are provider-agnostic and company-owned. The recipe, not the model or the prompt file, is the unit of versioning.

Why it matters

Provider-agnostic versioned recipes mean model swaps and harness changes are configuration changes rather than rewrites, and that the accumulated taste (evals, heuristics) is an asset that survives vendor churn. It reframes the repo's primary artifact.

Generalization

As models commoditize, the durable engineering artifact is the versioned, portable configuration-plus-evaluation bundle, not code bound to a single provider's interface.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Evals are framed as the encoding of qualitative human taste rather than generic accuracy, and experiments as the validator of that taste. The illustration is a sourcing agent whose value comes from applying specific taste filters to find candidates generic accuracy metrics would miss.

Why it matters

If taste is the differentiator, then eval design is product design and is the highest-leverage engineering work. It also implies most standard benchmarks are the wrong instrument for the actual value being delivered.

Generalization

Differentiated agent products require task-specific, taste-encoding evaluations; off-the-shelf accuracy metrics will select for the wrong behavior.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltyweak evidence

'Valued work per watt' is proposed as the scoring function: usefulness normalized against compute, latency, and risk, with recipe selection done as a Pareto-optimization over cost/tokens/latency rather than single-axis quality maximization.

Why it matters

It makes cost and risk first-class in the optimization target, which changes recipe selection from 'best eval score' to 'best frontier position.' That has direct consequences for model routing, loop depth, and where you spend tokens.

Generalization

Agent systems should be selected on a multi-objective efficiency frontier (utility per unit of compute, latency, and risk) rather than on capability alone.

Source video ↗
[O]Mental Modellow noveltyweak evidence

The OODA loop is repurposed as the worker's control structure, with the explicit input set being signals (traces, feedback, failures) and the output being verified work rather than a response.

Why it matters

Framing the worker's output as verified work rather than a message changes what the harness must include: verification and signal ingestion are inside the loop, not post-hoc.

Generalization

Long-running agent loops should be modeled as observe/orient/decide/act cycles whose contract is verified output, not text generation.

Deep dives

4

Artifact-to-diff compilers: how an outer loop derives candidate recipe edits from traces

Research question

Given a corpus of failure traces and successful trajectories, what mapping from observed pattern to structured recipe edit (add skill, add judge, change prompt, change tool policy) yields higher eval-delta per candidate than free-form prompt mutation at equal token budget?

Why

The summary asserts the outer loop 'generates candidate improvements' but gives no generation mechanism. Without a typed edit vocabulary, the outer loop degrades into random prompt mutation, which is both expensive and uninterpretable. A compiler also makes each promotion auditable as a diff.

Source video ↗

Goodhart behavior of taste-encoding eval suites under automated promotion

Research question

Over repeated outer-loop promotion cycles gated by taste-encoding judges, does correlation between eval delta and held-out human preference decay, and at what cycle count?

Why

If evals encode qualitative taste and also serve as the promotion gate, the same artifact is both the objective and the reward signal. That is the classic setup for proxy drift. The claim that taste is codifiable is asserted with no reported validity evidence.

Source video ↗

Trace-level accounting for valued work per watt

Research question

Can compute, latency, and incident risk be attributed per verified output in a trace such that Pareto-frontier recipe selection produces a different winner than quality-only ranking?

Why

The scoring function is named but never operationalized; risk is listed as a term with no measurement. If the metric cannot be computed from existing traces, 'Pareto-optimal recipes' is unfalsifiable, and recipe selection silently collapses back to best eval score.

Source video ↗

Typed feedback routing at ingestion versus single-channel accumulation

Research question

Does classifying incoming signals into behavior/failure/frustration classes and routing each to a distinct destination artifact (prompt, judge, memory) beat dumping all signals into one prompt-plus-memory store, per unit of engineering effort?

Why

The routing table is the most novel element in the talk and the most concretely testable. It also has an immediate cost: it requires a classification step at ingestion and separate pipelines per artifact type. Whether that cost buys reliability is unknown.

Article ideas

4

Your Agent's Moat Is a Diff, Not a Prompt

The outer loop is only worth building if candidate improvements arrive as structured, reviewable recipe edits derived from trace patterns; otherwise automated self-improvement is random mutation with an eval bill, and the moat claim collapses.

Angle

Take the distillation routing table seriously as an edit grammar, then show what breaks when you skip the compilation step: prompt sprawl, eval bloat, and unauditable promotions.

Source video ↗

Evals Encode Taste, So Your Promotion Gate Is Optimizing a Proxy

When the same eval suite both defines taste and gates automatic promotion, the loop will learn the suite rather than the taste; teams need held-out human preference checks and drift detection before they let the outer loop push to production.

Angle

Argue against the unexamined assumption that taste is codifiable, then specify concrete instruments: preference holdouts, judge-suite adversarial tests, and decay curves across promotion cycles.

Source video ↗

Valued Work Per Watt Is a Slogan Until You Can Bill It to a Trace

Multi-objective recipe selection is only real if compute, latency, and incident risk are attributed per verified output; without that accounting, Pareto optimization is quality ranking wearing a cost costume.

Angle

Show what an attribution scheme must contain (verified-output boundary, incident-to-trace linkage, latency accounting at loop depth), then argue that selection should emit a frontier of deployments rather than one winner.

Source video ↗

Models Are Commodities Is a Testable Claim, Not a Strategy

The commodity-model thesis should be settled by ablation, not assertion: swap models with the recipe fixed and swap recipes with the model fixed, then compare output-quality variance; the allocation rule between recipe and model follows from where variance actually lives.

Angle

Reframe a strategic slogan as an experiment design, and note that provider-agnostic recipes carry real abstraction cost that the portability benefit must repay.

Project ideas

4

Diff Compiler for Recipe Distillation

beyond-evals

A typed artifact-to-edit compiler (pattern class -> skill addition, judge addition, prompt change, tool-policy change) produces more eval-passing promotions per candidate than free-form prompt rewriting, at equal token spend.

Proof of concept

Ingest a fixed trace corpus from a single task domain; implement three edit operators with rejection sampling against an existing eval suite; run 50 candidate generations under both the compiler and a free-form mutation baseline.

Measurement

Promotion rate per candidate generation, eval delta per 1k tokens of outer-loop spend, and fraction of promoted edits a human reviewer rates as coherent with the underlying trace pattern.

Source video ↗

Judge Drift Sentinel

gatehouse

Automated eval-gated promotion cycles cause measurable divergence between judge-suite score and held-out human preference within a small number of cycles, and a preference holdout detects it before judge score does.

Proof of concept

Build a promotion simulator that repeatedly selects recipe variants against a judge suite; maintain a frozen, never-optimized human-preference holdout; log both curves across 30 simulated promotion rounds.

Measurement

Correlation between judge delta and holdout delta per round, round index at which correlation drops below a pre-set threshold, and count of promotions a human rater reverses.

Source video ↗

Wattmeter: Trace-Level Efficiency Accounting

gatehouse

Attribute compute, latency, and incident risk per verified output changes which recipe variant is selected relative to quality-only ranking in a material share of comparisons.

Proof of concept

Instrument a small agent harness to emit per-trace cost, wall-clock, and incident tags linked to verified outputs; evaluate 6-10 recipe variants; compute both a quality-only ranking and a Pareto frontier over the efficiency terms.

Measurement

Share of variant comparisons where the quality-only winner differs from the selected frontier point, plus the reproducibility of the accounting across repeated runs on the same traces.

Source video ↗

Signal Router

movement-lab

Classifying feedback into behavior, failure, and frustration classes at ingestion and routing each to its designated artifact type yields higher reliability per engineering hour than single-channel accumulation into prompts and memory.

Proof of concept

Implement a lightweight classifier over a real trace/feedback set, wire three destination pipelines (prompt/skill, judge, memory/extension), and run both strategies for four weeks on the same task with an identical eval suite.

Measurement

Change in failure rate on previously observed failure classes, artifact count growth in prompts and judges (sprawl proxy), and engineering hours spent per net reliability gain.

Architectural implications

4

Improvement machinery is separated from task execution and mediated by logged artifacts

Before

Optimization logic and prompt/persona tweaks are entangled with the agent's runtime path, tuned in place by humans

After

A distinct outer loop ingests artifacts, proposes candidate recipes, runs evals, and promotes winners; the inner loop only executes

Consequence

Artifact and trace capture becomes a required system output rather than a debugging convenience, and promotion requires an eval gate to be trustworthy

Source video ↗

The versioned artifact shifts from code or a prompt file to a portable recipe bundling harness config plus taste

Before

Agent behavior is versioned as application code coupled to a specific model provider's API

After

Recipes are git-versioned, provider-agnostic, and owned internally, so model and harness changes are configuration diffs

Consequence

Model migration becomes a re-run of the eval suite against the existing recipe rather than a re-engineering project

Source video ↗

Feedback is routed to typed destinations rather than accumulated in one place

Before

All learnings are funneled into prompt edits or an undifferentiated memory store

After

Repeated behaviors go to prompts/skills, failures go to judges/evals, user frustration goes to memories/extensions

Consequence

Requires a signal-classification step at ingestion and separate pipelines per artifact type, but prevents eval bloat and prompt sprawl

Source video ↗

Recipe selection is a multi-objective frontier search rather than a single quality ranking

Before

Pick the configuration with the best eval score

After

Select Pareto-optimal recipes over compute, tokens, latency, and risk using calibration and A/B tests

Consequence

Requires instrumenting cost and latency at the recipe level, not just quality metrics, and produces a set of candidate deployments rather than one winner

Tradeoffs and failure modes

4

Outer-loop automation of recipe changes

Benefit

Continuous self-improvement with candidates generated and validated by evals rather than manual tuning

Cost or risk

Changes are gated by evals that encode taste; taste is qualitative and potentially unmeasurable, so automated promotion can optimize the proxy and drift from real value. The summary gives no reported evidence of this loop working in production.

Source video ↗

Aggressive artifact-driven distillation

Benefit

Captures maker taste, failure modes, and successful trajectories into reproducible configuration

Cost or risk

Requires continuous capture of user frustration and failure traces; no coverage of privacy, data retention, or what happens when distillation writes bad lessons into persistent memory

Source video ↗

Long-running autonomous execution loops

Benefit

Agents execute multi-step commercial workflows end-to-end, as in the car-purchase case

Cost or risk

Cost, latency, and risk grow with loop length and are only loosely bounded; 'valued work per watt' becomes harder to control as loops extend, and the failure surface of a long loop is much larger than a single call

Source video ↗

Provider-agnostic recipes as the moat

Benefit

Portability and ownership; accumulated taste survives vendor churn

Cost or risk

Abstraction overhead and lowest-common-denominator access to provider-specific features; the moat claim is asserted as a thesis, not demonstrated
