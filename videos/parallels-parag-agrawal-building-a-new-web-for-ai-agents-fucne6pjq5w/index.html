Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Parag Agrawal
  • Channel: Sequoia Capital
  • Main topic: Building agentic search and web infrastructure for AI agents
  • Purpose: To explain the vision behind Parallel Web Systems, the economics of agentic web search, and the fundamental differences between human-driven and agent-driven information retrieval. Parag Agrawal, former CEO of Twitter and founder of Parallel Web Systems, discusses the evolution of web search from human-centric browsers to agent-centric search engines. He explains why human click data is a bug for AI agents, how incremental indexing and search agent architecture enable cost-effective scale, and why incentive alignment between content owners, seekers, and AI models is crucial for the future of the internet.

Topic Map

Human Click Data vs. Agent Feedback

  • Explanation: Human feedback loops are based on latency-optimized, click-driven heuristics designed for lazy humans, whereas AI agents require objective agent feedback and compressed information.
  • Key claims:
    • Human click data is a bug for agentic search.
    • Agents need agent feedback, not human feedback.
    • Indexes built for human latency are suboptimal for agents.
  • Examples:
    • Skimming Google search results pages 1 to 7 out of laziness.
  • Terminology:
    • agent feedback
    • human click data
    • latency optimization
  • Why it matters: Redefines how search engines must be engineered when the primary consumers are autonomous software agents rather than human users.

The Problem of Web Search at Scale

  • Explanation: Traditional web search requires crawling trillions of pages, building massive indexes, and running expensive matching algorithms for every query.
  • Key claims:
    • Web search is a billion-to-billion matching problem.
    • Crawling the entire web upfront is extremely expensive.
    • Indexes act as latency optimizations for humans.
  • Examples:
    • Matching 1,000 tokens of query against a trillion web pages.
  • Terminology:
    • crawling
    • indexing
    • retrieval layer
    • ranking layer
  • Why it matters: Illustrates why traditional search infrastructure does not scale economically for high-frequency agentic workloads.

Parallel's Incremental Indexing Approach

  • Explanation: Instead of crawling everything upfront, Parallel builds search agents that retrieve relevant excerpts and bring them into the agent's context window incrementally.
  • Key claims:
    • Parallel started by building search agents before search products.
    • Incremental index building reduces upfront capital expenditure.
    • Agent-driven search relies on precise context window feeding.
  • Examples:
    • Insurance underwriting workflows and financial data enrichment.
  • Terminology:
    • search agent
    • incremental indexing
    • context window
  • Why it matters: Offers a viable economic path for startups to compete with tech giants in web indexing.

Incentive Alignment and Shapley Values

  • Explanation: Ensuring content owners get paid fairly when their data is used by AI agents, utilizing game-theoretic concepts like Shapley values.
  • Key claims:
    • Fixed-price contracts in AI inference do not drive long-term sustainability.
    • Incentive alignment maximizes participation of content owners and seekers.
    • Shapley values help distribute collaborative value mathematically.
  • Examples:
    • Comparing a banker reading a report vs. a retired dad reading financial data.
  • Terminology:
    • Shapley values
    • incentive alignment
    • differential pricing
    • game theory
  • Why it matters: Addresses the economic sustainability of the web in an era where AI models ingest content without driving traditional ad clicks.

Key Points

Agents require agentic search

  • Explanation: AI agents operating 1,000x more frequently than humans need search infrastructure optimized for token efficiency and programmatic precision rather than human browsing habits.
  • Evidence: Web traffic from AI is scaling rapidly, matching or exceeding human page reads in specific domains.
  • Practical implication: Developers building multi-agent systems must integrate search APIs designed specifically for programmatic agent consumption.

Incremental building over upfront capital expenditure

  • Explanation: By deploying search agents that fetch data on demand and incrementally build indexes, companies can avoid the prohibitive costs of crawling the entire web upfront.
  • Evidence: Parallel's journey from building search agents for specific workflows to scaling a web-wide index.
  • Practical implication: Startups can build competitive search infrastructure without multi-billion-dollar data center investments.

The collapse of traditional advertising business models

  • Explanation: Ad-supported web business models rely on human attention and click-throughs, which break down when AI agents consume content directly.
  • Evidence: Publishers signing licensing deals with AI labs because traditional ad revenue streams are threatened.
  • Practical implication: Content creators and platform architects must adopt differential pricing and incentive alignment frameworks.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Shapley Values for Content Valuation

  • How it works: A game-theoretic approach to determine how much individual contributors (content owners) add to a collaborative outcome (AI inference result) when the whole is greater than the sum of parts.
  • Components:
    • Collaborative coalitions
    • Marginal contribution calculation
    • Differential pricing based on data uniqueness
  • When to use: When distributing revenue or compensation among multiple content sources that feed an LLM inference pipeline.

Core insights

6
[O]Mental Modelhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Signals derived from human behavior (clicks, latency-driven skimming) are actively harmful as training/ranking signal for agent-facing search: human click data is treated as a bug, and the correct signal is 'agent feedback' — outcome/objective signals from the agent that consumes the results.

Why it matters

If you copy a human-facing retrieval stack into an agentic system, you inherit a reward signal optimized for the wrong consumer. The feedback loop, not the index, becomes the thing that must be redesigned.

Generalization

Any system where the proxy signal (user clicks, dwell time, thumbs) was optimized for a different consumer class than the one now driving traffic will silently misalign; instrument the loop for the actual consumer's objective.

[A]Architecturehigh noveltystrong evidence

Invert the classic search architecture: instead of crawling and indexing the whole web up front, deploy search agents that fetch relevant excerpts on demand and incrementally materialize them into the consuming agent's context window, building the index as a side effect of usage.

Why it matters

Turns a fixed multi-billion-dollar capex crawl into a usage-proportional opex, and makes the context window the effective boundary of the retrieval index — a materially different cost and quality curve for anyone building retrieval for agents.

Generalization

Lazy/on-demand materialization of a derived index (build it when queried, not before) is a viable strategy for any derived-artifact system where full precomputation is economically prohibitive.

[>]Predictionmedium noveltyweak evidence

When the request rate of a new consumer class exceeds the incumbent's by orders of magnitude, the incumbent's optimization target (latency, UX) must be replaced by the new class's target (throughput, token/cost efficiency, machine precision).

Agent feedback as a replacement for click signals in retrieval ranking

Research question

If the consuming agent's task outcome (success/failure, downstream tool-call correctness, answer acceptance) is used as the ranking reward instead of human clicks, does retrieval quality on agent tasks measurably diverge from click-trained rankers — and in which direction?

Why

The claim that 'human click data is a bug' is an assertion without an operational definition of the replacement signal. Until 'agent feedback' has a collection mechanism, a noise model, and a measurable effect on agent task success, every downstream architectural decision (evaluation harnesses, ranker training, context assembly) rests on an unvalidated premise.

Source video ↗

The crossover point where incrementally built indexes match full-web crawl quality

Research question

As a function of query volume, query skew, and corpus size, at what point does on-demand incremental index materialization reach parity (recall/precision) with a prebuilt full-crawl index, and at what cost per query?

Why

Incremental indexing is the load-bearing economic claim ('reduces upfront capital expenditure') that lets a startup compete with web-scale incumbents. If parity only arrives at volumes nobody reaches, the strategy is a cost deferral, not a substitute architecture. This is explicitly flagged as the source's own open question and has no published benchmark.

Source video ↗

Tractability and gameability of Shapley-based content attribution at corpus scale

Research question

Can sampled or truncated Shapley approximations produce stable, non-gameable per-source payments across realistic content-coalition sizes, or does the approximation error swamp the payment signal that content owners would need to trust?

Why

Attribution is the precondition for the content supply chain existing at all under agentic consumption. But the summary presents Shapley values as mathematical theory with no demonstrated tractability, and a payment scheme that can be gamed by strategically-duplicated or subsidized content is worse than a flat contract because it invites adversarial participation.

Measuring agent-vs-human traffic crossover with server-side classification

Research question

Across a broad domain panel, what fraction of page reads originates from AI agents, how is that traffic identifiable server-side, and how much does the measured number move depending on the classification method (user-agent, protocol signature, request shape)?

Why

The 1,000x frequency multiplier and the parity claim are the empirical foundation for the entire argument that infrastructure must be redesigned. Both are qualified to 'specific domains' with no data shown. Without a reproducible measurement method, the premise cannot be confirmed or falsified, and any capacity/cost model built on it is unfounded.

The mechanical breakdown of attention-mediated revenue under agentic consumption

Research question

For a given publisher, how much does agent-originated consumption of the same content reduce click revenue per unit of content served, and does a per-contribution licensing channel recover it at a rate that keeps the supply side producing?

Why

This is a systemic reliability risk, not a business-model curiosity: an agentic retrieval product that reads the web at scale degrades the substrate it depends on. Treating it as an incentive-channel design problem alongside the technical one is the only way to avoid building on a supply side that quietly exits.

Article ideas

4

Your Retrieval Stack Is Optimized for a Consumer That No Longer Exists

The most consequential bug in agentic search is not the index but the feedback loop: a ranker trained on human clicks is a ranker optimized for skimming behavior, and porting it into an agent-facing system import a reward signal that actively rewards the wrong thing. The fix is not a better crawler but an evaluation harness built around agent task outcomes.

Angle

Treat the feedback loop as the migration artifact, not the index. Show what concretely breaks when CTR-shaped metrics are the only labels you have, and what must be instrumented instead.

Source video ↗

Build the Index You Actually Query: Lazy Materialization as an Economic Strategy

Precomputing a derived artifact for the whole universe of possible inputs is a capex decision that only incumbents can afford; materializing it on demand and caching the byproduct of use is a legitimate substitute, not a degraded fallback — but only up to a coverage threshold nobody has measured. The honest version of this argument states the threshold as an open question, not a solved one.

Angle

Cost-curve framing: compare usage-proportional retrieval cost against amortized full-crawl cost as query volume and skew vary, and make the crossover explicit rather than assumed.

Source video ↗

Agents Read the Web for Free; Someone Still Has to Pay for It

Ad-supported content is a load-bearing dependency of every agentic retrieval product, and its failure under agent consumption is mechanical rather than a matter of publisher preference. Attribution and provenance are therefore runtime requirements of the retrieval pipeline, not finance-side concerns — a system that cannot account for per-source contribution cannot participate in the market that keeps its inputs alive.

Angle

Trace the broken chain (attention to clicks to revenue) and derive what the pipeline must carry: per-excerpt provenance, per-source contribution accounting, and a pricing channel designed alongside the technical one.

Source video ↗

Bootstrap the Loop Before You Build the Index

When the general-purpose artifact is economically unreachable at the start, the right first move is not a smaller crawl but a higher-value domain: insurance underwriting and financial data enrichment supply cheap expert ground truth, which is what makes an agent-feedback loop trainable before web-scale index coverage exists. Sequencing, not architecture, is the binding constraint for teams without incumbent capital.

Angle

Contrast the vertical-bootstrap path against the general-crawl path on feedback cost per labeled outcome, and show why ground-truth availability — not corpus size — gates early progress.

Project ideas

4

Agent-Outcome Ranker Harness

beyond-evals

A ranker trained and selected on agent task-outcome labels will beat a click-trained ranker on agent task success rate, while scoring worse on human-behavior proxy metrics — meaning the two objectives are measurably divergent rather than correlated.

Proof of concept

Stand up a dual-objective retrieval loop over a fixed document set: two rankers (one trained on click-style proxy labels, one on downstream task-outcome labels), a scripted agent consumer that executes a task using retrieved excerpts, and automatic success grading. Run both rankers against an identical held-out agent task suite.

Measurement

Delta in agent task success rate between rankers, plus the correlation coefficient between agent-outcome scores and click-proxy scores across the ranked lists. A near-zero or negative correlation confirms divergence; a high positive correlation falsifies the premise that human click data is a bug.

Source video ↗

Provenance Gate and Contribution Ledger

gatehouse

Attaching per-excerpt provenance metadata at retrieval time and computing sampled per-source marginal contribution is tractable at production query rates, and the resulting payments remain stable when an adversarial source injects near-duplicate content.

Proof of concept

Instrument a retrieval pipeline so every excerpt carries a signed source identifier that survives into the model context and output. Implement sampled/truncated Shapley approximation over the retrieved coalition per query, write contributions to an append-only ledger, then run a gaming experiment where a synthetic source floods near-duplicates and observe whether its payout inflates beyond its true marginal value.

Measurement

Approximation error versus exact Shapley on small coalitions, query-time overhead percentage attributable to provenance and attribution, and payout inflation factor for the adversarial source. Failure mode is a payout inflation factor materially above 1.0.

Source video ↗

Incremental-vs-Full Index Crossover Benchmark

new

Incremental, query-driven index materialization reaches quality parity with a prebuilt full index on a realistic query distribution before the cumulative retrieval cost of the incremental approach exceeds the amortized cost of the prebuilt index — i.e., there exists a usable volume window where incremental wins on both axes.

Proof of concept

Build a paired benchmark over the same corpus and query set: (a) a fully prebuilt index, (b) a search agent that fetches and materializes excerpts on demand and caches them. Replay a synthetic query stream with controlled skew, sweeping query volume upward, and record recall and precision against a gold set at each volume step, alongside cumulative cost per query.

Measurement

Recall/precision curves as a function of cumulative query volume for both arms, plus cumulative cost per query. The hypothesis is falsified if the incremental arm's quality curve stays below the prebuilt arm's across the entire range where its cost is lower — i.e., no parity window exists.

Source video ↗

Traffic Classification Panel

movement-lab

Agent traffic share is measurable server-side with low classification ambiguity, and the agent-to-human read ratio varies by more than an order of magnitude across domain categories — meaning a single global multiplier is a misleading basis for capacity planning.

Proof of concept

Deploy request-level classification (user-agent, protocol signature, request shape, session behavior) across a panel of instrumented domains spanning content categories, run the classifier alongside manual ground-truth labeling on a sample, and compute per-category agent-to-human read ratios.

Measurement

Classifier precision/recall against manually labeled ground truth, and the spread of agent-to-human read ratios across categories. A tight spread would falsify the cross-domain-variance hypothesis; a broad spread would falsify any single-multiplier planning model.

Architectural implications

3

Where the index lives and when it is built changes from a global precomputed artifact to a per-query, agent-driven materialization.

Before

Crawl the web up front, build one large index, run a retrieval + ranking layer per query; the index is a latency optimization for human users.

After

Search agents retrieve relevant excerpts on demand and incrementally feed the consuming agent's context window, building index coverage as a byproduct of use.

Consequence

Retrieval cost becomes proportional to usage rather than to corpus size, and context-window assembly becomes a first-class component that must be budgeted and evaluated.

Source video ↗

The ranking/feedback loop changes owner and signal type.

Before

Ranking models trained and evaluated on human click behavior.

After

Ranking driven by agent feedback — objective outcome signals from the programmatic consumer.

Consequence

New evaluation harnesses are required that can capture and score agent outcomes rather than clicks; offline metrics like CTR lose meaning.

Source video ↗

Content access becomes an economic gate rather than an open crawl.

Before

Open crawl + ad monetization; the publisher is paid indirectly by human attention.

After

Licensing deals and differential pricing keyed to the marginal value of each content source.

Consequence

Retrieval systems must carry provenance/attribution metadata per retrieved excerpt, since payment and permission depend on per-source contribution accounting.

Cost or risk

Unknown quality ceiling: there is no established point at which on-demand incremental coverage matches a full crawl for a given query.
