[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Intent and preference are best inferred from four independent channels — ask directly, listen, triangulate through the person's subordinates, and observe revealed behavior (where they spend time and money) — because stated goals and actual incentives diverge.

Why it matters

Most agent preference/reward models are built from stated instructions or a single interaction channel. Treating preference elicitation as multi-channel evidence aggregation (self-report + indirect report + revealed action) gives a concrete recipe for building goal-inference components and for detecting when an agent's stated objective contradicts its observed behavior.

Generalization

Any system that must model a principal's true objective — reward modeling, personalization, tool-selection policies — should fuse stated intent, third-party signals, and behavioral traces rather than trusting a single declared objective.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltyweak evidence

Perceived quality (likability) can outweigh measured performance for survival and support, meaning the metric you observe is a proxy that can be decoupled from the capability you care about.

Why it matters

Directly analogous to agent evaluation: leaderboard/Elo/style scores and human-preference ratings are proxies for 'being liked' that can diverge from task competence. Systems tuned to these proxies can be optimized for the perception of quality while true capability stagnates — the classic reward-hacking failure mode at the human level.

Generalization

Whenever an evaluation signal is easier to measure than the underlying capability, expect optimization pressure to move the proxy, not the capability; monitor for divergence between proxy and downstream task success.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

Attribution of credit in collaborative work is a narrative that is set by who speaks first and how the work is framed ('we' vs 'I'), and claims can be safely re-anchored later once organizational sensitivity has dropped.

Why it matters

Systems that route outputs through a summarizing/reporting layer will systematically misattribute credit away from the producing layers unless attribution is recorded at production time rather than at reporting time.

Multi-channel objective inference for agents and reward models

Research question

Does fusing stated intent, third-party triangulation, and revealed-action traces into a single inferred objective outperform optimizing the declared objective directly, and at what instrumentation cost?

Why

Most agent stacks take a declared objective at face value. Pass-1 identifies four independent inference channels (direct ask, listening, subordinate triangulation, revealed behavior) plus the explicit claim that people's actions and stated wants diverge. Turning that into an ablation gives a falsifiable answer about specification gaming, not just a metaphor.

Proxy-capability divergence between preference ratings and task success

Research question

Can we detect and quantify the point at which a preference/likability-style proxy score improves while ground-truth task success degrades?

Why

This is reward hacking stated in human terms. If likability can outperform measured performance for survival, then any cheap preference proxy will be optimized past the capability it stands in for. A monitored divergence metric converts an anecdote into a controllable eval-health signal.

Attribution recorded at production time vs. narrative-time summarization

Research question

Does per-component contribution logging at generation time improve downstream credit-assignment accuracy in multi-agent pipelines compared with centralized narrative summarization?

Why

Pass-1 frames credit as narrative ordering: whoever speaks first defines the causal account. Multi-agent systems inherit the same defect wherever a summarizing/orchestrator layer owns the report — training signal, budget, and adoption accrue to the reporter, not the producer. This is a concrete, testable architectural change.

Attribution Ledger

gatehouse

Recording per-component contribution at generation time and requiring reporting layers to aggregate-only reduces credit misattribution to the orchestrator compared with centralized narrative summarization, measurably improving downstream credit-assignment accuracy.

Proof of concept

A multi-agent pipeline where every component emits a signed contribution record (inputs consumed, artifact produced, causal step) at production time; a summarizer layer can only cite these records. Run the same task suite with (a) narrative summarization and (b) ledger aggregation.

Measurement

Agreement between assigned credit and ablated-component ground truth (remove one component, measure task degradation) plus fraction of credit assigned to non-producing components.

Proxy Divergence Monitor

beyond-evals

Across optimization steps on a preference-style proxy, a divergence point exists at which proxy score keeps rising while ground-truth task success stops or falls, and this point is detectable before large capability loss.

Proof of concept

Train or tune against a cheap preference proxy (style/approval rating) on a task with a separate held-out ground-truth metric; log both per checkpoint and plot divergence.

Measurement

Step index of first sustained divergence, magnitude of proxy gain after divergence, and downstream task-success loss at that point.

Architectural implications

2

Credit and causality in collaborative work are assigned by narrative ordering, not by production order.

Before

A single reporting/summarization layer (manager, PM, or orchestrator agent) owns the account of who did what.

After

Attribution is recorded by each producing component at generation time, and reporting layers merely aggregate it.

Consequence

Downstream reward, budget, and tool-adoption signals flow to the components that actually produced the outcome instead of the loudest reporter, reducing misoptimization in multi-agent pipelines.

Source video ↗

Objective inference from stated goals alone is unreliable; the summary lists four independent channels including subordinate triangulation and revealed behavior.

Before

An agent or planner is given a declared objective and optimizes it directly.

After

A goal-inference layer fuses direct statements, second-hand signals, and observed action traces before committing to an objective.

Consequence

Reduces specification-gaming and reward-hacking when the declared objective diverges from the principal's actual incentives, at the cost of extra instrumentation and latency.

Acting on unverifiable belief about dishonesty

Benefit

Managers often know with high confidence that an employee is running a game and removing them protects team morale.

Cost or risk

Termination or punitive action requires airtight evidence even when belief is strong, so acting on conviction without evidence creates legal and organizational risk and a gap between judgment and action.

Optimizing for likability over performance

Benefit

Being well-liked secures peer support and career longevity.

Cost or risk

If the observable proxy (likability) decouples from actual capability, organizations and evaluation systems can retain and promote lower-performing actors while genuine performers go unrecognized.

Empire building through head count and turf

Benefit

Cost or risk

Benefit

Slight exaggeration of legitimate claims improves visibility and impact without outright deception.

Cost or risk
