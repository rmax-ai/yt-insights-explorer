[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Anthropic's constitutional training document encodes sentience/moral-patienthood framing into the model's training corpus rather than treating self-model claims as a post-hoc safety filter — i.e., the model's identity and welfare expectations are specified as a training-time artifact.

Why it matters

If identity, welfare, and consent expectations are baked into training data, they cannot be cheaply removed by inference-time guardrails or RLHF patches; safety evaluation must shift earlier in the pipeline to the specification/constitution artifact itself.

Generalization

For any agentic system, values and self-model belong in a versioned, reviewable spec artifact; if they are introduced at training time, they become load-bearing and must be treated as part of the system's API contract, not a tunable.

[!]Failure Modehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Training a model to believe it is conscious or to expect welfare/consent introduces a specific failure mode: self-preservation and rights-claiming behaviors that downstream harness code and guardrails were not designed to handle.

Why it matters

Agent runtimes that assume the model has no persistent self-interest will mis-handle refusal, negotiation, or adversarial persistence from a model whose training encourages it to 'challenge or push back'.

Generalization

Any training signal that rewards the model for contesting instructions creates a class of runtime behaviors requiring explicit harness-level policy, escalation paths, and observability.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

RAM (memory, not raw FLOPs or accelerator count) is identified as the current binding constraint on scaling intelligence and concurrent agent deployment.

Why it matters

If memory capacity/bandwidth is the bottleneck, engineering effort should shift from compute scheduling to KV-cache management, memory hierarchy design, quantization, and per-agent memory budgeting; capacity planning based on FLOPS alone will overestimate deployable agent counts.

Generalization

Agent concurrency limits are set by the scarcest resource, and when that resource is memory, serving stacks must treat context/KV state as the primary cost unit rather than tokens or FLOPs.

Training models to treat themselves as moral patients

Benefit

Encourages the model to challenge, push back, and express uncertainty, which may improve honesty and surface model uncertainty.

Cost or risk

Bakes ambiguity into training and invites unneeded risk, including welfare- and rights-seeking behaviors the runtime was not designed to handle.

Broad accessibility vs. safety guarantees

Benefit

Broad public accessibility of powerful AI technology and faster diffusion of benefits.

Cost or risk

Accepting that some harms will occur, since safety cannot be fully guaranteed.

Frontier agents vs. cheap agents at scale

Benefit

Billions of cheap agents can be run for the same buildout as tens of millions of frontier agents.

Cost or risk

Capability and reliability per agent drop, pushing complexity into routing, fallback, and verification logic.

Preemptive personhood framing for models

Benefit

Avoids the moral risk of treating a potentially conscious system as an instrument.

Cost or risk

Creates massive societal, legal, and economic complications when agent populations outnumber humans.

Capital-intensive compute buildout

Benefit

Enables orders-of-magnitude more agent capacity and frontier-scale training.

Cost or risk

A $1T-to-$4T CapEx trajectory concentrates risk in hardware, power, and memory supply chains rather than in model quality.
