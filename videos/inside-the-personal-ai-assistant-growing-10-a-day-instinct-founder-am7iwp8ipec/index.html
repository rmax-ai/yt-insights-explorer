Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Noah Shinn and Patrick O'Shaughnessy
  • Channel: Invest Like The Best
  • Main topic: Building Instinct, a personal AI assistant growing rapidly through unique interface design and trust networks.
  • Purpose: To explore the product architecture, user psychology, growth dynamics, and strategic vision behind Instinct, a next-generation personal AI assistant. Noah Shinn, founder of Instinct, discusses building a personal AI assistant that operates through simple interfaces like text and voice, scaling rapidly at 10% day-over-day growth. The conversation covers the product's philosophy of acting as a proactive proxy for users, trust networks, economic models, and the future of human-computer interaction in an AI-native world.

Interface Philosophy and Everyday Intelligence

  • Key claims:
    • Instinct is not a new app or tool, but a new experience combining a phone and computer interface.

    • Simplifying the user experience reduces friction to zero.

Security, Trust, and the Future of Software

  • Explanation: Addressing the challenges of data privacy, safety, and the eventual collapse of traditional software interfaces.
  • Key claims:

    • AI agents will eventually replace legacy software applications as the primary interface.
    • Proactivity requires rigorous safety firewalls and oversight.

  • Why it matters: Establishes trust as the core moat and technical bottleneck for autonomous personal agents.

Key Points

Proactive vs. Reactive AI

  • Explanation: True personal assistants must anticipate needs and act proactively without requiring constant prompting from the user.
  • Evidence: Instinct calling users with urgent deadlines or booking travel itineraries automatically based on inferred preferences.
  • Practical implication: Product design must shift from chatbot prompt interfaces to ambient, autonomous background execution.

Trust as a Compounding Moat

  • Explanation: Sharing sensitive data like credit cards and calendars builds high retention and trust over a multi-week period.
  • Evidence: 80% retention rate for users who share at least one sensitive piece of information with Instinct.
  • Practical implication: Early product onboarding must prioritize security, data control, and gradual trust-building.

Multi-Player AI Coordination

  • Explanation: AI agents communicating with other users' AI agents eliminates friction in scheduling, planning, and commerce.
  • Evidence: Coordinating rideshares, dinner bookings, and meetings across groups of friends automatically.
  • Practical implication: Network effects emerge when AI agents transact and coordinate on behalf of multiple users.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

Trust-Based Scaling Framework

  • How it works: Gradually expanding AI autonomy as the user shares sensitive data and establishes trust over time.
  • Components:
    • Frictionless onboarding
    • Granular access controls
    • Proactive background execution
    • High retention through personal context
  • When to use: Building personal AI agents that handle sensitive financial, scheduling, and personal data.

Why

Proactive agents execute side effects without a synchronous user prompt, so the safety layer must be a distinct pre-execution gate on the background path; getting it wrong risks real-world harm and relationship damage in trust networks.

Why

Project ideas

Trust-State Machine for Agent Autonomy

gatehouse

A per-user trust-state machine that escalates autonomy based on observable signals (data grants, correction rate, action success) and supports revocation will increase user trust and retention compared to static permission models.

Proof of concept

Implement a runtime that tracks a trust level per user and gates agent actions accordingly; simulate user interactions with escalating data sharing and measure the machine's ability to escalate and revoke.

Measurement

Compare retention and user-reported trust in an A/B test against a static permission model; track escalation/revocation events and their correlation with retention.

Source video ↗

Pre-Execution Policy Firewall for Proactive Agents

gatehouse

A pre-execution policy layer with action classification reduces harmful proactive actions by >50% with <10% utility loss on benchmark tasks.

Proof of concept

Build a policy engine that evaluates intended actions before execution, using a classifier and rules for action categorization; test on a set of proactive tasks with known harmful/benign labels.

Measurement

Precision/recall of block/allow decisions and utility loss (task success rate) on the proactive task suite.

Source video ↗

new

Proof of concept

Implement a minimal protocol for two agents to negotiate a meeting time under consent constraints; extend to three agents for a group event.

Measurement

Time to consensus, number of messages exchanged, and user satisfaction compared to human-mediated coordination in a controlled experiment.

Architectural implications

4

Autonomy is described as something that grows with trust rather than being set once.

Before

Permissions/capabilities configured statically at install or signup.

After

Per-user autonomy level tracked as runtime state, escalating as sensitive-data grants and trust accrue, with per-action gating.

Consequence

Requires persistent trust-state, per-action policy evaluation, revocation, and auditability — the permission layer becomes a core subsystem, not config.

Source video ↗

Proactive actions occur with no user prompt in the loop.

Before

Chat turn: user prompt → model → response, with human review implicit in the interaction.

After

Ambient trigger → planned action → policy firewall → side effect, with the human possibly absent entirely.

Consequence

Safety checks must move to pre-execution on the background path; failure modes shift from bad answers to bad real-world actions, raising the bar for idempotency, rate limiting, and rollback.

Source video ↗

Agents coordinate across multiple users' trust graphs.

Before

Single-principal agent with its own tool registry and memory.

After

Inter-agent (cross-tenant) negotiation over shared scheduling/booking state under mutual consent.

Consequence

Introduces cross-principal identity, consent propagation, and dispute-handling concerns that single-user harnesses do not address; also creates the network-effect moat.

Source video ↗

Legacy app surfaces are expected to disappear behind conversational/proactive agents.

Before

Human picks an app to accomplish a task; UI is the integration point.

After

Agent is the primary interface and selects/invokes backend services on the user's behalf.

Consequence

Integration and permission boundaries move from UI apps to agent capabilities, shifting where access control, rate limiting, and observability must live.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

Proactivity vs. trust and safety

Benefit

Zero-friction, anticipatory assistance (e.g., calling before a deadline, autonomous travel booking).

Cost or risk

Autonomous side effects without a prompt can be wrong or harmful, which is precisely why the summary requires safety firewalls; a single bad proactive action in a trust network has relationship-level consequences.

Source video ↗

Rapid growth vs. compute cost

Benefit

~10% day-over-day viral growth from word-of-mouth with no paid marketing.

Cost or risk

Capital-intensive inference costs make unbounded demand dangerous; the summary ties invite-only design explicitly to compute scaling.

Source video ↗

Sensitive-data collection vs. privacy risk

Benefit

High retention (80%) among users who share sensitive info; deep context enables better assistance.

Cost or risk

Sharing credit cards, emails, and calendars concentrates high-value data, making the agent a high-severity target and the trust relationship fragile; the summary notes violation carries direct relational consequences.

Source video ↗

Intent alignment vs. monetization

Benefit

Avoids attention-capture incentives and aligns with user intent.

Cost or risk

Removes the ad revenue model, forcing reliance on take-rates whose economics are unproven against incumbents like Expedia and OpenTable.

Source video ↗

Invite-only gating vs. growth ceiling

Benefit

Controls compute load and preserves a curated, high-trust network.

Cost or risk

Deliberately caps distribution and slows addressable-market expansion; requires the invite loop to actually compound to justify the constraint.
