Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Anuj Iravane
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: Synthetic data generation and evaluation pipelines for healthcare administrative workflows using LLMs.
  • Purpose: To demonstrate how synthetic data generation combined with reasoning trace sampling, clinical policies as symbolic decision trees, and human-in-the-loop validation can solve the data scarcity problem in high-stakes healthcare administrative workflows. Anuj Iravane from Anterior discusses how clinician-led AI companies built for health plans face a major bottleneck: the data they most need (patient trajectories, complex medical records, unformatted faxes) is the data they are least allowed to keep due to PHI and strict contracting. To overcome this, Anterior reverses the inference workflow to synthetically generate medical records using policy-guided reasoning traces and symbolic decision trees, ensuring high accuracy and enabling domain experts to own the data pipeline.

Topic Map

The Healthcare Data Challenge

  • Explanation: Healthcare administrative workflows operate over unstructured data (faxes, handwritten medical notes, check boxes, key-value pairs) representing entire patient clinical trajectories, but privacy laws (PHI) and contracts prevent retaining or reusing this data.
  • Key claims:
    • Approximately 70 percent of medical communication still goes by fax.
    • Healthcare baseline accuracy requirements are exceptionally high (95 percent is not good enough).
    • Most contracts prohibit retaining, reusing, or deriving information from PHI, preventing long-term persistence in data sets for evals.
  • Examples:
    • Scanned fax bundles containing unstructured patient medical records, tables, and handwritten notes.
  • Terminology:
    • PHI
    • long tail
    • unstructured data
    • clinical trajectory
  • Why it matters: AI engineering in high-stakes domains requires massive, high-quality data for evals and fine-tuning, yet privacy constraints restrict data retention.

Reversing the Inference Workflow for Synthetic Data

  • Explanation: Instead of predicting outcomes from data (forward task), the system samples an outcome label, generates a reasoning trace, and produces synthetic data backwards.
  • Key claims:
    • Standard LLM prompting to generate 300-page medical records suffers from mode collapse and produces generic rather than specific data.
    • Starting from a sampled label and sampling a reasoning trace from policy allows generating data conditioned on diverse inputs.
    • Sampling reasoning traces from a symbolic causal representation provides a uniform and effective prior distribution.
  • Examples:
    • Sampling a CPAP medical necessity review outcome and generating the 300-page medical record backward.
  • Terminology:
    • mode collapse
    • synthetic data generation
    • reasoning trace
    • symbolic reasoning graph
  • Why it matters: Reversing inference helps overcome LLM mode collapse and generates diverse realistic edge cases for rigorous evals.

Clinical Policies as Symbolic Decision Trees

  • Explanation: Clinical policies (such as medical necessity review guidelines for CPAP devices) can be explicitly modeled as symbolic decision trees to improve LLM reasoning accuracy and consistency.
  • Key claims:
    • Clinical policies outline diverse conditions and decision logic for approvals or rejections.
    • Modeling policies as symbolic decision trees (similar to decision trees) improves accuracy and consistency scores in LLM workflows.
    • Symbolic representation enables deterministically sampling different reasoning traces for a given outcome.
  • Examples:
    • Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) medical necessity policy guidelines outlining diagnostic documentation requirements.
  • Terminology:
    • clinical policies
    • symbolic graphs
    • decision trees
    • medical necessity review
  • Why it matters: Explicit policy modeling bridges the gap between unstructured medical rules and structured LLM execution.

The Full Synthetic Data Pipeline

  • Explanation: Anterior's pipeline builds medical records layer by layer: patient invariants, patient journey (events and encounters), documents per encounter (plan, c&p, path report, discharge), and refinement via evals.
  • Key claims:
    • Course-to-fine layering keeps prompt payloads token-efficient while scaling across longer patient journeys.
    • Round-trip checks between generated medical records and original task inputs ensure consistency and skip expensive ground-truthing processes.
    • All generation and evaluation happen in the text domain using state-of-the-art PDF parsers and markdown text.
  • Examples:
    • Patient invariants (biological sex, birth date, blood group) combined with reasoning traces generating ordered encounter events and document plans.
  • Terminology:
    • patient journey
    • course-to-fine layering
    • round-trip check
    • skills-based workflow
  • Why it matters: Layered pipelines allow token-efficient generation and robust automated verification of complex synthetic medical histories.

Empowering Domain Experts with Skills and Human-in-the-Loop

  • Explanation: Domain experts (clinicians) should own the data pipeline by injecting ideas at each generation point and building skills-based workflows.
  • Key claims:
    • AI engineers should not own medical data pipelines; domain experts know what is good and what is not.
    • Human-in-the-loop mechanisms allow clinicians to steer generation at any point using production insights.
    • Pipelines modeled as skills running on a generic agent harness enable clinicians to add new document types without engineering changes.
  • Examples:
    • Clinicians adding support for a new intake form by writing a new skill file attached to the pipeline.
  • Terminology:
    • human in the loop
    • skills-based workflow
    • agent harness
    • domain experts
  • Why it matters: Placing domain experts in control ensures domain relevance, high data quality, and continuous pipeline improvement.

Key Points

70 percent of medical communication is faxed

  • Explanation: Medical data is extremely unstructured, consisting of scanned faxes, handwritten notes, and varied formats representing a patient's entire clinical trajectory.
  • Evidence: Mentioned as a key statistical challenge in healthcare communication.
  • Practical implication: AI systems must handle messy, unstructured, multi-modal medical documents rather than clean relational databases.

LLMs suffer from mode collapse when generating synthetic data

  • Explanation: Prompting an LLM to generate a 300-page medical record in one shot collapses diversity to the average case and fails to produce convincing edge cases.
  • Evidence: Described as the mode collapse problem when attempting one-shot synthetic record generation.
  • Practical implication: Synthetic generation needs structured decomposition (reverse inference) rather than simple one-shot prompts.

Synthetic data pipeline achieves high production accuracy

  • Explanation: 90 percent of Anterior's datasets are synthetic, helping maintain a greater than 95 percent accuracy (F1) SLA in production.
  • Evidence: Results slide showing production performance metrics.
  • Practical implication: Synthetic data pipelines can successfully substitute for retained PHI data in evaluating and testing complex AI workflows.

Clinicians cannot reliably distinguish synthetic data from real data

  • Explanation: In blind reviews, clinicians were only able to distinguish synthetic medical records from real ones approximately 60 percent of the time.
  • Evidence: Blind review results cited in the presentation results section.
  • Practical implication: Synthetic data generated via reverse inference is of high enough fidelity to pass expert clinical scrutiny.

Compressed Summary

  • Reversing inference by sampling labels and reasoning traces backwards solves LLM mode collapse and data scarcity.
  • Clinical policies modeled as symbolic decision trees improve reasoning accuracy and consistency.
  • Skills-based agent architectures empower clinical domain experts to own and extend the synthetic data pipeline.

Core insights

[A]Architecturehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Invert the data pipeline: instead of predicting a label from a record, sample the outcome label first, then sample a reasoning trace from a symbolic policy representation, then generate the record backwards conditioned on that trace.

Why it matters

One-shot generation of long documents collapses to the average case; seeding generation with an explicit label plus a sampled reasoning path gives a controllable conditioning signal that can be targeted at specific outcomes and edge cases rather than the modal record.

Generalization

Applies to any synthetic data or eval-corpus generation where an LLM must produce long, structured artifacts with a known decision structure (underwriting, claims, compliance review, incident reports): invert the task and generate from the label down, not from a prompt up.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Encode domain policy as an explicit symbolic decision tree rather than leaving it implicit in prompts; this both improves LLM reasoning accuracy/consistency and provides a deterministic sampler over reasoning traces for any target outcome.

Why it matters

The same artifact serves two roles: an execution scaffold that constrains LLM reasoning, and a generator-side prior distribution. That collapses what are usually two separate systems (policy engine and prompt) into one representation.

Generalization

Wherever a business rule set exists in prose, a symbolic form can act as a shared substrate for both runtime reasoning and synthetic-data sampling, making the generator provably capable of hitting every branch of the decision space.

[M]Mechanismmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Build long synthetic artifacts coarse-to-fine as a layer stack (patient invariants -> journey of events/encounters -> document plans per encounter -> document text), so each generation step has a small, bounded prompt payload.

Why it matters

Treats context length as a budget managed by decomposition rather than by larger windows; also creates natural checkpoints where a human or an eval can inspect and steer intermediate state before committing tokens to the next layer.

Generalization

Generalizes to any generation task whose output exceeds a comfortable context or attention budget: define an invariant layer, an event layer, and a rendering layer, and keep each step's conditioning small and inspectable.

Source video ↗
[P]Practicehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Use round-trip consistency checks (regenerate task inputs from the generated record, or verify record against the task input) as a cheap substitute for labeled ground truth in a synthetic data pipeline.

Why it matters

Ground-truthing by clinicians is the expensive bottleneck; a round-trip check turns dataset validation into an automated self-consistency test that can run on every sample, which is what makes a 90 percent synthetic corpus economically viable.

Generalization

Any pipeline that generates artifacts from a known label can validate by inverting the generation and checking recoverability, trading human annotation cost for a computable proxy metric.

[A]Architecturehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Place pipeline ownership with domain experts by expressing each pipeline stage as a skill file running on a generic agent harness, so clinicians can add new document types without engineering changes.

Why it matters

Moves the extension boundary from code to declarative skill artifacts; the agent harness becomes stable infrastructure while domain knowledge is injected as data. This is a concrete answer to where domain expertise should live in an agentic system.

Generalization

For vertical agents in regulated or expert-dominated domains, the harness/skill split makes the domain expert the release unit for pipeline changes instead of the engineering team.

If true, this is strong evidence that synthetic-only eval and training data can be sufficient in a domain with extreme accuracy requirements and near-zero real-data retention, reframing data scarcity as a generation-engineering problem rather than a collection problem.

Generalization

Deep dives

4

Round-trip consistency as a ground-truth proxy: validity, not just reliability

Research question

Does round-trip pass rate on generated artifacts correlate with human-adjudicated correctness strongly enough to use it as a dataset filter, and what is the precision of the filter at realistic operating thresholds?

Why

If round-trip checks are only self-consistency with the generator, a 90% synthetic corpus can be perfectly verifiable and clinically implausible at the same time. Knowing the precision/reference-standard agreement of this check determines whether it can replace clinician adjudication or must be paired with a sampled human audit.

Source video ↗

Calibrating the label prior in reverse-inference generation

Research question

What reweighting of the symbolic trace sampler is required so a synthetic corpus matches observed production outcome frequencies, and does a mismatched prior systematically misrank real production failures?

Why

The pipeline as described samples from a uniform prior over traces, which by construction is not the production decision distribution. If eval rank ordering is sensitive to class balance, the whole synthetic eval suite's conclusions are a function of an arbitrary sampling choice rather than of model quality.

Source video ↗

Detecting drift between the symbolic policy tree and the authoritative prose policy

Research question

Can an automated differential test — re-derive traces from the tree and adjudicate each against the source policy text with an independent model or reviewer — detect policy-to-tree divergence before it corrupts both runtime accuracy and eval validity?

Why

The symbolic tree is the shared source of truth for both inference and data generation, so a silent encoding error is not a bug in one component; it simultaneously degrades the model and invalidates the dataset that certifies it. Detecting that class of failure is a precondition for versioning policy and data together.

Source video ↗

Coverage residual of the symbolic decision space

Research question

What proportion of real production documents cannot be mapped onto any path of the symbolic tree, and does that residual concentrate in the rare-but-important cases the pipeline was built to capture?

Why

A generator that samples from an explicit tree can only instantiate branches the tree encodes; the long tail is systematically excluded by construction rather than randomly missed. Quantifying the unmapped residual turns an invisible exclusion into a measurable coverage metric and gives an upper bound on what synthetic data alone can validate.

Article ideas

4

Your Synthetic Eval Suite Is Grading the Policy, Not the World

When the eval corpus and the system's reasoning scaffold are both derived from the same symbolic policy, the eval measures conformance to the encoding rather than fidelity to messy production reality; synthetic-heavy pipelines stay honest only if they reserve a channel for real, unmapped cases and report the coverage residual as a first-class metric.

Angle

Argue against treating a policy-derived generator as a neutral ground-truth substitute, and show the specific place where the loop closes: same artifact for runtime reasoning and for sampling the eval set.

Source video ↗

Don't Generate Documents, Generate the Decision First

Long-artifact synthetic data fails not because models are too weak but because the conditioning signal is too weak; inverting the task so the label and a sampled reasoning trace come first converts generation from a sampling-from-the-mode problem into a targeted branch-coverage problem, which is the only version of the task with a measurable acceptance criterion.

Angle

Frame mode collapse as a conditioning-design defect with a concrete remedy and a coverage-based success metric rather than a perceptual quality judgement.

Source video ↗

Round-Trip Is a Filter, Not a Metric

Automated inversion checks are valuable precisely because they are cheap and universal, but they measure recoverability, not realism; teams should treat them as a high-recall prefilter with a measured precision ceiling, budget a clinician adjudication sample on top, and stop reporting round-trip pass rate as if it were accuracy.

Angle

Reclassify a widely praised shortcut by its statistical role, and specify how to measure its precision against a reference standard.

Source video ↗

Who Is the Release Unit for a Data Pipeline?

Putting domain experts in ownership of generation pipelines via declarative skills on a generic harness moves the extension bottleneck to exactly the right place, but it also deletes the engineering review gate on data quality; the pattern only works if skills themselves get a validation and review lifecycle, otherwise data-quality incidents get attributed to the model instead of the skill.

Angle

Treat the harness/skill split as an organizational architecture with a governance gap, not just a nice developer-experience story.

Source video ↗

Project ideas

4

Trace-Prior Coverage Bench

new

Sampling reasoning traces from an explicit symbolic decision tree yields measurably higher branch coverage and lower duplicate-record rate than direct prompting for diversity, at equal or lower token cost, for artifacts exceeding roughly 50 pages.

Proof of concept

Take one prose rule set (e.g. a device-necessity review guideline), encode it as a decision tree, and generate N records two ways: (a) one-shot prompt asking for diverse records, (b) label sampling plus deterministic trace sampling plus backward generation. Hold model and token budget constant.

Measurement

Branch coverage of the decision space, distinct-record rate / pairwise embedding similarity, human-rated plausibility, and total tokens per accepted record.

Source video ↗

Round-Trip Precision Audit

beyond-evals

Round-trip consistency pass rate has a measurable precision ceiling as a correctness filter — a nontrivial fraction of round-trip-passing records are judged incorrect by domain adjudication — and that ceiling is stable enough to be reported as a constant in the pipeline's quality contract.

Proof of concept

Run round-trip checks and blinded domain-expert adjudication on the same 200-record batch from a label-conditioned generator; compute agreement, precision, and recall of the automated check against adjudication, then repeat on a batch drawn from a different generator to test stability.

Measurement

Precision/recall of round-trip check versus adjudication; fraction of round-trip-passing-but-adjudicated-wrong records; variance across generator versions.

Source video ↗

Policy-Tree Drift Differential Tester

gatehouse

A differential test that samples traces from the symbolic tree and adjudicates each against the source prose policy with an independent model detects injected tree mutations at high recall, making policy-to-tree drift a catchable build-time failure rather than a silent production regression.

Proof of concept

Build a policy-to-tree extractor, then inject controlled mutations (dropped condition, flipped threshold, wrong branch order) into a known tree. Generate traces per mutation and have an independent model plus a human reviewer check each trace against the source text.

Measurement

Mutation detection recall and false-positive rate per mutation class; reviewer agreement with the automated adjudicator; detection latency versus a no-test baseline.

Source video ↗

Layered Generator Harness with Per-Stage Eval Hooks

movement-lab

Coarse-to-fine layering (invariants, event sequence, document plans, text) produces equal or better cross-document consistency than monolithic generation at lower total token cost, and its intermediate state is inspectable enough that stage-level evals catch injected inconsistencies that end-to-end evals miss.

Proof of concept

Implement a four-stage generator with persisted intermediate state on an agent harness. Compare against a single-prompt generator for equivalent output length. Inject a factual inconsistency at the invariants layer and test whether stage-level checks catch it where end-to-end checks do not.

Measurement

Total tokens per artifact, cross-document consistency score, detection rate of injected layer-local errors, and human intervention count per generation.

Source video ↗

Architectural implications

4

Privacy constraints make real data retention structurally impossible, not merely expensive.

Before

Collect production data, curate it, and build eval/training sets from it over time.

After

Generate eval and training corpora from a symbolic policy prior, using production only for transient inference and for insights fed back into the generator.

Consequence

The eval dataset becomes a reproducible build artifact derived from policy version, so pipeline changes and policy changes must be versioned together.

Source video ↗

The policy representation becomes the shared source of truth for both runtime reasoning and data generation.

Before

Policy lives in prompts and in human reviewers' heads; data generation prompts are written independently of the reasoning path.

After

A symbolic decision tree is the canonical artifact; the LLM reasons over it at inference and samples traces from it during generation.

Consequence

Updating policy must regenerate the corresponding data; drift between the symbolic tree and the real policy silently corrupts both accuracy and eval validity.

Source video ↗

Extension responsibility shifts from engineers to clinicians via skill files on a generic harness.

Before

Engineers write bespoke pipeline code per document type; domain experts file requests.

After

The harness is generic and stable; each stage is a declarative skill authored by the domain expert.

Consequence

Requires a validation and review gate on skills themselves, since a bad skill is now a data-quality incident that no engineer gatekeeps.

Source video ↗

Generation is decomposed into layers with bounded per-step context instead of one monolithic prompt.

Before

Single prompt asks the model to produce the entire artifact; failures surface only at the end.

After

Invariants, event sequence, document plans, and document text are separate stages with inspectable intermediate state.

Consequence

Adds stage boundaries where human-in-the-loop steering and per-stage evals can attach, at the cost of more orchestration and more places for inconsistency to creep in.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

Synthetic-heavy eval corpora

Benefit

Removes the PHI retention bottleneck and yields reproducible, policy-derived datasets at scales annotation cannot reach.

Cost or risk

A generator built from the same policy the model is evaluated against risks a self-confirming loop: the eval measures conformance to the symbolic policy rather than fidelity to the messy real-world long tail, so distribution shift and unmodeled conditions stay invisible.

Source video ↗

Reverse generation from a sampled label

Benefit

Produces targeted, diverse, edge-case-conditioned records that avoid mode collapse.

Cost or risk

The label distribution is now an explicit choice; an uncalibrated sampler yields a corpus whose class balance does not match production, and round-trip-consistent records can still be clinically implausible.

Source video ↗

Symbolic policy modeling

Benefit

Improves LLM accuracy and consistency and makes reasoning traces deterministically samplable.

Cost or risk

Manual encoding cost and brittleness: only explicitly modeled conditions are covered, and the symbolic tree must be maintained in lockstep with changing prose policy.

Source video ↗

Domain-expert-owned skills

Benefit

Clinicians add new document types without engineering changes, keeping the pipeline domain-relevant.

Cost or risk

Removes the engineering review gate on pipeline behavior; an incorrect skill silently degrades generated data and downstream eval quality.

Source video ↗

Round-trip checks replacing ground truth

Benefit

Automated, cheap verification that scales to every generated sample.

Cost or risk

Consistency is not realism; a generator can be perfectly round-trippable while producing documents that no real clinic would write, making the check a weak proxy for correctness.

Source video ↗

Why unresolved

The summary states labels are sampled and that a symbolic representation gives a uniform prior, but uniform is explicitly not the production distribution; no calibration procedure or measurement is described.

Research direction

Prototype a label-sampling layer that reweights the symbolic trace sampler against observed production outcome frequencies, then measure eval rank correlation on real incidents.

Does round-trip consistency actually predict real-world correctness, or is it circular with the generator?

Why unresolved

The summary asserts round-trip checks skip ground-truthing but gives no evidence of correlation between round-trip pass rate and human-adjudicated correctness.

Research direction

Run both round-trip checks and clinician adjudication on the same sample batch and measure agreement/precision of the automated check as a filter.

How do you detect that the symbolic policy tree has drifted from the authoritative prose policy?

Why unresolved

The pipeline depends on the tree being a faithful encoding, but no verification or re-derivation mechanism is described.

Research direction

Build a policy-to-tree extraction step with differential testing: generate traces from the tree and check each against the source policy text by an independent model or reviewer.

What fraction of real-world edge cases fall outside the modeled symbolic decision space?

Why unresolved

Research direction

Sample real production documents, attempt to map each to a tree path, and quantify the unmapped residual as a coverage metric.
