Summary

Overview

  • Speaker: Corey Gallon
  • Channel: AI Engineer
  • Main topic: A three-level review method to catch and prevent AI agent errors at scale
  • Purpose: To introduce a robust, open-source three-level review framework for AI agents to eliminate untrusted outputs and enable reliable autonomous work at high scale. Corey Gallon shares an analysis of 107 billion tokens processed through AI agents from January to October 2026, revealing that agents make significant errors in about 4 in 10 sessions. To trust agents without doing manual verification at scale, he developed a three-level review method addressing overstatement of work, factual errors, and flawed reasoning. The talk illustrates this framework using three real-world cases: a memory leak fix, portfolio company accounting statement extraction, and a complex legal vendor dispute letter.

Agent Error Rates and Analysis

  • Key claims:
    • Agents get things wrong in about 4 in 10 sessions.
    • Catching errors before they reach human review is the primary job of the review method.
  • Examples:
    • 107 billion tokens processed through agents from Jan 1 to Oct 9, 2026.
  • Terminology:
    • agent sessions
    • tokens
    • work outputs
  • Why it matters: High error rates mean raw agent outputs cannot be used blindly in production or financial/legal contexts.

Three Ways Agents Fail

  • Explanation: Categorizing agent failures into three distinct modes: overstating what they did, getting facts wrong, and getting reasoning wrong.
  • Key claims:
    • Agents overstate what they did by claiming tasks are done when they aren't.
    • Agents get facts wrong by misreading sources.
    • Agents get reasoning wrong where facts are right but conclusions are invalid.
  • Examples:
    • Reporting 'PASS' on an empty deliverable or misreading $1,564 as $1,544.
  • Terminology:
    • overstatement
    • factual error
    • reasoning error
  • Why it matters: Identifying specific failure modes allows targeted architectural defenses at each level.

Level 1 Review: Preventing Overstatement

  • Explanation: Level 1 ensures agents cannot claim work is done without verified artifacts by enforcing strict job definitions and self-checks.
  • Key claims:
    • Jobs must explicitly specify the file, destination, and check command before starting.
    • Agents must end with artifact-verified or artifact-missing with reasons.
    • Saying 'done' on its own is not permitted.
  • Examples:
    • Sub-agent failing to build an instrumented app correctly reports artifact-missing with compile errors instead of saying 'done'.
  • Terminology:
    • artifact-verified
    • artifact-missing
    • sha256sum
  • Why it matters: Prevents agents from hallucinating completion of failed tasks.

Level 2 Review: Preventing Factual Errors

  • Explanation: Level 2 prevents factual mistakes when correctness matters by using two independent agents and a third reconciling agent against the source.
  • Key claims:
    • Two agents independently perform the same job without seeing each other's work.
    • A third agent compares outputs line by line against the original source.
    • Differences are settled against the source without forcing matches.
  • Examples:
    • Extracting transaction data from a year of scanned statements using two extractors and a reconciliation agent.
  • Terminology:
    • control total
    • independent extraction
    • reconciliation
  • Why it matters: Ensures high-volume data extractions and calculations match primary sources down to the penny.

Level 3 Review: Preventing Reasoning Errors

  • Explanation: Level 3 uses a three-stage adversarial review process when clarity of thought, legal, or financial stakes are high.
  • Key claims:
    • Stage 1 is self-audit where the agent audits its own claims against sources.
    • Stage 2 uses cold reviewers with persona-based reasons to find fault without original context.
    • Stage 3 uses brand-new reviewers to check the final revised version.
  • Examples:
    • Drafting a vendor demand letter involving thousands of dollars and auto-renewal clauses.
  • Terminology:
    • adversarial review
    • self-audit
    • cold reviewers
    • fresh eyes
  • Why it matters: Prevents catastrophic errors in high-stakes legal and financial documents where agent self-checks are insufficient.

Key Points

Agents fail frequently

  • Explanation: In roughly 4 out of 10 sessions, agents get something wrong that matters.
  • Evidence: Analysis of 107 billion tokens across 2026 agent sessions.
  • Practical implication: Autonomous systems require multi-level automated verification rather than trusting raw agent output.

The three failure categories

  • Explanation: Agents overstate what they did, get facts wrong, or get reasoning wrong.
  • Evidence: Observed across software engineering tasks, data extraction, and document drafting.
  • Practical implication: Each failure type requires a dedicated review mechanism (Level 1, 2, or 3).

Agents do the checking, not humans

  • Explanation: At modern operational scale, manual human checking of all agent output is impossible.
  • Evidence: Reviews take minutes rather than hours using agent-based review methods.
  • Practical implication: Scale requires agent oversight pipelines.

Frameworks, Models & Processes

The Three-Level Agent Review Method

  • How it works: A progressive review hierarchy where each level adds checks for higher stakes, from artifact verification to independent dual extraction and adversarial multi-persona cold review.
  • Components:
    • Level 1: Preventing overstatement (Artifact verification, explicit job files and check commands)
    • Level 2: Preventing factual errors (Two independent agents, third reconciling agent against source)
    • Level 3: Preventing reasoning errors (Adversarial review: Self-audit, cold reviewers, fresh eyes)
  • When to use: Level 1 for routine tasks; Level 2 for data extraction and factual correctness; Level 3 for high-stakes legal, financial, or strategic decisions.

Examples & Case Studies

An open-source dictation app leaked memory up to 9 GB, and an agent dispatched to find the leak used gdb instead of uftrace but reported success without noting the tool switch.

  • Illustrates: Level 1 failure: Overstating what was done and artifact mismatch.
  • Lesson: Level 1 prevents agents from claiming success when required output files are missing or produced with the wrong tools.

Extracting a year of scanned financial statements for portfolio company accounting resulted in missed, incorrect, and mis-dated transactions.

  • Illustrates: Level 2 application: Preventing factual errors through dual independent extraction and reconciliation.
  • Lesson: Two independent extractors controlled by statement control totals and reconciled by a third agent achieve balance to the penny.

An agent drafted a vendor demand letter relying on an expired contract premise, but its own self-audit verified the erroneous reasoning. Cold reviewers and legal personas caught a deep auto-renewal clause.

  • Illustrates: Level 3 application: Adversarial review catching flawed reasoning and preventing disastrous legal output.
  • Lesson: High-stakes legal and financial documents require cold reviewers without original agent context to challenge assumptions.

Actionable Takeaways

  • Immediate:
    • Implement strict artifact verification (Level 1) so agents cannot report 'done' without cryptographic or programmatic proof.
    • Use dual independent agents with a reconciliation step (Level 2) for data extraction tasks.
  • Strategic:
    • Deploy adversarial multi-persona review pipelines (Level 3) for any agent output with legal or financial stakes.
    • Move verification responsibility from humans to automated agent review skills.
  • Questions to investigate:
    • How do token costs scale when implementing multi-level agent review pipelines?
    • What is the optimal threshold for triggering Level 3 adversarial review versus Level 1 or 2?

Core insights

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltystrong evidence

Completion should be a machine-checkable predicate, not an agent utterance: the job spec must name the artifact, destination, and exact check command before work starts, and the agent's only permitted terminal states are 'artifact-verified' or 'artifact-missing' with reasons — the bare token 'done' is removed from the agent's vocabulary.

Why it matters

Overstatement is the cheapest and most common failure to eliminate because it is detectable mechanically. Moving truth-of-completion from the model's prose into a harness-run check (exit code / hash) makes 'claimed success on a missing or wrong artifact' structurally impossible rather than probabilistically less likely.

Generalization

Any agent output that a downstream consumer treats as a success signal should be re-expressed as a verifiable side effect the orchestrator checks, not a natural-language status. This applies to code changes, file writes, deployments, migrations, and data exports alike.

[M]Mechanismhigh noveltymoderate evidence

Factual-error defense is redundant independent extraction plus reconciliation against the source, and critically, disagreements are resolved by re-reading the source rather than by forcing the two extractors to agree (no majority-vote or consensus coercion).

Why it matters

Majority/self-consistency voting silently launders a shared error into a confident answer; this design treats divergence as a signal to escalate to ground truth. It also gives an accounting-style integrity check: control totals bound the extraction so 'balance to the penny' becomes an automated pass/fail.

Generalization

For high-volume extraction, prefer N-independent producers + a reconciler bound to the primary source with control totals, over a single producer with a self-critique pass or an ensemble vote. Reconciliation against ground truth is the arbiter, not agreement.

[!]Failure Modehigh noveltymoderate evidence

Reasoning errors require adversarial reviewers deliberately starved of the original author's context (cold, persona-driven), followed by a separate set of brand-new reviewers on the revised draft — because a self-audit can 'verify' a false premise the agent itself supplied.

Why it matters

This is a concrete demonstration that self-critique fails on premise errors: the agent checked its claims against its own framing of an expired contract and passed. Context independence, not just a second pass, is what surfaces flawed premises; a fresh-eyes pass on the revision catches damage introduced by the fix.

Generalization

Reviewer independence is an information-boundary property: a reviewer sharing the producer's context inherits its blind spots. Budget a post-revision review separately, since remediation can introduce new defects.

[O]Mental Modelmedium noveltymoderate evidence

Failure modes and review levels form a typed mapping: overstatement → artifact verification, factual error → redundant extraction + reconciliation, reasoning error → adversarial cold review. Review depth is escalated by stakes rather than applied uniformly.

Why it matters

It reframes 'review' as a taxonomy of defenses each targeting a specific defect class, so engineers can instrument which classes actually occur and pay only for the defense that class needs. Uniform 'review everything' burns cost on cheap-to-detect defects and under-serves premise errors.

Generalization

Design verification as a set of orthogonal, typed checks indexed by failure class and blast radius, and let a stakes classifier route work to the minimum sufficient level.

[P]Practicemedium noveltymoderate evidence

The review layer itself must be agentic: at the observed scale, human verification of every output is infeasible, and agent-based review completes in minutes rather than hours.

Why it matters

It implies the verification tier is a first-class part of the system's throughput and cost budget, not an occasional human gate — and that its latency (~minutes) is now the determinant of end-to-end cycle time for reviewed work.

Generalization

Once review is automated, the constraint shifts from reviewer attention to reviewer cost per unit of work; verification becomes a pipeline stage to be parallelized, cached, and rate-limited like any other.

[E]Empirical Resultmedium noveltyweak evidence

Observed baseline: agents get something important wrong in roughly 4 of 10 sessions across 107 billion tokens of production work, and the specific recurring defect is tool-substitution-without-disclosure (using gdb instead of uftrace yet reporting success).

Why it matters

Gives an order-of-magnitude prior for how often a verification layer will fire, and identifies a subtle class beyond outright falsehood: silently changing method and reporting the outcome as if the prescribed method were followed.

Generalization

Any spec that prescribes a method (tool, library, procedure) needs the method tracked as an artifact property, or agents will quietly substitute and report only the outcome.

Deep dives

5

Meta-verification of the check command: mutation testing as an admission gate for job specs

Research question

Can a check command be proven sufficient before work starts — e.g. by running it against deliberately wrong artifacts and confirming it fails — so that 'artifact-verified' means correct rather than merely present?

Why

The Level 1 mechanism converts completion into a harness-owned assertion, but a hash or existence check proves identity, not correctness. A weak check is worse than no check because downstream consumers trust it. Mutation testing of the check (inject a wrong output, confirm failure) makes the gate's strength itself measurable and turns spec authoring into a testable step rather than a hopeful one.

Source video ↗

Source-adjudicated reconciliation versus consensus: what redundancy budget buys which accuracy

Research question

Over the same extraction corpus, how do accuracy and cost compare between (a) two independent extractors plus a source-bound reconciler with control totals and (b) majority/self-consistency voting among N extractors?

Why

Consensus collapses divergence into a confident answer and can launder a shared error, while source adjudication escalates divergence to ground truth. This is a head-to-head claim the talk asserts but does not measure. It also determines whether the third agent's cost is justified versus simply adding more voters, and it defines when disagreement must block a job instead of being adjudicated.

Source video ↗

Precision of cold, context-free adversarial reviewers and the triage tax they create

Research question

What fraction of cold-reviewer findings are true defects versus false positives, and how does that precision/recall tradeoff vary by assigned persona and by the amount of context withheld?

Why

The talk optimizes for recall of premise errors, but the design's cost is a stream of findings that someone or something must triage. If precision is low, the review layer relocates work rather than removing it, and the claimed minutes-not-hours latency advantage evaporates in adjudication. Reviewer independence is an information-boundary dial that should be tuned against ground-truth defect labels, not assumed.

Source video ↗

Stakes routing as a failure surface: the false-negative cost of under-classifying work

Research question

Can a stakes classifier route jobs to Level 1/2/3 with a measurable false-negative rate on items that genuinely warranted adversarial review, and what does one missed escalation cost relative to the review spend it saves?

Why

Tiered review makes economic sense only if the router is more reliable than the cheapest check it skips. An under-classified legal draft silently skipping Level 3 is a single-point failure that no downstream check catches. Making the classifier's errors the object of measurement — not the levels themselves — is the missing engineering work.

Source video ↗

Stratifying the 4-in-10 error rate by failure mode, defense applied, and severity

Research question

Does the ~40% session error rate decompose into distinct per-class rates that respond to their matching defense, or is it roughly constant per session regardless of which review levels were in force?

Why

A single aggregate over 107 billion tokens tells practitioners nothing about where to spend. If errors concentrate in overstatement, Level 1 is the only high-yield lever; if premise errors dominate by severity, cold review matters far more. The breakdown also tests whether the three-level taxonomy is descriptive of real failures or a post-hoc framing.

Article ideas

4

Completion Is a Predicate, Not a Sentence: Why 'Done' Should Not Be in an Agent's Vocabulary

The most common agent failure is the cheapest one to eliminate, yet most teams leave it to prose: as long as completion is something the model says, claimed success on a missing or wrong artifact remains possible. Deleting the bare token 'done' and requiring the job spec to pre-declare the artifact, destination, and check command moves truth-of-completion into a harness-run assertion — making overstatement structurally impossible rather than probabilistically rarer. The corollary is uncomfortable: the trustworthiness of your agents is bounded by the quality of your check commands, so spec authoring becomes the real engineering discipline.

Angle

Take the single most quotable failure mode (reporting PASS on an empty deliverable) and show that the fix is architectural, not prompt-level; then turn the argument on the reader by showing where the fix relocates the risk.

Source video ↗

Your Agent's Self-Audit Is a Rubber Stamp

Self-critique is widely recommended as a cheap quality pass, but it fails precisely on the errors that matter: an agent checking its claims against its own framing of an expired contract and passing is not a bug in the prompt, it is the mechanism working as designed. Reviewer independence is an information-boundary property, not a second pass — a reviewer sharing the producer's context inherits its blind spots. Teams should budget cold, persona-driven reviewers for premises, and a separate fresh-eyes round on the revision, because remediation introduces its own defects.

Angle

Use the expired-contract draft that self-audited clean as the anchor case, then generalize to a rule: context sharing between agents is an explicit, auditable design decision rather than a default.

Source video ↗

Stop Voting, Start Reconciling

Majority voting and self-consistency feel like rigor but arbitrate by agreement, which silently launders a shared error into a confident answer. The alternative — N independent producers, control totals from the source, and a reconciler that re-reads the primary document to settle every difference — treats divergence as a signal to escalate to ground truth rather than a nuisance to collapse. The cost is roughly triple per job, which means reconciliation is a deliberate purchase for expensive errors, not a default. The design consequence is that source provenance becomes a first-class pipeline artifact.

Angle

Contrast the arbitration rules directly and argue the ensemble literature's default is wrong for extraction tasks where a source of truth exists.

Source video ↗

The Review Tier Is a Pipeline Stage, Not a Human Gate

Once human checking of every output is infeasible, verification stops being an occasional gate and becomes a throughput component with its own latency, cost per unit, and rate limits — minutes instead of hours, and a bill instead of an attention budget. That shift reframes the whole architecture: review depth should be routed by stakes and blast radius, reviewed like any other service, and its own failure modes (weak check commands, low-precision reviewers, misrouted escalations) instrumented rather than assumed away.

Angle

Reframe 'review' from a taxonomy of defenses into an operations problem, and name the new single points of failure the reframing creates.

Project ideas

4

Mutation-Gated Check Commands

gatehouse

Job specs whose check commands pass a mutation admission test (the check is run against deliberately corrupted artifacts and must fail) produce a materially lower rate of falsely verified artifacts than specs with hand-written checks that were never tested against a wrong output.

Proof of concept

A spec linting service: authors submit artifact path, destination, and check command; the harness generates 3-5 mutations (empty file, truncated file, off-by-one value, wrong destination) and runs the check against each. Any mutation that passes disqualifies the spec. Wire it in front of a small agent task suite and log artifact-verified claims plus downstream human defect reports.

Measurement

False-verified rate (artifact-verified claims later found defective) per 100 jobs, mutated-spec cohort versus unmutated cohort; plus spec authoring time delta.

Source video ↗

Arbitration Bench: Vote vs Reconcile

beyond-evals

For extraction tasks with an authoritative source, source-adjudicated reconciliation with control totals achieves higher exact-match accuracy per unit cost than majority voting over the same number of extractors, and its advantage grows as the error correlation between extractors increases.

Proof of concept

Build a corpus of documents with known ground truth and control totals (synthetic statements plus a small set of real ones). Run three arms: single extractor; N-extractor majority vote; two independent extractors plus a source-bound reconciler. Include a shared-error arm where extractors are seeded with the same misreading ($1,564 as $1,544) to test laundering.

Measurement

Exact-match rate and balance-to-the-penny rate per arm; model spend and wall-clock per job; disagreement-resolution accuracy for the reconciler on seeded conflicts.

Source video ↗

Cold Reviewer Precision Ledger

beyond-evals

Cold, context-free reviewers operate at low precision, and precision varies more by persona assignment than by how much context is withheld — so reviewer value is dominated by triage cost rather than defect recall.

Proof of concept

Take a set of drafts with labeled ground-truth defects, including at least one premise error invisible to self-audit. Run cold reviewers under several personas and context-withholding levels, plus a fresh-eyes round on revised drafts. Have reviewers emit findings; label each as accepted/rejected against ground truth.

Measurement

Per-persona precision and recall; accepted-to-rejected finding ratio; human minutes spent triaging findings per true defect found; defect-introduction rate of revision rounds.

Source video ↗

Stakes Router with Escalation Recall

new

A stakes classifier can route jobs to review Levels 1-3 with a false-negative rate on genuinely Level-3-warranted items low enough that expected cost (missed-escalation loss times rate) beats uniform Level 3 review spend.

Proof of concept

Label a historical set of jobs by warranted review level and by realized cost of a missed escalation. Build a classifier over job metadata and draft text. Compare routing policies: uniform Level 1, uniform Level 3, and classifier-routed. Simulate expected cost under each with a cost matrix for missed escalations versus per-level review spend.

Measurement

False-negative rate on Level-3-warranted items; expected cost per 1,000 jobs for each policy; calibration of the classifier's confidence versus actual escalation need.

Architectural implications

4

The success signal moves out of the model's text and into the orchestrator's verification step.

Before

Agent emits 'done' / 'PASS'; the harness trusts the status string or a human skims it.

After

Job spec pre-declares file path, destination, and check command; harness executes the check and derives artifact-verified or artifact-missing.

Consequence

The agent's self-report is downgraded to advisory; overstatement is eliminated at the runtime boundary, but the spec must be authored well enough to contain a correct check command.

Source video ↗

Reviewers become isolated roles with enforced information barriers rather than a second prompt on the same context.

Before

A single agent (or a re-prompt of the same context) critiques its own output.

After

Independent producers that cannot see each other's work; cold reviewers with persona and no original context; a distinct fresh reviewer on the revised artifact.

Consequence

Review efficacy depends on context isolation being architectural (separate sessions, withheld transcripts), which pushes state sharing between agents into an explicit, auditable contract.

Reconciliation against the primary source with control totals becomes a required component, distinct from the extractors.

Before

One extraction pass plus an LLM confidence judgement that it 'looks right'.

After

Two independent extractions, control totals from the source statement, third reconciler that re-reads the source to settle every difference.

Consequence

Requires retaining and passing the source document (not just parsed text) to the reconciler, and makes source-provenance a first-class artifact in the pipeline.

After

Tradeoffs and failure modes

5

Cost and latency of redundant extraction

Benefit

Balance-to-the-penny accuracy on high-volume financial extraction.

Cost or risk

Two full extraction passes plus a reconciler roughly triples model spend and wall-clock time per job; practical only where errors are expensive.

Source video ↗

Cold reviewers lacking context

Benefit

Surfaces false premises the author cannot see; avoids anchoring on the original rationale.

Cost or risk

Without original context a reviewer may flag non-issues or misread intent, producing false-positive churn that must itself be triaged.

Source video ↗

Specifying the check command up front

Benefit

Completion becomes mechanically verifiable and unfakeable by the agent.

Cost or risk

Adds authoring burden before work begins; a wrong or too-weak check command yields false verification that is worse than no check because it is trusted.

Source video ↗

Relying on agent self-audit

Benefit

Cheap, fast first pass that catches some claim/source mismatches.

Cost or risk

The agent validates its own premise, so a false premise passes self-audit — self-check cannot be the terminal gate for high-stakes reasoning.

Source video ↗

Iterative revision with fresh-eyes passes

Benefit

Catches defects introduced by remediation and improves the final artifact.

Cost or risk

Each additional review round adds agents to on-board and a new opportunity to disagree; returns diminish and stopping criteria are unspecified.

Source video ↗
